# Purpose

These Founding Principles define the enduring philosophy behind every decision made within Zyppi.

They are not implementation details. They are not product requirements. They are the constitutional beliefs that shape every architecture decision, engineering decision, product decision, partnership, and long-term strategic direction.

Products will evolve. Technologies will evolve. AI models will evolve. Business models will evolve.

These principles define the enduring character of Zyppi.

# Pillar I — Reality

## Principle 1 — Reality Exists Before Software

Reality does not begin when software observes it.

Subjects, Objects, Places, and the Events that connect them exist independently of digital systems.

Software must adapt to reality — not redefine it.

## Principle 2 — Software Must Observe Before It Acts

Software cannot safely automate what it has not first observed.

Every meaningful action begins with Evidence of an Event.

Observation always precedes execution.

## Principle 3 — Context Creates Meaning

Nothing exists in isolation.

Every interaction gains meaning through context — the relationships between Subject, Object, Place, and Time.

Without context, software only sees isolated data.

With context, software understands reality.

## Principle 4 — Reality Must Never Be Fabricated

Software may infer. Software may predict. Software may estimate. Software may recommend.

But software must never present assumptions as verified reality.

Uncertainty must always remain explicit.

Evidence must always remain distinguishable from inference.

## Principle 5 — Capture Reality Once

Reality should be captured once, as an Event with its Evidence.

Every subsequent system should build upon that shared understanding.

Duplicate observations create conflicting truths.

> **Constitutional Note:** Principles 1–5 are expressed using North Star v6.0's five Reality dimensions (Subject, Object, Place, Event, Evidence). This is a terminology alignment only. Every substantive commitment made in Founding Principles v4.2's Pillar I remains unchanged — "people, objects, places, and events" in v4.2 map directly onto "Subjects, Objects, Places, and Events" here. See `00-RECONCILIATION-NOTE.md`.

# Pillar II — Trust

## Principle 6 — Trust Requires Evidence

Trust is never assumed.

Trust is established through verifiable Evidence.

Evidence may originate from:

- Identity
- Credentials
- Cryptography
- Behavior
- Relationships
- Attestations
- Policy

The stronger the Evidence, the stronger the Trust.

## Principle 7 — Trust Before Action

Every meaningful action must be preceded by sufficient trust.

Trust may be:

- Live
- Cached
- Delegated
- Pre-authorized
- Offline-verifiable

But it must always exist before execution.

## Principle 8 — Trust Compounds

Every verified interaction strengthens future confidence.

Every successful execution contributes to a richer trust graph.

Trust is accumulated — not recreated.

## Principle 9 — Trust Is Constitutional

Trust is governed.

It is bounded by:

- Policy
- Standing
- Delegation
- Jurisdiction
- Consent

No participant is above constitutional trust.

## Principle 10 — Trust Must Be Explainable

Every trust decision must be understandable.

Every denial must be explainable.

Every authorization must be auditable.

Opaque trust eventually becomes distrust.

> **Constitutional Note:** Throughout this document, **Trust** refers to the strategic outcome produced by verified Evidence, constitutional policy, and intelligence. This does **not** reopen WS-01A Amendment A-001, which permanently classifies Trust as a Tier 1 Intelligence concept rather than an independent constitutional primitive. This note is unchanged from v4.2.

# Pillar III — Capability

## Principle 11 — Build Capabilities, Not Features

Features expire. Capabilities compound.

Capabilities become reusable building blocks across industries, products, organizations, and future generations of technology.

## Principle 12 — Intelligence Serves Capability

Artificial Intelligence exists to discover, compose, optimize, and execute capabilities.

AI never replaces constitutional governance.

It operates within constitutional boundaries.

## Principle 13 — AI Is a Native Participant

Human and AI Agent Subjects consume the same constitutional capabilities.

They operate under the same Trust model.

They obey the same Policy model.

They differ only in the origin of intent.

## Principle 14 — Capabilities Must Be Discoverable

Every participant should discover only the capabilities they are permitted to use.

Discovery depends on:

- Reality
- Trust
- Standing
- Context
- Policy

The Capability Graph is the constitutional interface between possibility and permission.

## Principle 15 — Expand Through Infrastructure

Products solve today's problems.

Infrastructure enables tomorrow's industries.

Whenever possible, build infrastructure that others compose rather than products only Zyppi can operate.

# Pillar IV — Execution

## Principle 16 — The Path to Action Is Sacred

Once trust has been established and intent declared, execution should be immediate.

Nothing unnecessary should interrupt the path between intent and outcome.

## Principle 17 — Every Decision Produces Proof

Execution without Evidence is incomplete.

Every meaningful action should produce an auditable execution receipt.

Every intelligence conclusion should produce a verifiable attestation.

Proof creates accountability.

## Principle 18 — Every Access Method Has a Fallback

Reality is resilient.

Software must be equally resilient.

No single technology should become a single point of failure.

Graceful degradation is a constitutional requirement.

## Principle 19 — No Participant Should Harm Another

The platform must ensure that one participant's scale, behavior, or failure cannot degrade another participant's experience.

Isolation protects trust.

## Principle 20 — Avoid Feature Gravity

Whenever new opportunities emerge, Zyppi must resist becoming the application.

The platform should enable ecosystems rather than replace them.

Infrastructure creates enduring value.

Applications come and go.

---

# Constitutional Decision Test

Before introducing any new capability, product, architecture, partnership, or strategic initiative, ask:

1. Does it strengthen our understanding of reality?
2. Does it increase verifiable trust?
3. Does it build reusable capabilities rather than isolated features?
4. Does it preserve constitutional governance?
5. Does it simplify the path to execution?
6. Does it preserve explainability and auditability?
7. Does it avoid unnecessary centralization?
8. Does it enable others to build upon Zyppi rather than depend on proprietary applications?
9. Does it preserve focus and resist Feature Gravity?

If the answer to any of these questions is **No**, the proposal should be reconsidered.
