## 5. Constitutional Definition of Delegation

Delegation is:

> *The explicit constitutional transfer or derivation of bounded Authority from a Subject possessing valid Authority to another Subject, while preserving the Authority's origin, provenance, scope, temporal validity and constitutional constraints.*

Delegation:

- `transfers Authority`

but:

- `does not transfer sovereignty`

and:

- `does not create Authority from nothing.`

Every valid Delegation therefore has an Authority origin.

---

## 6. Delegated Authority

Delegated Authority is Authority derived through a valid Delegation.

Delegated Authority **SHALL**:

- remain subordinate to its parent Authority;
- remain bounded;
- preserve its complete constitutional provenance;
- remain temporally constrained;
- remain revocable;
- remain subject to Standing and other constitutional prerequisites;
- never exceed its parent Authority;
- never create sovereignty;
- cease to authorize current execution when its constitutional validity has terminated.

Formally:

```text
DelegatedAuthority(child) ⊆ Authority(parent)
```

with respect to every constitutionally relevant scope dimension.

---

## 7. Delegation Is Not Identity

A Subject's Identity proves or represents which constitutional Subject is involved.

Identity does not establish:

> `may act for`

or:

> `may exercise Authority belonging to another Subject.`

Therefore:

```text
Identity ≠ Delegation
```

No execution **SHALL** infer Delegation merely because two Subjects possess valid Identity.

---

## 8. Delegation Is Not Trust

Trust concerns confidence established through valid constitutional Evidence and security processes.

Delegation concerns lawful Authority derivation.

Therefore:

```text
Trust ≠ Authority
Authority ≠ Delegation
```

A highly trusted Subject may possess no Delegation.

A Subject possessing a narrowly valid Delegation may possess no generalized privilege beyond that Delegation.

No Boolean such as:

```text
trustedToActFor = true
```

may substitute for constitutional Delegation.

---

## 9. Delegation Is Not Capability

Capability answers:

> *What governed ability may potentially be exercised?*

Delegation answers:

> *In relation to whose constitutional Authority or governed state may this Subject exercise it?*

Therefore:

```text
Capability ≠ Delegation
```

Possession of Capability `C` **SHALL NOT** prove that Actor `A` may exercise `C` on behalf of Governed Subject `B`.
