# 0. Purpose

Zyppi's North Star is universal.

Its resources are not.

A universal platform that attempts to enter every market at once will usually establish none.

A startup therefore requires concentration.

But concentration creates a constitutional danger:

> the first market may become confused with the identity of the platform.

`Marketing-002` establishes the doctrine that prevents both failures.

It governs:

- how Zyppi chooses an establishing market;
- how Zyppi chooses a market-entry wedge;
- what qualifies as a Trojan Horse;
- how present focus remains subordinate to the North Star;
- how entry utility should minimize adoption friction;
- how every entry should create durable platform value;
- how standards and commodity capabilities may be used strategically;
- how the first market may differ from the first meaningful revenue source;
- how Zyppi progresses from Baby to Grow to Compete to Dominate;
- how an entry wedge may be preserved, reworked, demoted, or abandoned;
- how future domains inherit the same entry doctrine.

The central rule is:

> **Enter narrowly enough to win, but compound broadly enough to become Zyppi.**

---

# 1. Governing Distinction — Platform Identity vs Establishing Market

The Marketing Constitution recognizes three distinct objects:

```text
UNIVERSAL PLATFORM
What Zyppi is

        ↓

ESTABLISHING MARKET
Where Zyppi concentrates now

        ↓

ENTRY WEDGE
What the market adopts first
```

These SHALL NOT be conflated.

The universal platform is governed by the North Star.

The establishing market is governed by strategic plans.

The entry wedge is governed by current market programs and experiments.

A successful wedge may become large.

It SHALL NOT automatically become Zyppi's constitutional identity.

---

# 2. Establishing Market Definition

An **Establishing Market** is the first market context in which Zyppi deliberately concentrates sufficient resources to achieve meaningful adoption, operational learning, repeatable use, and platform maturation.

An Establishing Market should offer some combination of:

- a real and understandable problem;
- accessible users or buyers;
- a practical distribution surface;
- reusable Zyppi primitives;
- standards or infrastructure that reduce entry cost;
- meaningful interaction with Reality;
- sufficient scale to test the platform;
- a credible path toward deeper Zyppi capabilities.

The Establishing Market is a strategic choice.

It is not a constitutional boundary.

---

# 3. Entry Wedge Definition

An **Entry Wedge** is the smallest coherent product, utility, protocol surface, developer primitive, or market-facing capability through which a participant can first experience useful Zyppi value.

An Entry Wedge SHOULD:

- solve a recognizable problem;
- be easy to understand;
- be easy to adopt;
- minimize setup;
- minimize procurement;
- minimize integration;
- minimize support dependency;
- create a reason to return;
- connect naturally to deeper Zyppi capabilities.

An Entry Wedge need not expose the entire constitutional architecture.

In many cases, doing so would create unnecessary friction.

---

# 4. Trojan Horse Definition

Within the Marketing Constitution, a **Trojan Horse** is a deliberately small, useful, low-friction entry mechanism whose adoption creates durable strategic assets that support the later expansion of Zyppi.

The term SHALL NOT imply deception.

The user SHALL receive the utility they were promised.

The "Trojan Horse" property exists in the architecture of compounding, not in misleading the customer.

A valid Trojan Horse therefore has two simultaneous properties:

```text
IMMEDIATE USER VALUE
        +
DURABLE ZYPPI COMPOUNDING
```

Without immediate user value, it is not a viable entry product.

Without durable compounding, it is merely a small product.

---

# 5. Constitutional Law — Utility Before Explanation

Zyppi's universal architecture may require extensive explanation.

The entry product SHOULD NOT.

The preferred sequence is:

```text
Useful Thing
        ↓
Adoption
        ↓
Repeated Use
        ↓
Deeper Need
        ↓
Discovery of More Zyppi
```

not:

```text
Explain Universal Trust Execution Platform
        ↓
Explain Reality Sync
        ↓
Explain Evidence / Standing / Policy / Receipt
        ↓
Ask customer to adopt
```

The market should be able to enter through utility before understanding the entire constitutional system.

No successful market entry may silently redefine Zyppi.

Examples:

```text

≠

Commerce success
≠
Zyppi is only a commerce company

Webhook utility success
≠
Zyppi is a webhook company

Short-link success
≠
Zyppi is a link-management company
```

An entry mechanism is a distribution decision.

The North Star remains the identity.

# 7. Constitutional Law — Focus Now, Preserve Future

The Marketing branch recognizes that focus is necessary.

During the Baby stage, Zyppi may allocate nearly all practical resources to one Establishing Market.

This concentration SHALL NOT require constitutional narrowing.

Therefore:

> **Operational focus may be narrow. Constitutional optionality SHALL remain broad.**

Future domains should be able to reuse the same constitutional Marketing doctrine even if:

- the objects are not products;
- the interactions are not scans;
- the identifiers are not GS1;
- the buyers are not brands;
- the workflows are not commerce workflows.

# 8. Baby → Grow → Compete → Dominate

The Marketing branch adopts the following strategic progression.

This is a governing progression model.

It is not a guaranteed forecast.

---

## 8.1 BABY — Establish

Primary objectives:

- choose one establishing market;
- solve one small useful problem;
- remove adoption friction;
- acquire real participants;
- generate operational evidence;
- learn cheaply;
- preserve cash;
- harden reusable infrastructure;
- avoid Feature Gravity.

Core question:

> **Can Zyppi become useful enough that real participants voluntarily adopt it?**

The Baby SHALL prioritize survival and learning over architectural completeness.

---

## 8.2 GROW — Deepen

Primary objectives:

- increase activation;
- retain participation;
- deepen value;
- activate additional reusable capabilities;
- strengthen economic leverage;
- improve distribution;
- begin repeatable monetization;
- reduce manual operations.

Core question:

> **Can the initial footprint compound into deeper recurring value?**

---

## 8.3 COMPETE — Become Difficult to Substitute

Primary objectives:

- strengthen product-system differentiation;
- activate Trust, Policy, workflows, integrations, or execution where justified;
- create measurable switching cost;
- grow developer and partner ecosystems;
- operate reliably at larger scale;
- expand into adjacent applications or domains when evidence supports it.

Core question:

> **Does Zyppi create enough integrated value that coherent alternatives become materially weaker or more expensive?**

---

## 8.4 DOMINATE — Compound Infrastructure Power

Primary objectives may include:

- cross-domain reuse;
- ecosystem dependency;
- trusted infrastructure status;
- network utility;
- institutional adoption;
- federated capability;
- governed execution at scale.

Core question:

> **Has Zyppi earned a position where participation and infrastructure dependence compound faster than substitution?**

Domination SHALL never be assumed from architecture.

It must be evidenced.

# 9. Trojan Horse Selection Criteria

A candidate Trojan Horse SHOULD be evaluated across the following dimensions.

---

## 9.1 Immediate Utility

Can a user understand the benefit quickly?

A wedge that requires extensive education is weak for acquisition.

---

## 9.2 Adoption Friction

Evaluate:

- account setup;
- integration;
- procurement;
- payment friction;
- technical complexity;
- content preparation;
- support dependency;
- organizational approval.

Lower friction is generally preferred during Baby.

---

## 9.3 Time to First Value

A strong entry wedge SHOULD produce useful output quickly.

Where practical:

```text
minutes
>
days
>
weeks
>
months
```

for initial activation.

---

## 9.4 Human Resource Consumption

The wedge SHOULD minimize:

- founder selling;
- onboarding;
- configuration;
- customization;
- customer success;
- support;
- manual data operations.

Human attention is scarce startup capital.

---

## 9.5 Marginal Cost

The wedge SHOULD have low marginal infrastructure cost relative to its acquisition and strategic value.

Low marginal cost allows aggressive adoption.

---

## 9.6 Distribution Surface

A preferred wedge has natural ways to spread.

Examples may include:

- developer discovery;
- packaging;
- physical carriers;
- websites;
- APIs;
- integrations;
- partners;
- templates;
- public artifacts;
- standards ecosystems.

---

## 9.7 Durable Footprint

The wedge SHOULD leave behind reusable assets.

Examples:

- persistent identity;
- domain;
- configuration;
- interaction history;
- developer integration;
- state source;
- policy;
- event history;
- partner relationship;
- operational context.

---

## 9.8 Compounding Depth

The wedge SHOULD create a credible path toward deeper capabilities.

A wedge with no natural progression is strategically weak.

---

## 9.9 Constitutional Reuse

The capabilities built for the wedge SHOULD be reusable elsewhere in Zyppi.

The more specific the implementation, the greater the risk of application fragmentation.

---

## 9.10 Incumbent Attention

During Baby, a wedge may be strategically attractive when:

- the market is useful but not strategically central to dominant incumbents;
- incumbents are economically disincentivized to serve tiny users;
- the entry is too small to trigger aggressive response;
- Zyppi can spread before becoming threatening.

Low incumbent attention is a strategic advantage.

It is not a requirement if other conditions are strong.

---

## 9.11 Commodity Compatibility

A wedge MAY use commodity capabilities.

Commodity does not mean strategically useless.

A commodity may be valuable as:

- acquisition infrastructure;
- standards compatibility;
- distribution;
- trust-building;
- developer familiarity.

The entry wedge does not need to be the moat.

---

# 10. Trojan Horse Compounding Test

A Trojan Horse SHOULD answer:

> **What durable thing becomes stronger every time this product is legitimately used?**

Possible answers include:

```text
more persistent identities
more interactions
more configuration
more retained context
more developer familiarity
more integrations
more policies
more operational history
more partner distribution
more reusable infrastructure
```

If the only answer is:

> more monthly subscription revenue

the product may be economically useful but strategically weak as a Trojan Horse.

# 11. Compounding Dimensions

The Marketing branch recognizes three primary dimensions of market compounding.

---

## 11.1 Horizontal Compounding

More participants, objects, developers, partners, integrations, or distribution nodes.

Examples:

```text
more organizations
more identities
more developers
more embedded partners
more domains
```

---

## 11.2 Vertical Compounding

Deeper use of Zyppi capability.

Example progression:

```text
Access
→ Observation
→ Policy
→ Operational Workflow
→ Verification
→ Trust
→ Governed Capability
→ Execution
→ Proof
→ Receipt
```

---

## 11.3 Temporal Compounding

Historical value increases over time through:

- accumulated interaction history;
- decision history;
- operational state history;
- evidence history;
- trust history;
- execution history;
- institutional learning.

A strong Trojan Horse may participate in one, two, or all three dimensions.

# 12. Entry Utility and Revenue Are Separate Questions

The Marketing Constitution SHALL distinguish:

```text
BEST ACQUISITION MECHANISM
```

from:

```text
BEST EARLY REVENUE MECHANISM
```

They may be the same.

They need not be.

A free or heavily subsidized entry wedge may be constitutionally correct if it creates sufficient adoption and compounding.

A separate low-touch revenue engine may support startup survival.

Therefore:

> **The Trojan Horse is an acquisition architecture, not necessarily the first cash register.**

This distinction SHALL protect strong entry mechanisms from artificial monetization pressure.

---

# 13. Do Not Tax the Doorway to Finance the Castle

If an entry wedge creates unusually strong strategic acquisition value, temporary cash needs SHALL NOT automatically justify:

- artificial usage limits;
- SKU taxes;
- identity taxes;
- scan taxes;
- arbitrary subscriptions;
- unnecessary feature gating.

Any proposed restriction SHOULD answer:

1. Does it protect scarce infrastructure?
2. Does it protect human support capacity?
3. Does it control abuse?
4. Does it reflect meaningful economic leverage?
5. Or does it exist only because Zyppi currently needs cash?

The fifth reason alone is insufficient constitutional justification.

---

# 14. Free Is a Strategic Instrument, Not a Moral Requirement

The Marketing branch does not require every Trojan Horse to be free.

A wedge may be:

- free;
- freemium;
- consumption-priced;
- low-cost;
- paid;
- partner-subsidized.

The correct price is determined by:

- market evidence;
- adoption friction;
- marginal cost;
- economic leverage;
- ZEA;
- strategic compounding.

"Free" is justified only when it strengthens the system.

"Paid" is justified only when the market receives sufficient value.

---

# 15. Standards as Entry Infrastructure

Open standards may be especially powerful Trojan Horse infrastructure because they:

- reduce customer fear;
- reduce lock-in concern;
- improve interoperability;
- create familiar developer language;
- increase portability;
- reduce implementation ambiguity;
- enable broader distribution.

Zyppi SHOULD prefer:

> **Adopt standards below Zyppi. Compete above them.**

Zyppi SHALL NOT pretend that standards support itself is the moat.

---

# 16. Commodity Doorway Doctrine

A low-moat capability may still be a strong doorway.

Examples might include:

- standards resolution;
- basic identity;
- simple links;
- QR generation;
- simple API access;
- basic developer utilities.

Such capabilities may be strategically justified when they:

```text
attract participants
+
reduce adoption friction
+
exercise reusable infrastructure
+
create durable footprint
```

The Marketing branch SHALL NOT reject an entry solely because its lowest-level feature is commoditized.

The question is:

> **What coherent system begins compounding after entry?**

---

# 17. Entry Wedge vs Moat

A Trojan Horse SHOULD NOT be required to carry the full investment thesis.

The strategic sequence may be:

```text
LOW-MOAT ENTRY
        ↓
DURABLE FOOTPRINT
        ↓
DEEPER CONTEXT
        ↓
HIGHER-LEVERAGE CAPABILITIES
        ↓
DEPENDENCE
        ↓
DEFENSIBILITY
```

Therefore:

> **A weak moat can still be a strong entry strategy.**

But:

> **A strong entry strategy is not evidence that a future moat will emerge.**

The moat remains a hypothesis until demonstrated.

---

# 18. One Wedge Does Not Need to Serve Every Future Buyer

A free developer may never become an enterprise buyer.

A small manufacturer may never use governed execution.

A utility user may never enter Commerce.

This does not automatically invalidate the entry strategy.

An entry wedge may contribute through:

- distribution;
- infrastructure utilization;
- developer familiarity;
- revenue;
- reliability;
- ecosystem credibility;
- operational learning.

The Marketing branch SHALL evaluate the whole system, not require every participant to travel the entire maturity ladder.

---

# 19. Buyer Transition Doctrine

As Zyppi deepens, the buyer may change.

Example pattern:

```text
Developer / Operator
        ↓
Team / Manager
        ↓
Business Owner
        ↓
Enterprise Operations
        ↓
Risk / Compliance / Governance
        ↓
Institutional Buyer
```

A change in buyer is not necessarily a broken funnel.

It may reflect a transition from low-consequence utility to high-consequence platform value.

The 1-, 3-, and 5-year plans SHALL identify expected buyer transitions where relevant.

---

# 20. Low-Sales Adoption Preference

During Baby, Zyppi SHOULD prefer entry wedges that can spread with limited direct sales.

Preferred characteristics include:

- self-service signup;
- instant activation;
- clear documentation;
- transparent economics;
- developer tooling;
- public or embedded distribution;
- partner-assisted discovery.

Human sales may become appropriate at deeper enterprise layers.

The acquisition wedge should not require enterprise sales merely to exist.

---

# 21. Human Attention Gate

A candidate entry wedge SHOULD be rejected or reworked when:

```text
Revenue
<
Human Attention Cost
+
Opportunity Cost
+
Feature Gravity
```

especially when the same human effort could create reusable platform infrastructure.

The Marketing branch recognizes human attention as a first-class economic resource.

---

# 22. Infrastructure Reuse Gate

Before approving an entry product, ask:

> **What reusable Zyppi primitive does this require or strengthen?**

Strong answers may include:

- persistent reference;
- resolver;
- event ingestion;
- state;
- policy;
- evidence;
- authority;
- capability;
- receipt;
- monitoring;
- delivery;
- developer tooling.

Weak answer:

> "It is a profitable but unrelated application."

Unrelated applications require an unusually strong strategic justification.

---

# 23. Feature Gravity Test

A wedge is at risk of Feature Gravity when customer demand begins pulling Zyppi into an adjacent application's full scope.

Warning signs include:

```text
one useful page
→ page builder

basic menu
→ restaurant operating system

simple warranty interaction
→ warranty management SaaS

short link
→ full marketing automation platform

state endpoint
→ generic database platform
```

The Marketing branch SHALL distinguish:

- the minimal useful infrastructure surface;
- the adjacent application business.

Zyppi SHOULD remain on the infrastructure side unless strategic doctrine explicitly authorizes otherwise.

---

# 24. Minimum Destination Principle

Where a useful entry requires a fallback surface, Zyppi may provide the minimum destination needed for continuity.

It SHOULD NOT become the destination business itself.

The governing principle is:

> **Provide the minimum surface required for useful and resilient interaction without absorbing the customer's content business.**

This preserves lean infrastructure.

---

# 25. Store Truth; Reference Content

Market entry SHALL preserve the infrastructure doctrine:

> **Store truth. Reference content.**

Zyppi SHOULD own:

- identity;
- relationships;
- policies;
- state where constitutionally appropriate;
- observations;
- evidence references;
- decisions;
- Proof;
- Receipts;
- governed operational records.

Zyppi SHOULD NOT become a general-purpose content warehouse merely because hosted content increases short-term product appeal.

Current strategic plans SHOULD evaluate candidate wedges against a common scorecard.

Suggested dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| User utility | Is the benefit immediate and understandable? |
| Adoption friction | Can adoption occur quickly? |
| Human burden | Can it operate self-service? |
| Marginal COGS | Can Zyppi afford aggressive adoption? |
| Distribution | Can the wedge spread efficiently? |
| Competition | How strong are coherent alternatives? |
| Commodity pressure | Is the capability becoming expected/free? |
| Durable footprint | What remains after use? |
| Horizontal compounding | Does participation expand the network? |
| Vertical compounding | Does it expose deeper capabilities? |
| Temporal compounding | Does history become more valuable? |
| Infrastructure reuse | Does it harden reusable Zyppi primitives? |

| Revenue compatibility | Can it coexist with ZEA? |
| Future-domain reuse | Can the underlying capability survive beyond the first market? |

The scorecard guides decisions.

It does not mechanically determine them.

# 27. Entry Wedge Outcomes

After attack and evidence review, a candidate may receive:

## ESTABLISH
Adopt as the current primary market-entry wedge.

## SUPPORT
Use as a secondary distribution or activation surface.

## PRESERVE
Keep the capability but not as the primary entry.

## DEMOTE
Move it to a supporting layer.

## REWORK
Modify the value proposition, scope, buyer, or economic model.

## REJECT
Do not rely on it under current evidence.

## HOLD
Insufficient evidence.

These outcomes SHALL remain auditable under `Marketing-001`.

# 28. Entry Wedge Kill Conditions

Every strategic plan SHOULD define explicit kill or review conditions.

Examples:

- adoption friction remains too high;
- self-service conversion does not emerge;
- human support burden dominates;
- infrastructure cost becomes uneconomic;
- a standard fully absorbs the value;
- coherent competitors make the wedge irrelevant;
- compounding footprint fails to emerge;
- deeper activation has no credible path;
- Feature Gravity becomes unacceptable;
- the wedge narrows Zyppi's future architecture;
- the buyer is inaccessible;
- distribution fails.

A wedge SHALL be allowed to die.

The North Star survives the death of a tactic.

---

# 29. Success Does Not Automatically Authorize Expansion

A successful wedge can create pressure to add more adjacent features.

Success itself is not permission.

Before expanding the wedge, ask:

1. Does the new capability increase reusable Zyppi depth?
2. Does it increase compounding?
3. Does it strengthen the current strategic horizon?
4. Is the adjacent capability better provided by an ecosystem partner?
5. Does it create Feature Gravity?
6. Does it distract from deeper Zyppi capability?

Growth SHALL remain governed.

# 30. Market Capture Doctrine

Where the entry market is strategically important, Zyppi may deliberately prioritize market capture over early extraction.

Market capture may justify:

- aggressive free allowances;
- low pricing;
- open developer access;
- simple migration;
- portability;
- partner distribution;
- reduced lock-in;
- standards-correct behavior.

However:

> **Market capture is not vanity scale.**

A capture strategy SHOULD produce one or more durable assets such as:

- installed references;
- active users;
- developer integrations;
- interaction history;
- partner channels;
- operational dependence;
- recurring usage.

---

# 32. Entry Economics

A strong Trojan Horse may be economically subsidized.

The strategic plan SHALL nevertheless understand:

- direct COGS;
- human support;
- abuse;
- payment cost where relevant;
- retention cost;
- operational complexity.

Subsidy without measurement is not strategy.

Therefore:

> **Free commercial treatment may coexist with internal economic measurement.**

The detailed interface between market pricing and ECU is governed by `Marketing-006` and ZEA.

# 33. Bootstrap Cash Separation

The Marketing Constitution recognizes that a strong market-entry wedge may take time to produce sufficient revenue.

Zyppi MAY therefore operate a separate Bootstrap Cash Engine when necessary.

Such a mechanism SHALL:

- remain subordinate to the North Star;
- avoid corrupting the primary entry wedge;
- require low human-resource consumption;
- prefer self-service;
- prefer low marginal cost;
- strengthen reusable infrastructure where practical;
- remain removable if it becomes distracting.

Specific bootstrap utilities belong in strategic plans and programs.

They SHALL NOT be frozen into this doctrine.

# 37. Current Lessons Preserved at Constitutional Level

The current Commerce reconnaissance has produced several lessons sufficiently general to inform this doctrine without constitutionalizing the Commerce tactic itself.

These include:

1. **A commodity layer can still be an excellent acquisition layer.**
2. **The entry wedge need not be the moat.**
3. **A strong free doorway should not be damaged solely to solve temporary cash needs.**
4. **Every entry should preferably leave durable platform value.**
5. **Self-service and low human burden matter disproportionately during Baby.**
6. **Feature-level overlap does not automatically destroy coherent system differentiation.**
7. **A low-cost entry may have a different buyer from deeper enterprise value.**
8. **The permanent economic model and temporary startup survival mechanisms may be different.**
9. **The current market may consume nearly all operational focus without becoming constitutional identity.**

These principles are domain-independent.

# 38. Constitutional Anti-Patterns

The following are prohibited or strongly discouraged.

---

## Anti-Pattern 1 — Universal Launch

Attempting to launch Zyppi across many domains simultaneously because the architecture is universal.

---

## Anti-Pattern 2 — Wedge Imprisonment

Allowing the first successful wedge to redefine the whole company.

---

## Anti-Pattern 3 — Entry-Must-Be-Moat

Rejecting a strong acquisition wedge merely because competitors can reproduce its lowest-level features.

---

## Anti-Pattern 4 — Revenue Panic

Weakening a high-potential acquisition mechanism to manufacture early revenue.

---

## Anti-Pattern 5 — Free-at-All-Costs

Giving away high-cost or high-leverage capability merely because free adoption sounds attractive.

---

## Anti-Pattern 6 — Feature Gravity

Allowing adjacent feature demand to convert infrastructure into an unrelated application company.

---

## Anti-Pattern 7 — Human-Heavy Baby

Choosing an entry that requires persistent founder-led sales, onboarding, and customization when self-service alternatives are plausible.

---

## Anti-Pattern 8 — Vanity Market Capture

Celebrating registrations, identities, scans, or traffic without retention, compounding, or deeper value.

---

## Anti-Pattern 9 — Standards Tollbooth

Using an open standard as justification for artificial extraction at the participation layer.

---

## Anti-Pattern 10 — Future Moat as Entry Claim

Promising defensibility that has not yet been earned.

# 46. Closing Principle

A universal platform does not become universal by launching everywhere.

It becomes universal by establishing itself somewhere, learning from Reality, building reusable capabilities, and expanding without losing its constitutional identity.

Zyppi therefore SHALL not confuse breadth with ambition.

During Baby, the correct strategy may appear extremely narrow.

That narrowness is acceptable when it serves a broader compounding architecture.

The Marketing branch therefore adopts the following governing principle:

> **Enter through the smallest useful doorway that can establish Zyppi, compound every legitimate interaction into reusable platform strength, and preserve the freedom to expand toward the North Star when Reality earns the next step.**

Or, in its shortest strategic form:

> **Baby → Grow → Compete → Dominate.**

Not by jumping stages.

By compounding what survives.
