# 0. Purpose

Marketing is the interface between what Zyppi knows and what the market believes Zyppi can do.

That interface creates risk.

A technically correct statement can still confuse the buyer.

A simple statement can become false through excessive compression.

A future architectural capability can accidentally be marketed as a current product.

Telemetry can be described as Evidence.

A Receipt can be described as proof of Reality.

A standards deadline can be framed as a requirement to buy Zyppi.

AI-generated copy can strengthen language beyond the evidence.

Investor storytelling can quietly turn scenarios into forecasts.

`Marketing-013` establishes the constitutional doctrine governing how Zyppi communicates with markets.

It governs:

- market claims;
- evidence-bound language;
- buyer-first messaging;
- positioning compression;
- category language;
- product capability claims;
- Trust and verification terminology;
- AI claims;
- standards and regulatory claims;
- competitor comparisons;
- pricing communication;
- investor narrative;
- sales material;
- developer communication;
- public website language;
- press and media;
- AI-generated marketing;
- claim review and correction.

The central rule is:

> **Say the strongest thing Zyppi can truthfully support, in the simplest language the intended buyer can correctly understand—no stronger and no more complicated.**

# 1. Governing Communication Hierarchy

Zyppi market communication SHALL follow:

```text
Reality / Evidence
        ↓
Constitutional Meaning
        ↓
Current Product Capability
        ↓
Buyer Job
        ↓
Positioning
        ↓
Message
        ↓
Copy
```

Copy SHALL not redefine the layers above it.

---

# 2. Claim Definition

A **Claim** is any explicit or implied market-facing assertion about:

- Zyppi;
- a product;
- a capability;
- a buyer outcome;
- a market;
- a competitor;
- a standard;
- regulation;
- economics;
- traction;
- future potential.

Claims include:

- headlines;
- captions;
- sales statements;
- pricing language;
- diagrams;
- charts;
- investor slides;
- testimonials;
- AI-generated summaries.

An implication can be a claim even if not written as a formal sentence.

---

# 3. Constitutional Law — Evidence Travels With the Claim

Every material claim SHALL remain consistent with its evidence grade under `Marketing-001`.

The internal record should permit Zyppi to know:

- what is being claimed;
- what evidence supports it;
- what scope applies;
- what uncertainty remains.

The public message does not need to display an E-code.

The evidence discipline still applies.

---

# 4. Constitutional Law — Claim Strength Cannot Exceed Evidence Strength

Examples:

```text
E2 competitor research
may support:
"Existing tools often charge for X."

does not support:
"Customers prefer Zyppi."
```

```text
One paying customer
may support:
"A customer paid for X."

does not support:
"The market has validated X."
```

Claim strength SHALL not be upgraded for rhetorical impact.

---

# 5. Constitutional Law — Simplicity May Compress Meaning, Not Distort It

Marketing MAY translate complex architecture into familiar language.

Example:

```text
Internal:
Evidence + Authority + Standing + Policy + Capability

External:
Decide what is allowed before an automated action runs.
```

This is valid when the underlying product actually performs the stated function.

Compression SHALL preserve semantic truth.

---

# 6. Constitutional Law — Buyer Job Before Platform Breadth

Entry-stage communication SHALL lead with:

- buyer;
- problem;
- trigger;
- outcome.

It SHOULD NOT lead with the universal platform unless the audience explicitly requires that context.

The Marketing branch adopts:

> **Remain universal internally. Speak specifically externally.**

---

# 7. Constitutional Law — Present Capability Before Future Ambition

Public communication SHALL distinguish:

```text
WHAT ZYPPI DOES NOW
```

from:

```text
WHAT ZYPPI IS BUILDING TOWARD
```

from:

```text
WHAT COULD BECOME POSSIBLE
```

Future architecture SHALL not be presented as current market availability.

---

# 8. Constitutional Law — Ambition May Be Bold; Facts Must Be Exact

Zyppi may communicate ambitious purpose.

Examples:

- mission;
- North Star;
- long-term vision.

Such statements SHALL be framed as:

- purpose;
- direction;
- ambition;
- thesis.

They SHALL not be silently converted into current-state claims.

---

# 9. Communication Layers

The Marketing branch recognizes four communication layers.

## Layer C1 — Immediate Job
What problem is solved now?

## Layer C2 — Product-System Value
Why is the coherent Zyppi solution better?

## Layer C3 — Platform Expansion
What deeper capability becomes possible?

## Layer C4 — North Star
What long-term system is Zyppi intended to become?

The intended audience determines how many layers should be exposed.

---

# 10. Entry Communication

For an entry buyer, communication SHOULD usually emphasize:

- immediate Job;
- recognizable category;
- speed;
- simplicity;
- concrete outcome.

The buyer should not need to decode Zyppi's constitutional vocabulary to begin.

---

# 11. Expansion Communication

For deeper users or buyers, communication MAY introduce:

- policy;
- history;
- Trust;
- workflow;
- governance;
- execution.

The language should match the buyer's role.

---

# 12. Institutional Communication

For enterprise, institutional, regulatory, or investor audiences, broader platform architecture MAY be appropriate.

Even then, current capability and future thesis SHALL remain separate.

---

# 13. Buyer-Specific Messaging

The same product may require different value expressions.

Example:

```text
Developer:
Integrate once and resolve correctly.

Operations:
Control outcomes without rebuilding infrastructure.

Compliance:
Preserve policy, Evidence, and decision traceability.

Executive:
Reduce operational risk and integration fragmentation.
```

The underlying product remains consistent.

---

# 14. One Message Does Not Fit Every Buyer

Marketing SHALL NOT force one universal slogan to carry:

- developer acquisition;
- enterprise sales;
- regulatory explanation;
- investor narrative.

Consistency means shared truth.

It does not mean identical wording.

---

# 15. Message Hierarchy

A market-facing surface SHOULD generally answer:

1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. Why is it better or easier?
5. What do I do next?

Only then should deeper architecture appear where useful.

---

# 16. Category Anchoring

Entry communication MAY use familiar market categories to reduce cognitive load.

Examples may include:

- resolver;
- developer API;
- managed infrastructure;
- workflow engine.

The chosen category SHALL remain accurate enough not to mislead.

---

# 17. Category Is a Comprehension Tool

A category label is not a constitutional definition.

Zyppi MAY occupy different market categories across:

- products;
- domains;
- maturity stages.

The North Star remains independent.

---

# 18. Category Creation Restraint

Zyppi SHOULD NOT invent a new category merely because existing categories feel incomplete.

New-category language may increase:

- education burden;
- buyer confusion;
- sales cycle.

A new category requires strategic justification.

---

# 19. Reality Sync Language

**Reality Sync** expresses Zyppi's brand-level purpose and relationship to the physical/digital world.

It SHALL NOT be represented as:

- a new runtime stage;
- a protocol object;
- an API primitive;
- proof that digital state equals Reality.

It is purpose and positioning language governed by the North Star.

---

# 20. Universal Trust Execution Platform Language

The phrase **Universal Trust Execution Platform** may describe the constitutional platform direction.

It SHOULD NOT be the default first-contact category for a narrow Baby-stage buyer when a more comprehensible product category exists.

---

# 21. Trust Language Discipline

The word **Trust** has constitutional meaning.

Marketing SHALL avoid using Trust as a vague synonym for:

- confidence;
- reputation;
- quality;
- branding.

Where Zyppi claims Trust, the underlying process should involve the relevant constitutional basis.

---

# 22. Evidence Language Discipline

Marketing SHALL distinguish:

```text
Information
≠
Observation
≠
Evidence
≠
Verification
```

A data source SHALL not be called Evidence merely because Zyppi received it.

---

# 23. Verification Language Discipline

"Verified" SHOULD mean an actual verification process occurred against relevant Evidence or authoritative criteria.

Marketing SHALL not use "verified" to mean:

- parsed;
- found;
- retrieved;
- formatted;
- syntactically valid.

---

# 24. Authenticity Claim Discipline

Zyppi SHALL NOT state that a physical product is:

- authentic;
- genuine;
- counterfeit;

without sufficient Evidence and verification authority.

A valid identifier alone does not prove authenticity.

---

# 25. Identity Claim Discipline

Marketing SHALL distinguish:

```text
identifier observed
```

from:

```text
physical object identified with sufficient Evidence
```

Carrier interaction alone may not establish physical-object truth.

---

# 26. Physical Interaction Claim Discipline

Without sufficient Evidence, Zyppi SHALL not describe telemetry as proof that:

- a consumer physically scanned the original product;
- the product was at a specific location;
- the product moved;
- a specific individual interacted.

Observed request context must remain scoped honestly.

---

# 27. Standing Language Discipline

Standing refers to current permission, eligibility, or state under constitutional governance.

Marketing SHOULD translate it into buyer language where appropriate.

It SHALL not overstate Standing as universal authority.

---

# 28. Authority Language Discipline

Authority claims SHALL specify the relevant domain and basis where material.

A source being authoritative for one fact does not make it universally authoritative.

---

# 29. Policy Language Discipline

Policy may determine an outcome.

Marketing SHALL not imply that policy alone establishes Reality.

Policy governs what the system should do with available context.

---

# 30. Capability Language Discipline

Capability means an allowed or available action under governing conditions.

A capability being technically possible does not imply it is authorized.

---

# 31. Execution Language Discipline

"Execution" should mean an actual action performed through the relevant governed pathway.

A recommendation or proposed action is not execution.

---

# 32. Proof Language Discipline

**Proof** SHALL be scoped to what was actually demonstrated.

Proof of:

- system decision;
- policy evaluation;
- execution result;

is not automatically proof that external Reality occurred exactly as represented.

---

# 33. Receipt Language Discipline

An Execution Receipt records the auditable basis and outcome of a Zyppi-governed process.

Marketing SHALL NOT claim:

> "The Receipt proves Reality."

The appropriate claim is closer to:

> "The Receipt records what Zyppi observed, evaluated, decided, and executed under the relevant policy and context."

---

# 34. AI Language Discipline

AI usage alone SHALL not be marketed as differentiation.

Avoid empty claims such as:

- AI-powered;
- intelligent;
- autonomous;

unless the buyer receives a meaningful AI-enabled capability.

---

# 35. AI Agent Communication

Where AI agents use Zyppi, messaging MAY emphasize:

- governed capabilities;
- trusted context;
- policy boundaries;
- explainability;
- auditable execution.

Marketing SHALL not imply unrestricted autonomous authority.

---

# 36. Autonomous Language

"Autonomous" SHALL be used carefully.

A system may automate a workflow without possessing unrestricted autonomy.

Where consequence is material, messaging should explain relevant governance.

---

# 37. Intelligence Language

"Intelligence" may refer to derived interpretation, patterns, or decision support.

Marketing SHALL distinguish intelligence from:

- fact;
- Evidence;
- verification.

---

# 38. Standards Claims

Claims about standards SHOULD specify accurately whether Zyppi:

- supports;
- implements;
- parses;
- resolves;
- complies with;
- integrates with;

the standard.

Marketing SHALL avoid suggesting endorsement by a standards body unless such endorsement exists.

---

# 39. Standards Transition Claims

A standards transition may support urgency.

Marketing SHALL NOT state:

> "The new standard requires Zyppi."

unless an authoritative source explicitly does.

---

# 40. Regulatory Claims

Regulatory communication SHALL distinguish:

```text
regulation creates obligation
```

from:

```text
regulation requires Zyppi
```

The second is almost always stronger and requires direct support.

---

# 41. Compliance Language

"Compliant" is a consequential claim.

Marketing SHOULD state the scope where practical:

- standard;
- function;
- version;
- jurisdiction.

A tool supporting compliance does not necessarily make the customer's organization compliant.

---

# 42. Security Claims

Security claims SHALL reflect actual controls.

Avoid unsupported absolutes such as:

- unhackable;
- completely secure;
- zero risk.

---

# 43. Reliability Claims

Claims such as:

- high availability;
- resilient;
- fault tolerant;

SHOULD be supported by actual architecture or measured performance.

Numeric uptime claims require actual measurement.

---

# 44. Performance Claims

Claims such as:

- faster;
- lower latency;
- easier;
- cheaper;

SHOULD identify the relevant basis.

Comparative claims require a coherent alternative.

---

# 45. Competitive Claims

Marketing MAY compare Zyppi with alternatives.

Comparisons SHOULD:

- use current evidence;
- compare coherent systems;
- specify scope;
- avoid misrepresentation.

---

# 46. Competitor Naming

Competitors MAY be named where useful.

Claims SHALL remain factual and professional.

Zyppi SHOULD compete on customer value rather than ridicule.

---

# 47. "No Competition" Prohibition

Marketing SHALL NOT claim:

> "Zyppi has no competitors."

Even where no identical product exists, substitutes exist.

---

# 48. "Only Platform" Claims

Claims such as:

- only platform;
- first ever;
- unique;
- unprecedented;

require strong, current evidence.

They are presumptively disfavored during Baby.

---

# 49. Feature Comparison Discipline

Marketing SHALL not imply:

> "Competitors lack X"

merely because a feature was not found during incomplete research.

The claim should be scoped to what was actually examined.

---

# 50. Whole-System Differentiation Language

Marketing MAY communicate system-level advantage even when individual features are common.

Examples:

- fewer integrations;
- continuity across decisions;
- one governed path;
- shared semantics.

Such claims should remain evidence-bound.

---

# 51. Integration Compression Communication

Where validated, Zyppi may explain value as reducing:

- systems;
- handoffs;
- custom glue;
- reconciliation.

This can be easier for buyers to understand than internal architecture.

---

# 52. Moat Language

Marketing SHALL distinguish:

```text
Current Differentiation
```

from:

```text
Compounding Advantage
```

from:

```text
Future Moat Thesis
```

from:

```text
Demonstrated Moat
```

Moat SHALL not be claimed merely because architecture appears difficult to copy.

---

# 53. Network Effect Language

"Network effect" requires evidence that value to participants increases because other participants join.

Scale alone is insufficient.

---

# 54. Data Moat Language

More telemetry does not automatically create a data moat.

Marketing SHALL only claim data defensibility where the accumulated data materially improves value and is difficult to recreate.

---

# 55. Infrastructure Language

Zyppi MAY describe itself as infrastructure when the context supports that role.

Marketing SHALL not imply:

- universal adoption;
- critical infrastructure status;
- unavoidable dependence;

before evidence exists.

---

# 56. Protocol Language

Calling Zyppi a "protocol" creates specific expectations.

Marketing SHALL not use protocol language merely to imply inevitability or toll economics.

---

# 57. Platform Language

"Platform" is acceptable where multiple capabilities or participants are actually supported.

It SHALL not replace product clarity at entry.

---

# 58. Ecosystem Language

"Ecosystem" requires independent participants building, integrating, distributing, or depending upon Zyppi.

A set of planned integrations is not an ecosystem.

---

# 59. Adoption Language

Marketing SHALL distinguish:

- registered;
- adopted;
- activated;
- retained.

These terms SHALL follow `Marketing-004` and `Marketing-012`.

---

# 60. Traction Language

"Traction" SHOULD identify the behavior.

Examples:

- active organizations;
- retained paid customers;
- production integrations.

Lifetime signups alone are weak traction.

---

# 61. Growth Language

Growth claims SHOULD state:

- metric;
- baseline;
- period.

Avoid percentage growth without absolute context when misleading.

---

# 62. Revenue Language

Revenue claims SHALL distinguish:

- core;
- bootstrap;
- enterprise;
- recurring;
- one-time;

where material.

Bootstrap revenue SHALL not be presented as proof of core market validation.

---

# 63. Customer Language

"Customer" SHOULD mean a real external customer under the relevant context.

Unpaid testers or internal projects SHOULD be labeled accurately.

---

# 64. Partner Language

"Partner" SHOULD represent an actual relationship.

A company listed as technically compatible SHALL not be called a partner without basis.

---

# 65. Enterprise Language

"Enterprise-ready" is a broad claim.

Where used, Marketing SHOULD be able to support the relevant:

- security;
- reliability;
- governance;
- deployment;
- support;

expectations.

---

# 66. Global Language

"Global" SHALL not imply:

- local compliance everywhere;
- support everywhere;
- data residency everywhere;

merely because the service is internet-accessible.

---

# 67. Pricing Communication Principle

Pricing communication SHOULD answer:

- what is free;
- what is paid;
- what is measured;
- what creates additional cost;
- how spend is controlled.

The buyer should not need to understand ZEA to estimate a bill.

---

# 68. ECU Communication

The term **ECU** MAY be:

- direct;
- hybrid;
- abstracted;

according to buyer comprehension.

Marketing SHALL not expose ECU merely to appear sophisticated.

---

# 69. Pricing Simplicity

The commercial surface SHOULD use familiar language when possible:

- requests;
- usage;
- credits;
- included allowance;
- budget.

The underlying ECU model remains intact.

---

# 70. Free Language

If foundational participation is free, Marketing SHOULD avoid framing paid depth as:

> "You are losing free access."

Paid communication should emphasize gaining:

- more infrastructure;
- more history;
- more control;
- more Trust;
- more execution.

---

# 71. "Free Forever" Restraint

Marketing SHALL not promise "free forever" unless the constitutional and economic commitment truly supports permanence.

Prefer accurate terms such as:

- free foundational tier;
- free allowance;
- currently free;

according to policy.

---

# 72. Overage Communication

Variable pricing SHALL communicate:

- thresholds;
- alerts;
- behavior at limits;
- overage.

Surprise billing undermines market trust.

---

# 73. Investor Communication Doctrine

Investor material may communicate:

- current evidence;
- strategic thesis;
- scenarios;
- North Star.

These SHALL remain visually and verbally distinguishable.

# 89. Brand Salience

Messaging SHOULD seek memorability through:

- clarity;
- repeated useful concepts;
- distinct buyer value.

Memorability SHALL not require unsupported exaggeration.

---

# 90. Slogan Discipline

A slogan may be broader or more emotional than a product description.

It SHALL not substitute for precise capability explanation.

---

# 91. "Reality Sync" and Product Copy

"Reality Sync" may serve as a memorable brand relationship.

A buyer-facing product page SHOULD still explain what the specific product does.

---

# 92. Message Testing

Messaging is a hypothesis.

Marketing SHOULD test:

- category;
- headline;
- value proposition;
- CTA.

---

# 92A. Out-of-Market Brand Communication

Not every legitimate marketing communication exists to produce immediate activation.

For long-cycle B2B markets, communication MAY deliberately build category recognition, buyer memory, Mental Availability, associations with validated Category Entry Points, and future shortlist probability.

Such communication SHALL still obey all truth and evidence rules.

Brand-building activity SHALL NOT be judged solely by short-window conversion where the intended objective is future market memory.

Likewise, awareness SHALL NOT be presented as E3 product validation.

The communication objective must be explicit:

```text
Demand Creation / Memory Building
```

or:

```text
Demand Capture / Immediate Action
```

or a deliberate combination.

# 93. Message Validation

Higher conversion can validate that a message performs better for the tested behavior.

It does not automatically validate every factual implication in that message.

Truth constraints remain independent.

---

# 94. Claim Registry

Material market claims SHOULD be traceable through the lightweight Claim Registry established by `Marketing-001`.

The registry SHALL remain operationally light.

---

# 95. Claim Lifecycle

A claim may be:

- proposed;
- approved;
- active;
- revised;

The exact workflow is implementation-level.

# 96. Time-Sensitive Claims

Claims about:

- competitor prices;
- laws;

- market statistics;

SHOULD include a review trigger or date where material.

---

# 97. Claim Expiry

A previously true claim may become stale.

Marketing SHALL not preserve outdated claims merely because assets are already published.

---

# 98. Correction Doctrine

When Zyppi discovers a material public claim is false or materially misleading, it SHOULD correct the claim promptly and proportionally.

Correction strengthens credibility.

---

# 99. No Silent Claim Mutation

The following progression is prohibited:

```text
hypothesis
→ repeated messaging
→ accepted internally
→ presented as fact
```

Repetition does not create truth.

---

Example:

```text
"supports GS1 Digital Link"
```

SHALL not quietly become:

```text
"solves GS1 compliance"
```

without sufficient support.

---

# 101. No Silent Temporal Expansion

Example:

```text
"planned"
```

SHALL not become:

```text
"available"
```

without product release.

# 102. No Silent Causal Expansion

Example:

```text
"customers using X had fewer manual steps"
```

does not automatically become:

```text
"X caused a 50% cost reduction"
```

without causal support.

# 103. AI-Assisted Marketing Doctrine

AI MAY be used to:

- draft;
- summarize;
- personalize;
- translate;
- generate variants;

---

AI SHALL NOT:

- invent customer quotes;
- invent market statistics;
- invent competitor limitations;
- infer product availability;
- fabricate validation.

# 105. AI Personalization

- terminology;
- examples;
- buyer outcome.

---

# 106. AI Translation

Translation SHALL preserve claim scope and evidence.

A translated superlative can still overstate.

---

# 107. AI Sales Agents

AI sales agents MAY explain approved product and pricing information.

- preserve uncertainty;
- distinguish roadmap;
- avoid unauthorized commitments;
- avoid unsupported competitive claims.

---

# 108. AI Support and Developer Agents

AI support agents MAY explain:

- product behavior;

- limitations.

They SHALL not invent technical behavior when evidence is absent.

---

# 109. Testimonials

Testimonials SHALL originate from real people or organizations with appropriate permission.

AI-generated testimonial simulation SHALL never be presented as real.

---

# 110. Case Studies

Case studies SHOULD distinguish:

- observed facts;
- customer statements;
- Zyppi interpretation.

Causal claims require appropriate support.

---

# 111. Social Proof

Valid social proof may include:

- real customers;
- real integrations;
- real usage;
- real testimonials.

Planned partnerships or private tests SHALL not be inflated into customer adoption.

---

# 112. Logo Use

Customer or partner logos SHOULD only be used with appropriate basis and permission.

Logo presence SHALL not imply a stronger relationship than exists.

---

# 113. Comparative Charts

Comparison tables SHOULD:

- use meaningful dimensions;
- use current evidence;
- avoid selective deception;
- disclose material limitations where needed.

---

# 114. Price Comparison

Competitor price comparisons SHOULD account for:

- comparable usage;
- package;
- support;
- included capability.

A lower entry price may not mean lower total cost.

---

# 115. ROI Communication

ROI MAY be modeled.

A modeled ROI SHALL be labeled as:

- estimate;

# 129. Communication Review Questions

Before publishing a material claim, ask:

1. What exactly are we claiming?
2. Is it current, future, or aspirational?
3. What evidence supports it?
4. Does the buyer understand the language?
5. Is any constitutional term being overstated?
6. Is a standard/regulation being misrepresented?
7. Is a competitor comparison fair?
8. Could a reasonable buyer infer something stronger than intended?
9. Does this need a review date?
10. What would make us retract it?

---

# 130. Messaging Review Questions

Before approving a market message, ask:

1. Who is the buyer?
2. What Job is being addressed?
3. Why now?
4. What category will they understand?
5. What immediate value is clear?
6. Is the message simpler than the architecture?
7. Is it still true?
8. What proof can we show?
9. What is the next action?

Trust, verification, compliance, security, execution.

Higher classes SHOULD receive stronger review.

# 131A. Claim Governance by Consequence

Claim governance SHALL be proportionate to claim consequence.

For **Q0 Low-Consequence** and ordinary **Q1 Commercial** copy:

- teams MAY iterate rapidly;
- Council pre-approval is not required by default;
- truth constraints remain binding;
- material claims SHOULD be logged or traceable where required by `Marketing-001`.

For **Q2 Strategic** and **Q3 Consequential** claims, pre-publication review SHOULD be stronger because error can materially affect market thesis, investor understanding, Trust, verification, compliance, security, execution, or institutional credibility.

The exact approval workflow remains operational.

> **Truth review scales with consequence. It SHALL not become a universal publishing queue.**

Moat, network effect, infrastructure status, and ecosystem claims SHALL remain evidence-bound.

## Invariant 11
Pricing communication SHALL remain understandable and predictable without requiring buyers to understand the full economic Constitution.

## Invariant 12
Investor materials SHALL separate current evidence from future thesis and scenarios.

## Invariant 13
AI-generated marketing SHALL follow identical truth standards to human-generated marketing.

## Invariant 14
Material false or misleading public claims SHOULD be corrected rather than silently preserved.

## Invariant 15
Repetition SHALL not transform a hypothesis into fact.

Communication governance SHALL remain proportionate to claim consequence and SHALL not become a publishing bureaucracy.

Bootstrap products SHALL communicate their immediate utility without redefining Zyppi.

# 138. Closing Principle

Marketing does not become powerful by saying more.

It becomes powerful by saying the right thing, to the right actor, at the right level of abstraction, with enough evidence that the promise survives contact with Reality.

Zyppi's Constitution is complex because Reality, Trust, and execution are complex.

The buyer does not need to carry that complexity.

Marketing does.

The Marketing branch therefore adopts:

> **Know the full truth internally. Express only the part the buyer needs now. Use the strongest claim the evidence supports, never the strongest claim the copywriter can invent. Separate current capability from future ambition. Translate complexity without corrupting meaning.**

And:

> **Clarity wins the first decision. Accuracy earns the second. Repeated delivery earns Trust.**
