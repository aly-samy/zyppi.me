# 0. Purpose

Research can tell Zyppi what appears plausible.

Competitive analysis can reveal alternatives.

Economic modeling can estimate viability.

AI can simulate decisions.

None of those can tell Zyppi with certainty whether real participants will:

- care;
- adopt;
- activate;
- pay;
- retain;
- expand;
- depend.

That boundary belongs to Reality.

`Marketing-011` establishes the constitutional doctrine for crossing from hypothesis into real market evidence.

It governs:

- market experiments;
- validation classes;
- hypothesis design;
- testability;
- success conditions;
- failure conditions;
- kill criteria;
- experiment sequencing;
- E2 → E3 graduation;
- E3 → E4 repetition;
- reversible vs irreversible tests;
- experiment ethics;
- decision gates;
- null results;
- contradictory results;
- evidence preservation;
- strategic re-rating.

The central rule is:

> **An experiment exists to change a decision through contact with Reality. If the result cannot change the decision, it is not a meaningful validation experiment.**

---

# 1. Governing Boundary — Research vs Validation

The Marketing branch distinguishes:

```text
RESEARCH
asks:
What appears true?

        ↓

EXPERIMENT
asks:
What happens when Reality is given a choice?

        ↓

VALIDATION
asks:
Did repeated real behavior support the hypothesis strongly enough for the current decision?
```

Research may produce E2/E2+.

Real Zyppi market behavior produces E3.

Repeated Zyppi evidence may produce E4.

---

# 2. Constitutional Law — Research Is Not Validation

No amount of:

- competitor analysis;
- market sizing;
- advisor agreement;
- AI simulation;
- standards research;
- ROI modeling;

creates E3 adoption, payment, retention, or expansion evidence.

This inherits `Marketing-001`.

---

# 3. Constitutional Law — Experiment Must Be Decision-Relevant

Before running an experiment, Zyppi SHALL identify:

> **What decision will change depending on the result?**

Examples:

- build / do not build;
- preserve free;
- charge;
- change buyer;
- change positioning;
- change distribution;
- kill the wedge;
- continue to next validation stage.

If both success and failure produce the same decision, the test is weak or ceremonial.

---

# 4. Constitutional Law — Define Failure Before Launch

An experiment SHALL define failure before results are known.

This protects against:

- moving goalposts;
- confirmation bias;
- post-hoc interpretation.

Failure may mean:

- insufficient activation;
- no payment;
- excessive support;
- no retention;
- buyer mismatch;
- distribution failure.

---

# 5. Constitutional Law — Null Result Is a Result

If the experiment produces:

- no signups;
- no payment;
- no retention;
- no response;

the absence of behavior SHALL be preserved.

Silence is not automatically proof that the hypothesis is false.

It may indicate:

- weak distribution;
- wrong buyer;
- wrong message;
- weak job;
- poor timing.

The experiment must be interpreted at its actual scope.

---

# 6. Constitutional Law — Negative Evidence Must Be Actionable

A failed experiment SHALL lead to one or more of:

- REWORK;
- DEMOTE;
- REJECT;
- HOLD;
- new test.

The organization SHALL not repeatedly rerun essentially the same failed hypothesis without a materially changed premise.

---

# 7. Constitutional Law — Validation Is Claim-Specific

A successful experiment validates only what it actually tested.

Example:

```text
Users click "Start Free"
```

may support:

- message interest.

It does not validate:

- product activation;
- willingness to pay;
- retention;
- moat.

Evidence scope SHALL remain bounded.

---

# 8. Validation Classes

The Marketing branch recognizes distinct validation classes.

## V1 — Problem Validation
Does the problem exist for real actors?

## V2 — Solution Validation
Does the proposed solution help?

## V3 — Adoption Validation
Will participants begin real use?

## V4 — Value Validation
Do participants receive meaningful value?

## V5 — Economic Validation
Will participants pay or create sufficient economic value?

## V6 — Retention Validation
Does value persist?

## V7 — Expansion Validation
Does deeper value repeat or broaden?

## V8 — Distribution Validation
Can qualified participants be acquired repeatedly?

## V9 — Moat / Dependence Validation
Does accumulated value create resistance to substitution?

One class SHALL NOT be assumed from another.

---

# 9. Problem Validation

Problem validation may use:

- real interviews;
- workflow observation;
- historical incidents;
- support requests;
- operational evidence.

Problem validation SHOULD answer:

- who experiences it;
- how often;
- consequence;
- current workaround;
- urgency.

---

# 10. Solution Validation

Solution validation asks:

> **Does the proposed Zyppi response materially improve the job?**

Possible tests:

- prototype;
- manual concierge execution;
- API mock;
- limited production utility.

Solution validation does not automatically prove willingness to pay.

---

# 11. Adoption Validation

Adoption validation requires real participants to:

- deploy;
- integrate;
- configure;
- use;
- invoke;

the product in a way that goes beyond curiosity.

A signup is weaker than production use.

---

# 12. Value Validation

Value validation requires evidence that the product improved a meaningful condition.

Possible evidence:

- less work;
- lower failure;
- faster workflow;
- better control;
- successful compliance outcome;
- reduced integration burden.

---

# 13. Economic Validation

Economic validation may include:

- real payment;
- paid conversion;
- committed spend;
- usage exceeding free allowance;
- signed contract.

Hypothetical willingness-to-pay statements are weaker than behavior.

---

# 14. Retention Validation

Retention validation asks whether participants continue because value persists.

A one-time transaction may not demonstrate retention.

---

# 15. Expansion Validation

Expansion validation asks whether:

- use broadens;
- deeper capability activates;
- new buyer enters;
- more ECU is consumed;
- additional workflows are adopted.

Expansion is distinct from retention.

---

# 16. Distribution Validation

Distribution validation asks whether a channel repeatedly produces:

- qualified discovery;
- adoption;
- activation;
- acceptable acquisition economics.

Traffic does not validate distribution.

---

# 17. Moat / Dependence Validation

Moat validation requires real evidence of:

- switching difficulty;
- operational dependence;
- ecosystem dependence;
- history value;
- network utility.

It is the highest and latest validation class.

---

# 18. Evidence Graduation

The preferred graduation pattern is:

```text
E1
Logical hypothesis

↓ external research

E2 / E2+
Market-supported hypothesis

↓ Zyppi experiment

E3
Real Zyppi evidence

↓ repeated cohorts / time / contexts

E4
Repeated Zyppi evidence
```

Doctrine may then be reconsidered only when the principle is durable beyond the current tactic.

---

# 19. E3 Definition

E3 requires actual Zyppi interaction with real external participants.

Examples:

- production activation;
- actual payment;
- real retention;
- real channel conversion.

Synthetic or simulated participants SHALL NOT qualify.

---

# 20. E4 Definition

E4 requires repeated Zyppi evidence sufficient to show the behavior is not an isolated anomaly.

Repetition may occur across:

- multiple customers;
- multiple cohorts;
- time;
- segments;
- geographies;
- use cases.

Exact repetition thresholds are decision-specific.

---

# 21. Experiment Definition

A **Market Experiment** is a bounded intervention designed to observe real market behavior relevant to one or more strategic hypotheses.

A useful experiment SHOULD specify:

```text
Hypothesis
Audience
Intervention
Behavior measured
Success condition
Failure condition
Duration / sample logic
Decision
Evidence grade target
```

---

# 22. Hypothesis Quality

A strong hypothesis is:

- specific;
- falsifiable;
- decision-relevant;
- tied to one buyer/job;
- measurable.

Weak:

> "Developers will like Zyppi."

Strong:

> "Developers searching for GS1 Digital Link resolution will activate a self-service resolver at a materially higher rate when the first request works without account setup."

---

# 23. One Experiment, Few Questions

A single experiment SHOULD avoid testing too many major hypotheses simultaneously.

If:

- buyer;
- message;
- price;
- product;
- distribution;

all change at once, interpretation becomes weak.

The smallest useful test is preferred.

---

# 24. Experiment Ladder

The Marketing branch supports progressively more expensive tests.

## Level X0 — Desk Validation
Research only.

## Level X1 — Message Test
Landing page, ad, waitlist.

## Level X2 — Behavior Test
Signup, API request, configuration.

## Level X3 — Value Test
Real utility or workflow.

## Level X4 — Payment Test
Real economic commitment.

## Level X5 — Retention Test
Repeated use/payment.

## Level X6 — Expansion Test
Deeper or broader adoption.

Experiments SHOULD escalate only as prior risk is reduced.

---

# 25. Fake Door Tests

A Fake Door MAY test:

- interest;
- click intent;
- category comprehension.

It SHALL clearly avoid deceptive collection of money or false delivery promises.

Fake-door evidence does not validate product value.

---

# 25A. Institutional Experiment Safety

Fake-door and highly iterative market tests are not equally appropriate for every buying context.

For enterprise, institutional, infrastructure, Trust, compliance, or other high-consequence buyers, Zyppi SHALL prioritize credibility-preserving experiment designs.

The following are presumptively inappropriate when they could reasonably damage institutional trust:

- presenting an unavailable consequential capability as available;
- inviting procurement or purchase for a capability Zyppi cannot actually deliver without clear disclosure;
- covertly presenting materially inconsistent commercial terms inside the same active enterprise buying process;
- creating false security, compliance, verification, or continuity expectations merely to measure interest.

The preferred alternatives include:

- concept tests;
- explicitly labeled prototypes;
- structured buyer interviews;
- non-binding proposals;
- sandbox capability;
- clearly disclosed pilots;
- controlled commercial offers.

> **Experiment on the proposition without experimenting with the buyer's right to know what is real.**

---

# 25B. Enterprise Price-Testing Discipline

Price experimentation remains permitted.

For high-consequence or committee-based institutional buying, price tests SHOULD preserve commercial consistency, explainability, buyer trust, and contractual integrity.

Where individualized enterprise pricing is normal, different offers MAY exist because scope, capacity, SLA, support, or commitment differ.

Price variation SHALL NOT be disguised as arbitrary experimentation once a buyer reasonably believes a stable commercial offer has been made.

# 26. Landing Page Tests

Landing pages may test:

- positioning;
- buyer language;
- channel;
- signup intent.

They SHALL NOT be called product-market fit evidence.

---

# 27. Prototype Tests

A prototype may test:

- usability;
- comprehension;
- workflow desirability.

It may not validate production reliability.

---

# 28. Concierge Experiments

Manual execution MAY be used to validate whether a job is valuable before automating.

Concierge tests are useful when:

- volume is low;
- learning is high;
- automation cost is high.

They SHALL record human burden.

Concierge economics SHALL not be mistaken for scalable product economics.

---

# 29. Wizard-of-Oz Experiments

A participant may perceive an automated flow while humans perform some backend work where this is ethically appropriate and does not misrepresent consequential safety or evidence.

The purpose is learning.

The manual layer SHALL remain disclosed internally and removed before scalability claims.

---

# 30. Payment Tests

A strong economic experiment requires a real cost to the participant.

Examples:

- purchase;
- prepaid credit;
- paid pilot;
- subscription;
- deposit.

"Would you pay?" is weaker than "Will you pay now?"

---

# 31. Price Tests

Price experiments may compare:

- amount;
- packaging;
- included allowance;
- subscription vs consumption;
- ECU abstraction.

They SHALL preserve buyer comprehension and avoid hidden surprise.

---

# 32. Discounted Payment Evidence

Discounted payment is still E3 economic evidence.

Its scope must include the discount.

A customer paying $10 under a launch offer does not prove $100 willingness to pay.

---

# 33. Free Adoption Evidence

Free adoption may validate:

- problem;
- activation;
- distribution.

It does not validate payment.

Free participation can still have strategic value under `Marketing-005`.

---

# 34. Retention Test Design

Retention experiments SHOULD define:

- meaningful return interval;
- active behavior;
- cohort;
- reason for continued use.

"Account still exists" is insufficient.

---

# 35. Expansion Test Design

Expansion tests may observe:

- more objects;
- more workflows;
- more users;
- deeper capability;
- new buyer.

The program SHALL state which expansion it expects.

---

# 36. Buyer Bridge Experiments

Where entry and economic buyers differ, the program SHOULD test the Buyer Bridge.

A Buyer Bridge experiment may ask whether a Bridge Artifact such as:

- report;
- operational metric;
- audit output;
- reliability evidence;
- decision history;

causes the deeper buyer to engage.

---

# 37. Distribution Experiments

Distribution tests may compare:

- search;
- developer ecosystem;
- partner;
- marketplace;
- outbound;
- physical surfaces.

The unit of success is qualified activation, not traffic alone.

---

# 38. Physical Deployment Experiments

Before mass physical deployment, experiments SHOULD validate:

- reference continuity;
- fallback;
- standards behavior;
- operational reliability.

This follows the Physical Deployment Gate in `Marketing-009`.

---

# 39. Bootstrap Utility Experiments

Bootstrap utility tests SHOULD emphasize:

- self-service activation;
- real payment;
- low support;
- retention;
- gross margin;
- infrastructure reuse.

The test should reveal both cash and platform value.

---

# 40. Experiment Cost

Every experiment consumes:

- time;
- engineering;
- distribution;
- human attention;
- money.

The experiment should be cheaper than the uncertainty it resolves.

---

# 41. Minimum Viable Experiment

The Marketing branch prefers:

> **the smallest experiment capable of producing decision-relevant evidence.**

Do not build the complete system to test whether the problem exists.

---

# 42. Reversibility Principle

Reversible experiments MAY proceed with lower evidence thresholds.

Irreversible actions require stronger prior evidence.

High-irreversibility examples:

- mass packaging print;
- permanent pricing promise;
- large architecture commitment;
- major contract.

---

# 43. Experiment Timebox

Every experiment SHOULD have a predefined time or sample boundary.

An experiment SHALL NOT remain open indefinitely because no clear answer emerged.

At the boundary, choose:

- PASS;
- PROVISIONAL PASS;
- REWORK;
- REJECT;
- HOLD.

---

# 43A. External-Dependency Timebox

An experiment timebox governs when Zyppi reviews the evidence.

It does not require the external world to complete its own physical, regulatory, procurement, manufacturing, or organizational cycle within a software-style sprint.

Where activation depends materially on an external lifecycle, the experiment SHOULD distinguish:

```text
Zyppi-Controlled Readiness
+
External Incubation
+
Final Activation
```

Examples include packaging change cycles, retailer rollout windows, procurement, legal approval, scheduled migration, and physical installation.

A participant that has made a real qualifying commitment but is waiting on a documented external dependency SHALL NOT be automatically classified as failed, churned, or non-activated merely because the calendar timebox ended.

The timebox SHALL instead force a decision about the evidence state: continue observing, classify as incubation, rework, hold, or stop.

No universal incubation duration is constitutionalized.

# 44. Sample Size Discipline

The Constitution does not impose universal sample sizes.

Sample adequacy depends on:

- decision consequence;
- effect size;
- market size;
- cost;
- reversibility.

A small sample may justify a reversible next step.

It may not justify a universal claim.

---

# 45. Statistical Significance

Statistical methods SHOULD be used where appropriate.

However:

> **statistical significance is not strategic significance.**

A tiny conversion improvement may be statistically real but commercially irrelevant.

---

# 46. Directional Evidence

During Baby, directional evidence may be sufficient for reversible decisions.

The evidence grade SHALL remain honest.

---

# 47. Experiment Pre-Registration

For material tests, the program SHOULD record before launch:

- hypothesis;
- metric;
- success;
- failure;
- decision.

This may be a few lines.

The purpose is avoiding post-hoc goal manipulation.

---

# 48. Decision Gate Definition

A **Decision Gate** is a predefined point at which evidence determines whether Zyppi should:

- continue;
- deepen;
- scale;
- rework;
- stop.

Decision Gates prevent endless experimentation without strategic consequence.

---

# 49. Gate Types

The Marketing branch recognizes:

## Gate G0 — Research Gate
Enough E2 evidence to justify a real test?

## Gate G1 — Problem Gate
Problem exists strongly enough?

## Gate G2 — Adoption Gate
Will users start?

## Gate G3 — Value Gate
Do users receive value?

## Gate G4 — Economic Gate
Will expected economic participation occur?

## Gate G5 — Retention Gate
Does value persist?

## Gate G6 — Scale Gate
Can distribution and economics repeat?

## Gate G7 — Expansion Gate
Can deeper value or new buyers emerge?

## Gate G8 — Defensibility Gate
Does dependence begin to emerge?

Not every program must pass all gates.

---

# 50. Gate Sequence Is Not Universal

A free infrastructure program may validate adoption before economic conversion.

An enterprise product may test payment early.

Programs MAY alter sequence.

They SHALL state why.

---

# 51. Kill Gate

A **Kill Gate** is a Decision Gate where predefined failure should terminate or materially demote the hypothesis.

Examples:

- no real buyer;
- repeated non-payment;
- impossible CAC;
- excessive human support;
- Feature Gravity;
- no strategic carryover.

Kill Gates protect scarce resources.

---

# 52. Kill Does Not Mean Forget

A killed hypothesis SHALL remain in Market Memory with:

- reason;
- evidence;
- date;
- reconsideration trigger.

This prevents repetitive rediscovery.

---

# 53. Preserve Gate

A failed paid product may pass a Preserve Gate as:

- free doorway;
- activation layer;
- infrastructure primitive.

Role-specific outcomes remain governed by `Marketing-007`.

---

# 54. Scale Gate

Scaling SHOULD require stronger evidence than launching.

Possible Scale Gate evidence:

- repeated activation;
- repeat channel performance;
- acceptable unit economics;
- bounded support;
- reliability.

---

# 55. Engineering Scale Gate

Major engineering investment SHOULD follow proof that the market risk it addresses is sufficiently reduced.

Avoid:

```text
build scale
before
validate demand
```

unless architecture requires it for safety or continuity.

---

# 56. Marketing Spend Gate

Material paid acquisition SHOULD generally follow evidence that:

- activation works;
- retention is plausible;
- acquisition economics can be measured.

Do not buy traffic to an unvalidated funnel solely to create activity.

---

# 57. Enterprise Sales Gate

Hiring or scaling enterprise sales SHOULD require evidence that:

- buyer exists;
- contract value supports sales cost;
- Buyer Bridge works or direct buying motion exists.

---

# 58. Partnership Scale Gate

A partner program SHOULD scale only after evidence that partners:

- understand the value;
- have incentive;
- produce qualified adoption.

Partner logos are not distribution validation.

---

# 59. Experiment Metrics

Every experiment SHOULD designate:

## Primary Metric
The behavior most directly tied to the hypothesis.

## Secondary Metrics
Useful context.

## Guardrail Metrics
Signals that prevent false success.

Example:

```text
Primary:
paid activation

Guardrail:
support hours
```

---

# 60. Vanity Metric Protection

Metrics such as:

- impressions;
- raw traffic;
- accounts;
- identities;
- scans;

may be useful diagnostics.

They SHALL NOT substitute for the validated behavior the experiment targets.

---

# 61. Counter-Metric

Where practical, important experiments SHOULD identify a counter-metric.

Example:

```text
Higher conversion
but
massive support burden
```

may indicate false economic success.

---

# 62. Experiment Ethics

Zyppi SHALL not create harmful or materially deceptive experiments.

Examples prohibited:

- fabricating trust;
- pretending verification occurred;
- executing consequential actions without proper authority;
- taking payment for unavailable service without clear disclosure.

---

# 63. Evidence Integrity in Experiments

A test involving Evidence or Trust SHALL preserve the constitutional difference between:

- observed signal;
- Evidence;
- verification;
- Trust;
- authorization.

Experiments SHALL not lower epistemic standards for convenience.

---

# 64. Safety and Consequence

Higher-consequence experiments require stronger safeguards.

A landing-page test may be low consequence.

A governed execution test may require:

- Authority;
- Standing;
- policy;
- Proof;
- Receipt.

---

# 65. Customer Consent

Where an experiment materially changes customer experience, risk, or data use, appropriate consent or disclosure SHOULD be provided.

---

# 66. Experiment Segmentation

Results SHOULD be analyzed by relevant segment where behavior materially differs.

An aggregate result can hide:

- strong developer adoption;
- weak enterprise adoption.

---

# 67. Cohort Discipline

Retention and expansion SHOULD be analyzed by cohort.

Mixing old and new participants may distort learning.

---

# 68. Contradictory Results

If experiments conflict, Zyppi SHOULD examine:

- segment;
- channel;
- buyer;
- message;
- timing;
- product version.

Contradiction SHALL be preserved.

---

# 69. Experiment Replication

A surprising result SHOULD ideally be replicated before major irreversible commitment.

Replication increases confidence toward E4.

---

# 70. External Replication

If another company demonstrated similar behavior, it can strengthen E2.

It does not substitute for Zyppi replication.

---

# 71. Geographic Replication

A result in one geography SHALL not automatically generalize globally.

Plans SHOULD state geographic scope.

---

# 72. Buyer Replication

A result with one buyer role SHALL not automatically generalize to another buying center.

---

# 73. Price Replication

A willingness-to-pay result at one price SHALL not automatically validate another price.

---

# 74. Product Version Replication

Material product changes may require renewed validation.

---

# 75. Experiment Failure Diagnosis

A failed test may result from:

- hypothesis failure;
- poor execution;
- weak channel;
- unclear message;
- reliability issue.

Diagnosis should be evidence-based.

Failure SHALL not automatically be blamed on execution to protect the thesis.

---

# 76. Rework Rule

A failed experiment may justify REWORK only if:

- a plausible cause is identified;
- the new test materially changes the hypothesis.

Repeated cosmetic changes are not strategic learning.

---

# 77. Hold Rule

Use HOLD when:

- evidence is contradictory;
- sample is inadequate;
- timing is distorted;
- infrastructure is unreliable.

HOLD is not a permanent parking lot.

A review trigger is required.

---

# 78. Experiment Backlog

Programs MAY maintain a lightweight experiment backlog.

Prioritize by:

```text
Decision Impact
×
Uncertainty
×
Reversibility
÷
Experiment Cost
```

The expression is conceptual.

---

# 79. P0 Experiment

A P0 experiment addresses an existential uncertainty.

Examples:

- no buyer;
- no adoption;
- impossible economics.

P0 should receive priority.

---

# 80. P1 Experiment

A P1 test addresses material optimization:

- buyer;
- positioning;
- pricing;
- channel.

---

# 81. P2 Experiment

A P2 test improves:

- copy;
- minor UX;
- packaging.

P2 SHALL not delay P0 market truth.

---

# 82. Experiment Velocity

The Marketing branch adopts:

> **Run the fastest ethically valid experiment capable of resolving the highest-impact uncertainty.**

---

# 83. Learning Velocity

The purpose of experiment velocity is not maximum test count.

It is:

```text
important decisions improved
per
unit of time and capital
```

---

# 84. Experiment Debt

When a major assumption remains untested while Zyppi builds around it, the organization accumulates **Experiment Debt**.

Examples:

- buyer assumption;
- WTP;
- Buyer Bridge;
- distribution;
- retention.

High Experiment Debt increases strategic risk.

---

# 85. Engineering Debt vs Experiment Debt

A startup may rationally accept some technical debt.

It should avoid unknowingly accumulating huge strategic Experiment Debt.

---

# 86. Validation Before Optimization

The preferred sequence is:

```text
validate the behavior
then
optimize the behavior
```

Do not optimize conversion on a proposition whose job is unvalidated.

---

# 87. Optimization Ceiling

An optimization experiment SHALL not be used to conceal a failed core thesis.

Example:

```text
0.1% conversion
→ button-color tests
```

when the real problem is weak demand.

---

# 88. Experiment Stop Rule

Stop an experiment when:

- success condition is clearly met;
- failure condition is clearly met;
- sample/time boundary is reached;
- external event invalidates the test.

Do not extend merely to seek a preferred outcome.

---

# 89. Program Stop Rule

Stop or rework a program when repeated experiments fail the same core assumption.

Persistence is not evidence.

---

# 90. Evidence Promotion Gate

A claim may be promoted from E2 to E3 only when a real Zyppi market event supports that exact claim.

A claim may be promoted to E4 only after sufficient repetition.

Promotion SHALL be recorded.

---

# 91. Evidence Demotion

Evidence may be demoted when:

- market changes;
- product changes;
- behavior stops repeating;
- source becomes obsolete.

Validation is not permanent.

---

# 92. Decision Receipt

A material experiment SHOULD end with a lightweight Decision Receipt:


---

# 93. Minimum Viable Experiment Governance

The artifact SHOULD be as lightweight as possible.

A few structured lines may be sufficient.

The objective is not paperwork.

The objective is preserving:

- what was tested;
- what happened;
- what decision changed.

---

# 93A. No Governance Sign-Off Bottleneck

Experiment and Decision Receipts exist for institutional memory, not permission theater.

For low-consequence reversible experiments, completion of the lightweight record SHALL NOT require Council review or formal pre-approval unless another constitutional or legal rule requires it.

The organization SHOULD automate or standardize experiment logging where practical.

Escalation is appropriate when an experiment creates consequential Trust or execution risk, makes a long-lived external commitment, materially changes market identity, or crosses constitutional boundaries.

> **Record the decision basis. Do not turn the record into the decision bottleneck.**

# 94. No Experiment Theater

The Marketing branch prohibits:

- tests designed only to confirm;
- vanity metrics presented as validation;
- changing success criteria after results;
- experiments whose result cannot change action.

---

# 95. No Interview Theater

Conducting many interviews without changing the hypothesis or decision is not validation progress.

Interview count is not evidence quality.

---

# 96. No Pilot Theater

A pilot that requires:

- heavy custom work;
- free consulting;
- founder attention;

may create learning.

It SHALL not be described as scalable adoption evidence without appropriate qualification.

---

# 97. No Revenue Theater

One customer payment does not establish:

- repeatable WTP;
- retention;
- scalable market.

It may create E3 economic evidence for that scope.

---

# 98. No Scale Theater

High traffic obtained through temporary promotion does not prove durable distribution.

# 100. Commerce Activation Requirement

# 109. Constitutional Invariants

The following are proposed as ratifiable invariants.

## Invariant 1
Research ≠ Experiment ≠ Validation.

## Invariant 2
Every meaningful experiment SHALL be capable of changing a decision.

## Invariant 3
Success and failure conditions SHOULD be defined before results.

## Invariant 4
Null and negative results SHALL be preserved.

## Invariant 5
Validation is claim-specific and SHALL not exceed the experiment's scope.

## Invariant 6
Problem, solution, adoption, value, economic, retention, expansion, distribution, and moat validation SHALL remain distinct.

## Invariant 7
E3 requires real Zyppi market behavior.

## Invariant 8
E4 requires repeated Zyppi evidence.

## Invariant 9
The smallest decision-relevant experiment is preferred.

## Invariant 10
Irreversible commitments require stronger prior evidence than reversible experiments.

## Invariant 11
A failed hypothesis may be preserved in a different strategic role.

## Invariant 12
Scaling requires stronger evidence than launching.

## Invariant 13
Experiment metrics SHALL prioritize behavior over vanity activity.

## Invariant 14
Experiment ethics SHALL preserve Evidence, Trust, Authority, and consequence boundaries.

## Invariant 15
Experiment governance SHALL remain lightweight.

## Invariant 16
Repeated failure of the same core assumption SHALL trigger rework or termination.

## Invariant 17
Evidence may be promoted or demoted as Reality changes.

## Invariant 18
No future domain may skip real validation because the platform architecture is universal.

## Invariant 19
Experiment timeboxes SHALL distinguish Zyppi-controlled learning velocity from external physical, procurement, regulatory, or organizational lead time.

## Invariant 20

## Invariant 21
Low-consequence experiment logging SHALL not require Council sign-off by default.

# 114. Constitutional Success Criteria

`Marketing-011` succeeds when:

- Zyppi moves quickly from E2 analysis into E3 Reality;
- every major experiment can change a decision;
- failure criteria are explicit;
- null and contradictory results remain visible;
- validation classes remain separate;
- real payment, retention, and expansion are not simulated;
- experiments remain small and reversible where possible;
- scaling waits for stronger evidence;
- experiment governance does not become bureaucracy;
- current Commerce and future domains use the same evidence discipline;
- failed hypotheses save Zyppi resources rather than becoming institutional embarrassment.

# 115. Closing Principle

Zyppi should reason aggressively.

It should research deeply enough to avoid obvious mistakes.

But Market Reality cannot be reached through documents alone.

Eventually the system must be placed in front of a real actor who is free to:

```text
ignore
adopt
pay
leave
return
expand
replace
```

Those choices create the evidence architecture cannot manufacture.

The Marketing branch therefore adopts:

> **Research until the next uncertainty belongs to Reality. Then run the smallest ethical experiment that can change the decision. Define success and failure before launch. Preserve the result. Scale only what survives repeated contact with real participants.**

And:

> **A failed experiment is not a failure of Zyppi. It is a successful purchase of truth—provided the organization actually changes its decision because of what Reality revealed.**
