# 0. Purpose

This doctrine exists to prevent Zyppi from drifting into five failure modes:

1. **Philosophical abstraction** — describing “Reality Sync” without a concrete operational problem.
2. **Integration-category collapse** — becoming another system-integration or enterprise-service-bus product.
3. **Centralization drift** — attempting to create one enterprise “golden truth” that overrides legitimate systems, departments, or authorities.
4. **Opinion-led validation** — requiring individual interviewees to evaluate a distributed cross-functional architecture they do not individually own.
5. **Self-confirming research** — collecting only evidence that proves fragmentation is bad while ignoring cases where separation is intentional, cheap, adequate, or safer.

This doctrine establishes:

- the precise pain Zyppi is attempting to solve;
- the role of the Five Reality Primitives and Golden Question;
- the distinction between **node problems** and **edge problems**;
- the difference between **federation** and **unification**;
- the existential hypotheses Zyppi must prove;
- a **research-first validation model**;
- a reduced and later role for interviews;
- the proper role of the GS1/DPP Trojan Horse as an empirical deployment mechanism;
- telemetry limits;
- controlled intervention tests;
- economic and institutional proof gates;
- explicit falsification conditions.

The governing maxim is:

> **The architecture has earned the right to be tested. It has not earned the right to be believed. Reality gets the vote.**

# 2. The Fundamental Pain

The fundamental Zyppi pain SHALL NOT be stated merely as:

> “The digital world cannot perceive Reality.”

That statement may remain philosophically true and constitutionally useful, but it is not sufficient as a market pain statement.

The operational pain is:

> **Consequential actions increasingly cross fragmented systems, actors, organizations, and authorities, while no single participant possesses the complete trusted context required to determine what is true enough, authorized enough, and current enough for the action to safely proceed.**

This produces one or more of:

- manual reconciliation;
- delayed decisions;
- inconsistent outcomes;
- stale decisions;
- unauthorized actions;
- avoidable errors;
- duplicated logic;
- audit reconstruction;
- fraud exposure;
- compliance exposure;
- inability to automate safely;
- inability to explain later why an action was permitted or denied.

This is the primary problem class Zyppi SHALL investigate and attempt to solve.

# 3. Fragmentation Itself Is Not the Enemy

Zyppi SHALL NOT assume that all fragmentation is bad.

Separate systems and organizational boundaries may exist legitimately because:

- different departments have different authority;
- legal duties require segregation;
- privacy rules restrict visibility;
- suppliers remain independent;
- regulators must remain independent;
- systems have distinct operational responsibilities;
- organizations intentionally distribute risk;
- one party must not silently become the owner of another party's truth.

Therefore the thesis is NOT:

```text
Fragmentation
→ bad
→ unify everything
```

The thesis is:

```text
Legitimate distributed sources
        ↓
a consequential decision crosses them
        ↓
no node alone has decision-sufficient context
        ↓
manual / brittle / duplicated reconciliation occurs
        ↓
Zyppi MAY create value at the decision edge
```

# 4. Node vs Edge

A **node** is an actor, department, organization, system, sensor, registry, authority, or data source with a bounded responsibility.

An **edge** is a relationship, handoff, conflict, decision, or action that requires context from more than one node.

Examples:

```text
NODE:
ERP

NODE:
WMS

EDGE:
“May this shipment be released?”
```

```text
NODE:
Product record

NODE:
Technician credential

NODE:
Warranty policy

EDGE:
“May this repair be authorized?”
```

```text
NODE:
Supplier declaration

NODE:
Lifecycle record

NODE:
Regulatory rule

EDGE:
“May this product be represented as compliant / eligible?”
```

Zyppi's highest-value problem is expected to occur primarily at **edges**, not inside nodes.

The doctrine therefore adopts:

> **Do not ask one node to explain the whole edge.**

Zyppi SHALL ultimately pass three separate tests.

## Test A — Computational

# 12. Why Conventional Interviews Are Secondary

Broad conventional interviews SHALL NOT be the primary method for establishing whether the fundamental problem family exists.

Reasons:

1. **Distributed ownership**  
   No individual may own the entire cross-system problem.

2. **Node-local cognition**  
   Individuals naturally report the pain within their own responsibility.

3. **High explanation burden**  
   Explaining the complete Zyppi architecture can contaminate the response and consume disproportionate time.

4. **Stated preference weakness**  
   Interviewees may approve or reject an imagined system without behavioral commitment.

5. **Cost**  
   Recruiting appropriate enterprise actors can be expensive and slow relative to desk evidence and telemetry.

6. **Founder resource constraint**  
   Zyppi must use scarce human attention where it produces the highest information gain.

Therefore:

> **Zyppi SHALL prefer observed incidents, empirical research, public investigations, regulatory evidence, operational telemetry, and controlled interventions before relying on broad stated-preference interviews.**

# 13. Interviews Are Deferred — Not Banned

Interviews remain useful when they answer questions other evidence cannot answer efficiently.

They SHOULD occur later and narrowly for:

- incident reconstruction;
- internal authority boundaries;
- why a human overrode or accepted a decision;
- procurement path;
- permission model;
- organizational trust;
- workflow semantics;
- budget ownership;
- willingness to pay;
- reasons for non-adoption.

The preferred interview format is NOT:

> “Do you need Zyppi?”

It is:

> “Tell me what happened in this specific incident.”

or:

> “When this system disagreed with that system, how was the decision actually made?”

or:

> “If a system had held this exact action and shown these exact sources, would that have helped, interfered, or been ignored?”

Interviews SHALL be **incident-bound and decision-bound**, not ontology-bound.

# 18. External Evidence Baseline — Seed Findings

The following evidence already establishes that the problem family is not imaginary.

## EB-01 — Provenance Is an Established Trust Problem

W3C PROV defines provenance as information about entities, activities, and people involved in producing data or things, and explicitly states that provenance can support assessments of quality, reliability, and trustworthiness.

**Implication:** the need to know where information came from and how it was produced is independently established.

**Does Not Prove:** that Zyppi is needed.

**Source:**  
W3C, *PROV-Overview*, 2013.  
https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/

---

## EB-02 — Cross-Organization Event Visibility Is an Established Supply-Chain Problem

GS1 EPCIS exists to capture and share interoperable visibility information across organizations and trading partners, including the “what, when, where, why and how” of products and assets, status, location, movement, and chain of custody.

**Implication:** real operations already require cross-system and cross-party event context.

**Does Not Prove:** that Zyppi should replace EPCIS or own all event data.

**Source:**  
GS1, *EPCIS & CBV*.  
https://www.gs1.org/standards/epcis

---

## EB-03 — Physical Reality and System Records Commonly Diverge

DeHoratius and Raman examined nearly 370,000 inventory records across 37 stores and found 65% inaccurate.

**Implication:** system representations can materially diverge from physical Reality at large scale.

**Does Not Prove:** that every industry has the same rate or that Zyppi solves inventory accuracy.

**Source:**  
DeHoratius, N. & Raman, A. (2008), *Inventory Record Inaccuracy: An Empirical Analysis*, Management Science 54(4):627–641.  
https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1070.0789

---

## EB-04 — Record Inaccuracy Has Operational and Economic Consequences

A later peer-reviewed study using more than 5.25 million inventory items across 81 retail stores found inventory record inaccuracy affects inventory and sales management performance; the study notes stockouts and revenue losses as consequences.

**Implication:** mismatch between digital representation and physical state can produce real operational cost.

**Does Not Prove:** that a generalized Trust layer is the optimal remedy.

**Source:**  
Shabani, A., Maroti, G., de Leeuw, S., Dullaert, W. (2021), *Inventory record inaccuracy and store-level performance*, International Journal of Production Economics 235, 108111.  
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpe.2021.108111

---

## EB-05 — Traceability Failures Can Exist Across the Supply Chain

FDA reports that outbreak source identification can be slowed by lack of records and non-harmonized data elements as food moves through the supply chain. Its Food Traceability framework requires Key Data Elements associated with Critical Tracking Events to enable faster tracing and removal of contaminated products.

**Implication:** fragmented records across independent actors can materially impair high-consequence decisions.

**Does Not Prove:** that Zyppi should become a food traceability platform.

**Sources:**  
FDA, *CORE 2023 Annual Report*.  
https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/core-2023-annual-report

FDA, *Tracking and Tracing of Food*.  
https://www.fda.gov/food/new-era-smarter-food-safety/tracking-and-tracing-food

---

## EB-06 — DPP Architecture Is Explicitly Decentralized

The European Commission states that the DPP Registry is a common EU-level index while detailed product data remains under the responsibility of economic operators, who may host it themselves or use service providers.

The underlying rules require access controls, data authentication, reliability, integrity, security, privacy, and fraud avoidance.

**Implication:** modern regulatory architecture can require trusted cross-system state while deliberately avoiding one centralized data owner.

**Strategic Relevance:** this strongly supports Zyppi's **federated thin-layer** direction over a replacement-database strategy.

**Does Not Prove:** Zyppi demand or differentiation.

**Sources:**  
European Commission, *The DPP Registry*.  
https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport/dpp-registry_en

Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, EUR-Lex.  
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:02024R1781-20240628

---

## EB-07 — Provenance Is Recognized as Risk-Relevant in Supply Chains

NIST supply-chain risk guidance describes provenance as recording origin, change history, and who made changes; it states provenance improves traceability after adverse events and is critical for understanding and mitigating risks.

**Implication:** provenance is not an abstract software concern.

**Does Not Prove:** Zyppi's particular model.

**Source:**  
NIST SP 800-161, *Supply Chain Risk Management Practices*.  
https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-161.pdf

---

## EB-08 — Trustworthy Automated Decision Systems Require More Than Data Access

NIST's AI RMF identifies validity/reliability, accountability/transparency, safety, security/resilience, explainability, privacy, and fairness as trustworthiness characteristics. It also treats AI as socio-technical and emphasizes roles, responsibilities, and documented risk-management processes.

**Implication:** as automated agents gain capability, “data exists” is insufficient for safe action.

**Does Not Prove:** that Zyppi is an AI-governance product.

**Source:**  
NIST AI RMF 1.0.  
https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10

---

## EB-09 — Enterprise Unification Itself Can Fail

IBM documents that centralized ESB architectures, while useful in many organizations, often became bottlenecks: shared changes could destabilize integrations, centralized teams created queues, availability costs grew, and cross-enterprise funding became difficult.

AWS similarly notes centralized ESB complexity, bottlenecks, latency, specialized maintenance, and potential single points of failure.

**Implication:** “connect everything centrally” is not automatically beneficial.

**Strategic Relevance:** Zyppi must remain a bounded federated decision layer, not recreate the ESB.

**Sources:**  
IBM, *What Is an Enterprise Service Bus (ESB)?*  
https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/esb

AWS, *What is an Enterprise Service Bus?*  
https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/enterprise-service-bus/

---

## EB-10 — Data-Silo Resolution Is Organizational, Not Merely Technical

A 32-month ethnographic MDM study found numerous challenges in bridging organizational and information-system silos, including data ownership, definitions, legislation, domain granularity, and organizational alignment.

**Implication:** centralization can fail because responsibilities and authority are organizationally real.

**Strategic Relevance:** Zyppi SHALL preserve legitimate boundaries rather than assume one canonical “golden record” should replace them.

**Source:**  
Vilminko-Heikkinen, R. & Pekkola, S. (2017), *Master data management and its organizational implementation*, Journal of Enterprise Information Management 30(3):454–475.  
https://doi.org/10.1108/JEIM-07-2015-0070

---

## EB-11 — Counterfeit Trade Demonstrates Real Supply-Chain Trust Exposure

OECD's 2025 analysis estimates counterfeit and pirated goods represented up to 2.3% of global trade and up to 4.7% of EU imports using 2021 customs-seizure data, while noting increasingly complex global supply chains.

**Implication:** supply-chain trust and authenticity problems can carry large economic and safety consequences.

**Does Not Prove:** that Zyppi can detect counterfeit goods, or that provenance alone proves authenticity.

**Source:**  
OECD, *Mapping Global Trade in Fakes 2025*.  
https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/mapping-global-trade-in-fakes-2025_94d3b29f-en.html

# 19. Evidence Interpretation Rule

The External Evidence Baseline establishes:

> **The problem family is sufficiently observed externally to justify building and testing Zyppi.**

It does NOT establish:

> **A universal market already wants Zyppi.**

Therefore Zyppi SHALL NOT spend scarce founder resources trying to repeatedly prove through broad interviews that fragmentation, record inaccuracy, provenance problems, traceability failures, or cross-system coordination problems exist.

Those classes are externally documented.

The unresolved questions are narrower:

1. Can Zyppi solve them?
2. Can Zyppi solve them without creating a worse centralization problem?
3. Does Zyppi materially outperform current workarounds?
4. Will organizations trust its decisions?
5. Can value be exposed with low adoption friction?
6. Will organizations retain, expand, and pay for deeper capability?

The doctrine adopts:
