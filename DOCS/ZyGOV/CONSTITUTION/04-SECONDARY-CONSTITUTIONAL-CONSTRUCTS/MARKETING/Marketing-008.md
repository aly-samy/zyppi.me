# 0. Purpose

Zyppi can solve a real problem and still fail commercially if it chooses the wrong buyer.

The person who experiences the problem may not control the budget.

The person who integrates the API may not own the business outcome.

The person who approves procurement may not use the product.

The executive who signs the contract may not understand the technical architecture.

The compliance team may care deeply about Trust while the developer who first adopted Zyppi cares only about a fast API.

Therefore:

> **A market does not contain one buyer. It contains a buying system.**

`Marketing-008` establishes the constitutional doctrine governing:

- users;
- buyers;
- economic buyers;
- technical buyers;
- approvers;
- blockers;
- champions;
- beneficiaries;
- Jobs-To-Be-Done;
- buyer urgency;
- segment qualification;
- buying centers;
- Buyer Bridges;
- entry buyer vs deeper buyer;
- segment progression;
- buyer evidence;
- market prioritization.

The central rule is:

> **Zyppi SHALL design the platform universally, but sell each market expression to a specific actor with a specific job, trigger, alternative, and authority.**

---

# 1. Governing Distinction — Market Is Not Buyer

A market may contain:

- thousands of organizations;
- multiple professions;
- several economic incentives;
- different regulatory pressures;
- multiple users of the same system;
- many separate budgets.

Therefore:

```text
Market
≠
Segment
≠
Organization
≠
Buying Center
≠
Buyer
≠
User
```

These objects SHALL remain distinct.

---

# 2. Buyer Reality Principle

The Marketing branch adopts:

> **A product does not have a buyer because Zyppi needs one. A buyer exists only where an identifiable actor has sufficient problem ownership, authority, urgency, and access to resources to act.**

Buyer assumptions SHALL remain evidence-bound under `Marketing-001`.

---

# 3. Jobs-To-Be-Done Principle

A buyer should be understood through the progress they are trying to make in a real situation.

The preferred structure is:

> **When [situation], I need to [job], so I can [desired outcome].**

The Job SHALL be expressible without requiring Zyppi-specific terminology.

Weak:

> "I need a Reality Sync platform."

Stronger:

> "When my product links must serve different trusted resources across markets, I need one reliable way to control those destinations without rebuilding packaging."

The precise wording remains market-specific.

The constitutional principle is universal.

---

# 4. Job Before Architecture

The Marketing branch adopts:

> **The buyer's immediate job precedes the explanation of Zyppi's architecture.**

The internal chain may be:

```text
Evidence
→ Authority
→ Standing
→ Policy
→ Capability
→ Execution
→ Receipt
```

The buyer may simply need:

> "Prevent unauthorized service actions."

Both expressions can be true.

The first belongs to architecture.

The second belongs to buyer value.

---

# 5. Universal Platform, Specific Buyer

The North Star remains universal.

The external buyer proposition SHALL remain specific.

This relationship is:

```text
Universal Zyppi Platform
        ↓
Domain Expression
        ↓
Specific Use Case
        ↓
Specific Buyer
        ↓
Specific Job
        ↓
Specific Outcome
```

A market-facing proposition SHALL NOT require the buyer to understand every future domain Zyppi may eventually serve.

---

# 6. Buyer Role Architecture

The Marketing branch recognizes the following roles.

One person may occupy more than one role.

## 6.1 User

The person who directly uses or operates the Zyppi capability.

## 6.2 Technical Buyer

The actor responsible for determining whether Zyppi is technically acceptable.

## 6.3 Economic Buyer

The actor with authority over the budget or commercial commitment.

## 6.4 Operational Buyer

The actor accountable for the operational problem or workflow.

## 6.5 Approver

An actor whose consent is required.

## 6.6 Blocker

An actor capable of preventing adoption.

## 6.7 Champion

An actor who actively advocates for Zyppi inside the organization.

## 6.8 Beneficiary

An actor who receives value without necessarily using or purchasing Zyppi.

---

# 7. Buyer Role Separation

Marketing SHALL NOT assume:

```text
User = Buyer
```

or:

```text
Technical Buyer = Economic Buyer
```

or:

```text
Champion = Approver
```

Every material market program SHOULD identify the relevant buying center.

---

# 8. Buying Center Definition

A **Buying Center** is the set of actors whose combined decisions determine whether Zyppi is:

- discovered;
- evaluated;
- approved;
- purchased;
- deployed;
- retained;
- expanded.

A buying center may be simple for self-service utilities.

It may be complex for enterprise execution.

---

# 9. Buying Center Complexity

A market program SHOULD classify buying-center complexity.

## BC0 — Individual

One actor can decide and act.

## BC1 — Small Team

A user may require one manager or technical approver.

## BC2 — Cross-Functional

Multiple functions influence the decision.

## BC3 — Enterprise Governance

Procurement, security, legal, operations, risk, and executive sponsorship may be required.

Higher complexity increases sales cycle, implementation friction, human-resource burden, and evidence requirements.

---

# 10. Buyer Bridge Definition

A **Buyer Bridge** is the mechanism by which value discovered by one actor becomes sufficiently visible and credible to another actor who controls a deeper purchase or organizational decision.

Example:

```text
Developer adopts free resolver
        ↓
Operational reliability becomes visible
        ↓
Operations manager adopts policy
        ↓
Risk team sees verification value
        ↓
Enterprise governance contract
```

The bridge SHALL NOT be assumed.

It must be designed or validated.

---

# 11. Capability Progression ≠ Buyer Progression

Zyppi capabilities may deepen continuously:

```text
Access
→ Observation
→ Policy
→ Trust
→ Execution
```

The buyer journey may be discontinuous.

Therefore:

> **Platform depth may be continuous even when organizational buying is not.**

This is a constitutional distinction.

---

# 12. Entry Buyer

An **Entry Buyer/User** is the actor who can adopt the first useful Zyppi expression with the least friction.

An Entry Buyer SHOULD ideally have:

- direct access to the problem;
- sufficient authority for initial use;
- low procurement friction;
- high time-to-value.

The Entry Buyer may not be the long-term economic buyer.

---

# 13. Expansion Buyer

An **Expansion Buyer** controls a deeper capability, budget, or organizational decision after Zyppi has already established value.

A market plan SHALL identify the bridge between entry and expansion where relevant.

---

# 14. Institutional Buyer

At advanced maturity, Zyppi may encounter buyers such as governments, regulators, large enterprises, industry bodies, and infrastructure operators.

These buyers may value governance, auditability, interoperability, continuity, sovereign control, and Trust.

These buyers SHALL NOT define Baby-stage positioning.

---

# 15. Constitutional Law — Buyer Job Must Be Immediate

The entry proposition SHOULD answer:

> **What immediate problem does this buyer need solved now?**

The answer SHOULD NOT be:

> "Because Zyppi may become important infrastructure in five years."

Future strategic value may support the investment thesis.

It does not replace current buyer value.

---

# 16. Constitutional Law — Why Now Must Exist

The Marketing branch strongly prefers a clear trigger.

Possible triggers include:

- compliance deadline;
- retailer mandate;
- operational failure;
- cost pressure;
- integration change;
- product launch;
- customer demand;
- security issue;
- AI automation need.

Without a trigger, adoption may face status quo inertia.

---

# 17. Constitutional Law — Do Nothing Is a Buyer Choice

The buyer's alternative is often:

> **wait**

or:

> **continue current process**

A market proposition SHALL explain why action is preferable.

---

# 18. Buyer Pain Architecture

Pain SHOULD be evaluated across:

- Functional Pain;
- Economic Pain;
- Risk Pain;
- Time Pain;
- Cognitive Pain;
- Political / Organizational Pain;
- Compliance Pain.

A strong buyer proposition may address one or several.

---

# 19. Pain Is Not Budget

A user can experience severe pain without access to a budget.

The Market Program SHALL identify:

- pain owner;
- budget owner;
- Buyer Bridge.

---

# 20. Budget Is Not Urgency

An organization may have budget but no trigger.

A budget category does not prove immediate willingness to purchase.

---

# 21. Urgency Is Not Market Size

A highly urgent niche can be commercially useful.

A large market with weak urgency can be difficult.

Market prioritization SHALL consider both.

---

# 22. Segment Definition

A **Segment** is a group of potential participants whose Job, buying process, urgency, alternatives, economics, and adoption conditions are sufficiently similar to support a coherent strategy.

A segment SHOULD NOT be defined only by demographic or company-size labels.

---

# 23. Segment Quality

A useful segment should allow Zyppi to answer:

- What job?
- Who uses?
- Who pays?
- Why now?
- What alternative?
- What price logic?
- What distribution?
- What proof?

If these differ radically within the group, the segment may be too broad.

---

# 24. Company Size Is Not a Complete Segment

Labels such as:

```text
SMB
Mid-Market
Enterprise
```

may be useful.

They are insufficient alone.

---

# 25. Domain Is Not a Complete Segment

"Commerce" is a domain.

It is not automatically one buyer segment.

Commerce may contain:

- packaging teams;
- developers;
- manufacturers;
- retailers;
- compliance teams;
- system integrators.

The Market Program SHALL narrow further.

---

# 26. Use Case Is Not Always a Segment

A use case such as:

```text
GS1 Digital Link
```

may involve many buyer types.

Segmentation should follow real buying behavior, not only capability.

---

# 27. Segment Qualification Dimensions

A candidate segment SHOULD be evaluated across:

- problem severity;
- urgency;
- buyer accessibility;
- budget ownership;
- distribution accessibility;
- adoption friction;
- competitive intensity;
- willingness-to-pay evidence;
- Zyppi capability fit;
- implementation burden;
- expansion potential;
- compounding value.

---

# 28. Segment Qualification Scorecard

A plan MAY use:

| Dimension   | Core Question                              |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Pain        | Is the problem meaningful?                 |
| Urgency     | Why act now?                               |
| Buyer       | Who controls the decision?                 |
| Budget      | Does a relevant budget exist?              |
| Access      | Can Zyppi reach the buyer?                 |
| Alternative | What is used today?                        |
| Friction    | How hard is adoption?                      |
| WTP         | Is payment plausible/evidenced?            |
| Reuse       | Does Zyppi already possess the capability? |
| Support     | How much human effort?                     |
| Expansion   | Can value deepen?                          |
| Compounding | What durable asset remains?                |

A score SHALL NOT override a fatal buyer problem.

---

# 29. Segment Priority

A segment may be prioritized because it has acute pain, easy distribution, low adoption friction, low incumbent attention, strong reuse, high urgency, or strong compounding.

Highest theoretical market size is not automatically highest priority.

---

# 30. Segment Beachhead

A **Beachhead Segment** is the narrow group where Zyppi intentionally seeks its first repeatable market evidence.

A strong beachhead SHOULD be narrow enough for:

- clear messaging;
- specific product scope;
- repeatable distribution;
- comparable evidence.

It should still connect to broader strategic expansion.

---

# 31. Beachhead Is Not Permanent Identity

A beachhead may dominate current operational focus.

It SHALL NOT define Zyppi constitutionally.

---

# 32. Segment Expansion

Expansion may occur to a neighboring segment when:

- the core job is similar;
- capability reuse is high;
- distribution is accessible;
- implementation remains coherent.

Expansion should not be assumed solely because market size is attractive.

---

# 33. Jobs Architecture

A Market Program SHOULD distinguish:

## Core Job

The primary progress sought.

## Supporting Jobs

Adjacent tasks required to achieve the core job.

## Emotional Job

How the actor wants to feel.

## Social / Organizational Job

How the actor wants to be perceived or evaluated.

For infrastructure markets, functional and organizational jobs often dominate.

---

# 34. Job Statement Requirements

A strong job statement SHOULD identify:

- situation;
- desired progress;
- outcome;
- obstacle;
- current alternative.

Example:

```text
When [trigger],
I need to [progress],
without [major obstacle],
so that [outcome].
```

---

# 35. Product Feature Is Not Job

Weak:

> "Customer needs dynamic routing."

Stronger:

> "When product information changes across markets, the operations team needs to update destinations without changing physical packaging."

---

# 36. Architecture Primitive Is Not Job

Weak:

> "Customer needs Standing."

Stronger:

> "Before an automated action happens, the customer needs to know whether the actor is currently permitted to perform it."

The architecture may solve the job.

It is not the job.

---

# 37. Buyer Outcome

A Market Program SHOULD express the result in buyer language such as:

- reduce work;
- avoid failure;
- satisfy requirement;
- gain control;
- automate safely;
- reduce risk;
- launch faster;
- preserve continuity.

---

# 38. Buyer Value Hierarchy

The same capability may create different value for different actors.

Example:

```text
Developer:
less integration work

Operations:
central control

Compliance:
auditability

Executive:
lower risk / cost
```

Marketing SHOULD not use one universal benefit statement for all.

---

# 39. Buyer Message Firewall

The Marketing branch reinforces `Marketing-003`:

> **External first-contact messaging SHALL lead with the immediate buyer Job-To-Be-Done and current market category required for comprehension.**

The universal North Star may appear in company narrative, strategic documentation, investor materials, and deeper platform explanation.

It SHOULD NOT create unnecessary cognitive load at entry.

---

# 40. Category Familiarity

Buyers use existing categories to reduce uncertainty.

Where practical, Zyppi should anchor the first market expression in a category the buyer already understands.

The category may be narrower than Zyppi.

This is permitted.

---

# 41. Category Familiarity Is Not Category Imprisonment

The entry label may change as Zyppi expands.

The buyer-facing category is a comprehension tool.

It is not constitutional identity.

---

# 42. User-Led Adoption

Some products may begin through users who can act independently.

Examples:

- developer tool;
- utility;
- free standards service.

User-led adoption can reduce sales cycle, procurement, and marketing cost.

It may create Buyer Bridge challenges later.

---

# 43. Top-Down Adoption

Some high-consequence capabilities may require executive or institutional sponsorship from the beginning.

This may be appropriate for compliance, governance, sovereign infrastructure, or critical execution.

Top-down entry carries higher friction.

It SHOULD not be imposed on simple Baby-stage utility without need.

---

# 44. Bottom-Up to Top-Down Bridge

A common Zyppi progression may be:

```text
Bottom-Up Utility
        ↓
Observable Operational Value
        ↓
Managerial Recognition
        ↓
Enterprise Requirement
        ↓
Top-Down Contract
```

The bridge must be validated.

---

# 45. Champion Quality

A strong Champion usually has:

- real problem ownership;
- credibility;
- enough influence;
- incentive to change;
- evidence of Zyppi value.

A user who loves the product but has no organizational influence may not be a viable Champion.

---

# 46. Champion Risk

Overreliance on one Champion is risky.

If the Champion leaves, the project may die.

Enterprise programs SHOULD seek institutionalized value beyond one individual.

---

# 47. Blocker Analysis

A Market Program SHOULD identify predictable blockers such as:

- security;
- IT architecture;
- procurement;
- incumbent vendor;
- legal;
- compliance;
- budget;
- data residency.

Blockers should be addressed with evidence.

---

# 48. Procurement Is Part of Buyer Reality

Enterprise value can be strong while procurement remains impossible.

Therefore:

> **Commercial viability includes procurement viability.**

This may affect segment priority during Baby.

---

# 49. Distribution-Buyer Fit

A segment is stronger when the buyer can be reached efficiently through a natural channel.

Distribution doctrine is governed by `Marketing-009`.

---

# 50. Buyer-Product Fit

A capability should be exposed to the actor who can understand and act upon its value.

A technically advanced feature may fail if sold to a buyer who cannot interpret it.

---

# 51. Economic Buyer Value

The Economic Buyer generally needs evidence of:

- cost reduction;
- revenue increase;
- risk reduction;
- strategic necessity;
- operational leverage.

Technical elegance alone is weak.

---

# 52. Technical Buyer Value

The Technical Buyer may prioritize:

- standards;
- security;
- reliability;
- API quality;
- architecture;
- portability;
- integration cost.

---

# 53. Operational Buyer Value

The Operational Buyer may prioritize:

- control;
- speed;
- less manual work;
- fewer failures;
- visibility;
- automation.

---

# 54. Risk / Compliance Buyer Value

May prioritize:

- Evidence;
- traceability;
- policy;
- authorization;
- auditability;
- explainability;
- regulatory confidence.

This buyer may appear only at deeper Zyppi capability layers.

---

# 55. Developer Buyer Doctrine

Developers may be users, champions, technical buyers, and ecosystem multipliers.

They often have low procurement tolerance.

Developer products SHOULD prioritize:

- instant comprehension;
- self-service;
- documentation;
- predictable economics;
- production utility.

---

# 56. Developer Is Not Always Economic Buyer

A developer may successfully adopt Zyppi while having no authority to purchase:

- enterprise SLA;
- governance;
- compliance;
- Trust execution.

The Market Program SHALL define the later Buyer Bridge.

---

# 57. Small-Business Buyer Doctrine

Small organizations often combine roles.

The same person may be user, buyer, approver, and owner.

This can make adoption fast.

It can also reduce high-value enterprise expansion.

Both facts should be understood.

---

# 58. Enterprise Buyer Doctrine

Enterprise markets can offer larger budgets, deeper workflows, and stronger retention.

They also create long sales cycles, procurement, custom integration, and support burden.

Enterprise revenue SHALL not be assumed superior without accounting for human cost.

---

# 59. Government / Sovereign Buyer Doctrine

Government or sovereign buyers may prioritize institutional continuity, jurisdiction, transparency, governance, interoperability, and auditability.

These markets may require separate long-range plans.

They SHALL not distort Baby-stage entry.

---

# 60. Partner as Buyer / Channel

A partner may be buyer, reseller, integrator, distribution channel, or ecosystem participant.

Partner incentives SHALL be explicit.

A partner cannot be assumed to distribute Zyppi merely because the integration is technically possible.

---

# 61. Multi-Sided Buyer Systems

Some future Zyppi markets may involve several sides.

Example:

```text
Organization A publishes
Organization B verifies
Organization C executes
Consumer benefits
```

The Marketing branch SHALL identify:

- who pays;
- who receives value;
- who must participate;
- who can block adoption.

---

# 62. Beneficiary-Payer Separation

The actor receiving the largest benefit may not be the payer.

This is common in infrastructure markets.

The business model must reflect it.

---

# 63. Regulatory Buyer Fallacy

A regulator creating a requirement does not necessarily become a customer.

Similarly:

```text
regulation creates need
≠
government pays Zyppi
```

The actual budget owner must still be identified.

---

# 64. Standards Buyer Fallacy

A standards organization may influence adoption without becoming the buyer.

Participation in an ecosystem does not imply standards-body revenue.

---

# 65. Buyer Urgency Ladder

Programs MAY classify urgency:

## U0 — No Trigger

Interesting but optional.

## U1 — Improvement

Useful efficiency.

## U2 — Operational Need

Clear pain with near-term action.

## U3 — External Pressure

Customer, channel, audit, or regulation creates deadline.

## U4 — Critical

Material risk or inability to operate.

The classification guides prioritization.

It does not determine price automatically.

---

# 66. Buyer Authority Ladder

Programs MAY classify authority:

## A0 — User only

## A1 — Can trial

## A2 — Can purchase small amount

## A3 — Controls team/department budget

## A4 — Enterprise authority

This helps distinguish self-service from enterprise motion.

---

# 67. Buyer Accessibility

A buyer may be attractive but difficult to reach.

Accessibility includes:

- discoverability;
- contactability;
- channel access;
- procurement;
- language;
- geography.

A segment with high pain but inaccessible buyers may be weak for Baby.

---

# 68. Buyer Density

A market may contain many potential buyers with similar jobs and distribution paths.

Higher buyer density may support efficient acquisition.

Low-density markets may require expensive sales.

---

# 69. Segment Economics

Segment evaluation SHOULD consider:

```text
Expected Value
×
Reachability
×
Repeatability
÷
Human Burden
```

The formula is conceptual.

No fixed mathematical weighting is constitutionalized.

# 70. Segment Evidence

A segment hypothesis may progress:

```text
E1:
logical segment

E2:
external evidence of problem/budget

E3:
real Zyppi adoption/payment

E4:
repeatable segment behavior
```

Segment definitions SHOULD evolve as evidence improves.

---

# 71. Buyer Evidence

Evidence may include:

- interviews;
- observed adoption;
- trial behavior;
- pricing behavior;
- procurement behavior;
- retention;
- expansion.

AI buyer simulation does not create E3.

---

# 72. Interview Discipline

A customer interview may create evidence about:

- problem;
- process;
- alternative;
- urgency.

Statements about hypothetical payment are weaker than actual payment behavior.

Interview evidence should be scoped correctly.

---

# 73. Buyer Simulation

AI MAY simulate objections, stakeholder conflicts, and likely decision criteria.

It SHALL NOT be treated as evidence of actual buyer behavior.

Canonical rule remains:

> **Simulate decisions. Never simulate evidence.**

---

# 74. Buyer Discovery vs Buyer Validation

Buyer discovery asks:

> **Who might buy?**

Buyer validation asks:

> **Who actually acts, pays, retains, and expands?**

The first may be E1/E2.

The second requires E3/E4.

---

# 75. Buyer-Market Fit

A product may solve the right problem for the wrong organizational actor.

Buyer-Market Fit exists when:

- actor experiences or owns the problem;
- actor can influence action;
- buying process is viable;
- economics are viable.

---

# 76. Buyer Bridge Failure

A program SHOULD be reworked when:

- entry user loves Zyppi;
- deeper buyer does not care;
- no internal evidence transfer exists;
- economic buyer cannot see value;
- enterprise transition requires a new sale from zero.

This may mean the entry and expansion products are commercially disconnected.

---

# 77. Buyer Bridge Strength

A strong Buyer Bridge may include:

- visible operational metrics;
- audit artifacts;
- accumulated history;
- risk reduction;
- departmental usage;
- integration dependency;
- internal reporting.

The product should help value become legible to the next buyer.

---

# 78. Internal Value Evidence

Where appropriate, Zyppi SHOULD make it easy for an entry user to demonstrate value internally.

Examples:

- usage report;
- reliability improvement;
- workflow savings;
- policy history;
- audit receipt.

This can strengthen the Buyer Bridge.

---

# 78A. Bridge Artifact

Where a strategy depends upon movement from an entry user to a different economic or governance buyer, the current program SHOULD identify a concrete **Bridge Artifact** or equivalent evidence-transfer mechanism.

A Bridge Artifact is the observable output that makes entry-layer value legible to the next buying center. It may be:

- an operational report;
- a risk signal;
- an audit artifact;
- a measured saving;
- a policy history;
- a reliability record;
- a Receipt or other governed proof where appropriate.

The exact artifact is domain- and buyer-specific and SHALL NOT be constitutionalized here.

If no credible Bridge Artifact or equivalent mechanism can be identified, the Buyer Bridge remains a hypothesis and SHALL be treated accordingly under `Marketing-001` and `Marketing-007`.

---

# 79. Expansion Trigger

A deeper buyer should encounter Zyppi because a meaningful trigger emerges.

Examples:

```text
volume grows
→ operational controls needed

workflow becomes consequential
→ Trust needed

multiple teams depend on system
→ governance needed
```

Expansion should follow real need.

---

# 80. Cross-Sell Is Not Buyer Bridge

Showing a user another feature in the dashboard does not prove organizational expansion.

A Buyer Bridge requires transfer of value and authority.

---

# 81. Persona Anti-Pattern

The Marketing branch discourages superficial personas based on:

- age;
- personality;
- fictional names;
- irrelevant lifestyle traits.

Buyer architecture should prioritize:

- job;
- authority;
- pain;
- process;
- trigger;
- alternative.

---

# 82. ICP Anti-Pattern

An Ideal Customer Profile that consists only of company size, revenue, and sector is strategically incomplete.

A useful ICP must explain:

- why they need Zyppi;
- who buys;
- why now;
- how reached.

---

# 83. TAM-First Anti-Pattern

Large Total Addressable Market does not validate a buyer.

The Marketing branch rejects:

```text
large industry
→ therefore good segment
```

Segment quality matters more during Baby.

---

# 84. Executive-First Anti-Pattern

Starting with a C-level buyer merely because they control larger budgets can increase friction unnecessarily.

Entry should use the lowest-authority actor capable of legitimate adoption when this strengthens distribution.

---

# 85. Developer-Only Anti-Pattern

The opposite failure is assuming developer love automatically creates enterprise economics.

The Buyer Bridge must be explicit.

---

# 86. One-Buyer-Fits-All Anti-Pattern

A single message for developer, operations, compliance, and executive usually produces weak positioning.

The architecture may be shared.

The buyer outcome differs.

---

# 87. Architecture-First Anti-Pattern

Explaining Reality Sync, constitutional primitives, or the Trust stack before the buyer understands the job is discouraged.

---

# 88. Regulation-Equals-Buyer Anti-Pattern

Regulation creates urgency.

It does not identify the payer automatically.

---

# 89. Pain-Equals-WTP Anti-Pattern

Pain can exist without budget, authority, or willingness to change.

Payment remains evidence-bound.

---

# 90. Segment Proliferation Anti-Pattern

The Marketing branch discourages creating dozens of theoretical micro-segments before E3 evidence.

During Baby:

> **Fewer, clearer segment hypotheses are preferred.**

---

# 91. Minimum Viable Segmentation

During Baby, segmentation governance SHALL remain proportionate.

The purpose is to improve the next market decision.

A program SHOULD maintain only the segment distinctions necessary to answer:

- who has the job;
- who can act;
- who can pay;
- why now;
- how reached.

Segmentation SHALL NOT become a research bureaucracy that delays real market contact.

---

# 105. Constitutional Invariants

The following are proposed as ratifiable invariants.

## Invariant 1

Market ≠ Segment ≠ Buyer ≠ User.

## Invariant 2

A buyer exists only where problem ownership, authority, urgency, and access to resources are sufficiently real.

## Invariant 3

Every market expression SHALL lead with a specific buyer Job-To-Be-Done.

## Invariant 4

The universal North Star SHALL not replace entry-stage buyer specificity.

## Invariant 5

User, technical buyer, economic buyer, approver, blocker, champion, and beneficiary SHALL remain distinguishable.

## Invariant 6

Capability progression SHALL not be mistaken for a linear buyer journey.

## Invariant 7

Where buyers change across depth, a Buyer Bridge SHALL be identified rather than assumed.

## Invariant 8

Pain does not automatically imply budget.

## Invariant 9

Budget does not automatically imply urgency.

## Invariant 10

Regulatory or standards pressure does not automatically identify the payer.

## Invariant 11

Company size and domain labels alone are insufficient segmentation.

## Invariant 12

A segment should be coherent in Job, buying process, urgency, and alternative.

## Invariant 13

A beachhead segment is a strategic focus, not constitutional identity.

## Invariant 14

The entry buyer may differ from the expansion buyer.

## Invariant 15

Developer-led adoption SHALL not be assumed to produce enterprise purchase without an evidenced Buyer Bridge.

## Invariant 16

Buyer-facing language may simplify architecture but SHALL preserve truth.

## Invariant 17

Buyer hypotheses remain evidence-bound under `Marketing-001`.

## Invariant 18

Future domains SHALL identify their own buyers and jobs rather than inherit Commerce assumptions.

# 109. Segment Kill Conditions

A segment SHOULD be reworked, demoted, or rejected when:

- pain is weak;
- urgency is absent;
- buyer cannot be reached;
- no budget path exists;
- procurement is disproportionate;
- adoption requires excessive customization;
- distribution is uneconomic;
- Buyer Bridge fails;
- Zyppi capability fit is weak;
- strategic carryover is low.

# 112. Closing Principle

Zyppi's architecture may eventually serve many domains, organizations, and autonomous systems.

No individual buyer needs to understand all of that.

The buyer needs to understand:

```text
my problem
my trigger
my current alternative
my outcome
my risk
my cost
my decision
```

Zyppi must understand everything else.

The Marketing branch therefore adopts:

> **Build universally. Segment deliberately. Enter through one clear Job. Sell to the actor who can act. Bridge value to the actor who can buy deeper capability. Never mistake architectural breadth for buyer relevance.**

And:

> **The market does not buy Zyppi's future. A buyer buys progress in a specific situation today. Zyppi earns the right to reveal more of the platform after that progress becomes real.**
