# 0. Constitutional Purpose

Zyppi requires a permanent constitutional branch governing how the platform encounters the market.

The North Star defines **why Zyppi exists**.

The Foundations define **how Zyppi must behave**.

`What is Zyppi?` explains **what Zyppi is and how its first product expression connects Reality to digital systems**.

`MARKET-REALITY-001` establishes the discipline by which Zyppi may interpret competition, market power, differentiation, moat, adoption, standards, and commercial progress.

`ZEA-001` establishes the economic laws governing participation, monetization, pricing, compounding, adoption, and long-term value creation.

The `Marketing-*` branch exists to translate those higher authorities into a durable constitutional system governing:

- how Zyppi enters markets;
- how it chooses entry products;
- how it identifies buyers and customer jobs;
- how it positions itself;
- how it competes;
- how it acquires and activates participants;
- how it distributes capabilities;
- how it communicates value;
- how it interfaces with pricing and monetization;
- how it tests commercial hypotheses;
- how it expands from one application or domain into another;
- how it distinguishes present market evidence from future strategic possibility.

This branch is not an advertising manual.

It is the constitutional architecture governing the relationship between **Zyppi and Market Reality**.

# 7. Marketing Constitutional Laws

The following laws govern the entire `Marketing-*` family.

---

## Law M1 — Market Reality Before Internal Preference

Zyppi SHALL prefer evidence about actual markets over assumptions derived from architecture, aspiration, elegance, or internal enthusiasm.

---

## Law M2 — Entry Is Not Identity

No market wedge, application, standard, product, or utility may redefine Zyppi's universal constitutional identity.

---

## Law M3 — Utility Before Explanation

The preferred market entry is a useful thing that can be understood and adopted without first requiring the market to understand the full Zyppi Constitution.

---

## Law M4 — Adoption Friction Must Be Minimized

Early-market products SHALL minimize:

- setup;
- procurement;
- integration;
- human onboarding;
- cognitive complexity;
- support dependency;
- pricing fear.

---

## Law M5 — The Trojan Horse Must Compound

A market-entry product is strategically preferred when every legitimate use leaves behind one or more durable assets such as:

- persistent identity;
- interaction history;
- developer familiarity;
- integration;
- operational context;
- reusable capability;
- distribution surface;
- Trust relationships;
- policy context.

A Trojan Horse that generates no reusable platform value is strategically weak even if it produces revenue.

---

## Law M6 — Do Not Tax the Doorway to Finance the Castle

The foundational acquisition mechanism SHALL NOT be artificially weakened, restricted, or overpriced merely to solve temporary startup cash needs.

Bootstrap financing and long-term market-entry economics may be separate systems.

---

## Law M7 — Product-System Differentiation Precedes Feature Differentiation

Zyppi SHALL NOT evaluate differentiation only by subtracting every feature available across the union of competitors.

The correct competitive unit is:

> **one coherent alternative system versus one coherent Zyppi system.**

Feature overlap may weaken feature-level differentiation.

It does not automatically invalidate system-level differentiation.

---

## Law M8 — Feature Is Not Product System; Product System Is Not Moat

These concepts SHALL remain separate:

```text
Feature
≠
Product System
≠
Moat
```

A coherent system may be commercially differentiated before it becomes defensible.

A moat exists only after adoption, dependence, compounding context, network effects, or other defensibility is actually demonstrated.

---

## Law M9 — Standards Below; Zyppi Above

Zyppi SHALL adopt and support open standards below the proprietary value layer whenever practical.

Standards may be acquisition infrastructure.

They SHALL NOT be misrepresented as Zyppi's core moat.

---

## Law M10 — Distribution Is Architecture

APIs, SDKs, MCP, integrations, domains, partners, packaging channels, developer tooling, and embedded workflows may serve as distribution infrastructure even when they are not themselves differentiated features.

---

## Law M11 — Do Not Confuse Usage With Value

Traffic, scans, identities, events, requests, clicks, and API calls are not automatically economic value.

Monetization SHALL follow increasing customer leverage and economic consequence.

---

## Law M12 — Bootstrap Revenue Must Not Corrupt Permanent Economics

Temporary founder-stage cash constraints SHALL NOT justify permanent pricing anti-patterns.

Bootstrap revenue mechanisms may exist separately when they:

- preserve constitutional pricing;
- remain low-friction;
- avoid high human-resource consumption;
- strengthen reusable Zyppi infrastructure.

---

## Law M13 — Human Attention Is Scarce Capital

Especially during Zyppi's early stage, commercial products SHALL be evaluated against the human attention required to:

- sell;
- onboard;
- configure;
- customize;
- support;
- maintain.

Revenue that requires disproportionate human effort may be strategically inferior to lower revenue that scales automatically.

---

## Law M14 — Evidence Grade Must Travel With the Claim

Every meaningful market claim SHALL preserve its evidence grade.

Research, simulation, reasoning, and competitive analysis SHALL NOT be silently upgraded into customer validation.

---

## Law M15 — Simulate Decisions; Never Simulate Evidence

AI reasoning may be used to:

- pressure-test hypotheses;
- compare alternatives;
- model economics;
- simulate buyer decisions;
- identify likely failure modes.

AI simulation SHALL NOT count as evidence that a real buyer behaved, paid, adopted, churned, or valued the product.

# 8. Marketing Evidence Ladder

The Marketing branch adopts the following evidence ladder.

## E0 — Unsupported

Assertion, intuition, preference, or speculation without supporting evidence.

---

## E1 — Logical / Architectural

Supported by coherent reasoning, technical architecture, constitutional logic, or economic modeling.

Useful for hypothesis formation.

Not market validation.

---

## E2 — External Evidence

Supported by credible external sources such as:

- official competitor pricing;
- official product capabilities;
- standards documentation;
- credible market reports;
- documented public deployments;
- published customer cases.

---

## E2+ — Triangulated External Evidence

Supported by multiple independent or materially different external sources.

Stronger than E2.

Still not Zyppi customer validation.

---

## E3 — Zyppi Pilot / User Evidence

Observed behavior from actual Zyppi users, pilots, design partners, customers, or market experiments.

Examples:

- activation;
- usage;
- payment;
- retention;
- conversion;
- qualitative buyer feedback tied to an actual Zyppi proposition.

---

## E4 — Repeated Zyppi Evidence

Repeated and sufficiently consistent evidence across multiple real Zyppi users, customers, segments, or market cycles.

E4 supports stronger commercial doctrine.

# 14. Amendment Discipline

A `Marketing-NNN` document may be amended when:

- new E3/E4 evidence contradicts an earlier hypothesis;
- competitor behavior materially changes;
- regulation changes;
- Zyppi's product capability changes;
- a market assumption is falsified;
- a higher constitutional document changes.

Amendment SHALL preserve:

- historical decision trace;
- superseded version;
- reason for change;
- evidence that justified the change.

Marketing doctrine should evolve through **evidence**, not fashion.

# 15. Marketing Anti-Patterns

The following are constitutionally discouraged or prohibited.

## Anti-Pattern 1 — Architecture as Market Proof

Assuming Zyppi will win because its architecture is elegant.

---

## Anti-Pattern 2 — Future Moat as Present Claim

Presenting a possible future compounding advantage as an existing moat.

---

## Anti-Pattern 3 — Feature Union Subtraction

Comparing Zyppi against the union of every competitor feature and concluding that every overlapping capability is strategically worthless.

---

## Anti-Pattern 4 — Random Feature Expansion

Building adjacent applications merely because they can generate revenue.

---

## Anti-Pattern 5 — Revenue Panic

Damaging free participation or long-term economics to manufacture short-term ARR.

---

## Anti-Pattern 6 — Enterprise Pain Forced Into Starter Pricing

Attempting to compress a high-integration enterprise problem into a cheap self-service product merely to create a pricing ladder.

---

## Anti-Pattern 7 — Vanity Adoption

Optimizing identities, scans, registrations, users, or traffic without measuring progression into real value.

---

## Anti-Pattern 8 — Simulated Validation

Treating AI-generated buyer responses, Council reasoning, or desk research as evidence of real willingness to pay.

---

## Anti-Pattern 9 — Market Category Imprisonment

Allowing a successful first application to permanently redefine Zyppi's category.

---

## Anti-Pattern 10 — Human-Heavy Bootstrap Products

Using scarce founder/engineering time to operate low-margin services that cannot become reusable infrastructure.
