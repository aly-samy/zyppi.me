# 0. Purpose

Zyppi can collect enormous amounts of telemetry.

That does not mean Zyppi understands its market.

A startup can report:

- traffic;
- accounts;
- API calls;
- identities;
- scans;
- transactions;
- revenue;

and still misunderstand whether users are:

- activated;
- receiving value;
- retaining;
- expanding;
- becoming dependent;
- creating distribution;
- strengthening the platform.

`Marketing-012` establishes the constitutional doctrine governing how Zyppi measures market health.

It governs:

- metric selection;
- metric hierarchy;
- participant-state measurement;
- activation metrics;
- retention;
- expansion;
- distribution;
- economics;
- ECU-linked measurement;
- bootstrap metrics;
- compounding;
- governed execution;
- cohort analysis;
- numerator/denominator integrity;
- vanity metrics;
- metric gaming;
- metric review;
- dashboard design;
- North Star metric discipline.

The central rule is:

> **A metric is useful only when it represents a real state, behavior, value, constraint, or strategic transition that can improve a decision.**

---

# 1. Governing Principle — Measure States, Not Activity Alone

The Marketing branch adopts:

> **Measure what state the participant reached, not merely what event happened.**

Examples:

```text
Account created
≠
Adopted

API call
≠
Activated

Scan
≠
Value

Payment
≠
Retention

Traffic
≠
Distribution

Usage growth
≠
Moat
```

Events may help infer state.

They SHALL NOT replace explicit state definitions.

---

# 2. Metric Definition

A **Metric** is a defined quantitative or categorical measure used to observe a market, product, participant, economic, distribution, or strategic condition.

A metric SHOULD have:

- name;
- definition;
- unit;
- numerator;
- denominator where relevant;
- population;
- time window;
- data source;
- interpretation;
- known limits.

A number without a definition is not a reliable metric.

---

# 3. Metric Is Not Evidence Grade

A precise number may still have weak evidence.

For example:

```text
1,000 simulated users
```

is not E3.

Metric precision SHALL NOT upgrade evidence grade.

Evidence remains governed by `Marketing-001`.

---

# 4. Metric Is Not Strategy

A metric may indicate what happened.

It does not automatically explain:

- why;
- whether it matters;
- what Zyppi should do.

Metrics support decisions.

They do not replace strategic judgment.

---

# 5. Constitutional Law — One Metric Cannot Represent the Whole Company

The Marketing branch rejects the idea that one number can fully define Zyppi health during Baby, Grow, Compete, or Dominate.

A single North Star metric MAY be used for focus.

It SHALL be accompanied by guardrails and stage-specific context.

---

# 6. Constitutional Law — Metric Must Match Strategic Stage

The most important metrics change as Zyppi matures.

Conceptually:

## Baby
- activation;
- qualified adoption;
- retention;
- time to value;
- support burden;
- early payment;
- channel viability.

## Grow
- retained cohorts;
- expansion;
- recurring ECU;
- repeatable acquisition;
- margins.

## Compete
- product-system depth;
- switching behavior;
- enterprise dependency;
- ecosystem participation.

## Dominate
- governed executions;
- cross-domain participation;
- network utility;
- institutional dependence.

A Dominate-stage metric SHALL not distract Baby-stage execution.

---

# 7. Metric Hierarchy

The Marketing branch recognizes five classes.

## M0 — Activity Metric
What happened?

## M1 — State Metric
What participant state was reached?

## M2 — Value Metric
Did meaningful value occur?

## M3 — Economic / Compounding Metric
Did value deepen or create durable economics?

## M4 — Strategic Power Metric
Did dependence, ecosystem, or market power emerge?

Higher classes generally carry more strategic meaning.

Lower classes remain useful operationally.

---

- identities created.

Activity metrics are often important for:

- capacity;

# 9. State Metrics

State metrics are stronger because they map to participant progression.

# 10. Value Metrics

Value metrics attempt to measure the outcome experienced by the participant.

Examples may include:

- successful contextual decisions;
- reduced manual work;
- reduced failure;
- operational continuity;
- verified outcome delivered;
- workflow completed.

Value metrics must remain scoped to actual observable behavior.

Later-stage metrics may include:

- partner dependency;
- third-party integrations;
- ecosystem reuse;
- cross-domain participation;
- network utility.

These SHALL not be claimed prematurely.

# 12A. Market Availability Is Different From Participant Activation

Zyppi markets may contain a large population of qualified future buyers who are not actively purchasing today.

The Marketing branch distinguishes:

```text
IN-MARKET DEMAND
buyers currently entering a decision

        from

OUT-OF-MARKET FUTURE DEMAND
qualified buyers whose relevant buying situation has not yet activated
```

Marketing health SHALL NOT be judged solely through immediate conversion of the currently in-market population.

For B2B categories with infrequent buying cycles, plans SHOULD consider both:

- **Demand Capture** — becoming easy to find and buy when the buyer is in-market;
- **Demand Creation / Mental Availability** — becoming easy to remember when the relevant buying situation eventually occurs.

The commonly cited B2B "95:5" formulation MAY be used as a planning heuristic where relevant.

The exact ratio SHALL NOT be constitutionalized or assumed universal.

The actual in-market/out-of-market balance depends on category and buying cycle.

---

# 12B. Category Entry Points

A **Category Entry Point (CEP)** is a recurring buying situation or memory cue that can cause a category buyer to retrieve possible solutions from memory.

Programs MAY use CEPs to understand why a buyer enters the market, when Zyppi should come to mind, and which buying situations deserve communication investment.

CEPs SHALL be validated for the relevant category rather than copied mechanically from another market.

---

# 12C. Mental Availability Metrics

Where long-cycle B2B demand is strategically material, programs MAY measure Mental Availability using metrics such as:

- **Mental Penetration** — the share of relevant category buyers who link Zyppi with at least one relevant CEP;
- **Network Size** — the breadth of relevant CEP associations among buyers who know Zyppi;
- **Mental Market Share** — Zyppi's share of relevant brand–CEP associations within the measured category.

Other indicators such as branded search, share of search, direct traffic, and aided or unaided awareness MAY be useful diagnostics.

They SHALL NOT be treated as exact substitutes for Mental Availability without evidence.

Mental Availability is a **leading market-memory metric**.

It does not substitute for E3 product adoption, payment, retention, or market power.

> **Future demand may be cultivated before it can be activated, but brand memory is not the same thing as product validation.**

# 13. Participant-State Model

A Market Program SHOULD define participant states appropriate to the product.

A domain-neutral model may be:

```text
Discovered
→ Participating
→ Adopted
→ Activated
→ Repeating
→ Retained
→ Economically Engaged
→ Expanded
→ Compounding
```

The program may simplify or modify this model.

The state definitions must remain explicit.

---

# 13A. Incubation State

A program MAY define an **Incubating** participant state when:

- a qualified participant has made a real commitment, adoption decision, implementation start, or equivalent credible step;
- final meaningful activation is blocked primarily by a documented external dependency.

External dependencies may include physical production or packaging cycles, procurement, regulatory approval, scheduled migration, retailer/system readiness, and physical installation.

Incubating SHALL remain distinct from merely aware, unqualified lead, activated, and churned.

An Incubating participant SHALL not be counted as activated until the activation event actually occurs.

It SHALL not be counted as churned merely because the external dependency takes time.

The state exists to measure Reality accurately, not to improve conversion statistics.

# 14. State Transition Metric

A **State Transition Metric** measures movement from one state to another.

Examples:

```text
Participating → Activated

Activated → Retained

Retained → Paid

Paid → Expanded
```

State-transition metrics are generally more actionable than raw totals.

---

# 15. Funnel Is Not Always Linear

The Marketing branch recognizes that buyer and capability progression may be non-linear.

Therefore:

> **Funnels are useful models, not universal truths.**

A participant may:

- skip;
- return;
- branch;
- deepen through another buyer.

Metrics SHOULD preserve this reality where relevant.

---

# 16. Activation Metric

Every material product or market program SHOULD define one primary behavioral activation event.

Activation SHALL correspond to first meaningful value.

Examples may include:

- first successful production resolution;
- first context-dependent decision;
- first useful webhook replay;
- first live state retrieval;
- first verified outcome.

The exact event remains program-level.

---

# 17. Activation Rate

Activation Rate SHOULD specify:

```text
Activated qualified participants
÷
Eligible adopted participants
```

The population and window must be explicit.

---

# 18. Time to First Value

**Time to First Value (TTFV)** measures the elapsed time between a meaningful entry point and activation.

Examples of starting points:

- signup;
- API key creation;
- first installation;
- first configuration.

The start event SHALL be defined.

---

# 18A. Controlled Time to Value vs External Incubation Time

Where external dependencies materially affect elapsed activation time, programs SHOULD separate:

```text
Zyppi-Controlled TTFV
```

from:

```text
External Incubation Time
```

and, where useful:

```text
Total Elapsed Time to Activation
```

This prevents a slow physical or institutional cycle from being misdiagnosed as poor software activation while preserving the real total buyer experience.

# 19. Activation Quality

An activation may be weak or strong.

Programs MAY distinguish:

- test activation;
- production activation;
- repeated production activation.

A sandbox success is not necessarily operational adoption.

---

# 20. Qualified Adoption

A **Qualified Adoption** is adoption by a participant who matches the target segment and actually places Zyppi into legitimate use.

Raw signup counts SHALL remain separate.

---

# 21. Adoption Rate

Adoption Rate SHOULD identify:

- eligible discovered participants;
- actual adopters;
- time period.

The program SHALL avoid denominator manipulation.

---

# 22. Denominator Integrity

Metrics SHALL not silently change denominators to improve appearance.

Examples prohibited:

- excluding failed users after the fact;
- redefining active accounts only around successful cohorts;
- changing eligible population without disclosure.

---

# 23. Numerator Integrity

The numerator SHALL represent the exact behavior claimed.

Example:

```text
Paying customer
```

shall not include:

- unpaid pilot;
- internal test;
- promotional zero-dollar account;

unless explicitly classified.

---

# 24. Retention Definition

Retention SHALL measure continued meaningful value over time.

Possible units:

- active organization;
- active developer;
- active workflow;
- recurring ECU;
- recurring paid usage.

"Account exists" is not retention.

---

# 25. Retention Window

Retention windows SHOULD match the natural frequency of the Job.

A daily tool and annual compliance workflow should not use the same retention window.

---

# 26. Cohort Retention

Retention SHOULD be analyzed by cohort where practical.

Examples:

- signup month;
- activation month;
- first payment;
- channel;
- segment.

Cohorts reveal behavior hidden by aggregate growth.

---

# 27. Logo Retention

**Logo Retention** measures whether organizations remain active or paying.

It is useful.

It does not show whether depth increased.

---

# 28. Usage Retention

Usage Retention measures whether meaningful activity persists.

This may be more relevant for free infrastructure than revenue retention.

---

# 29. Revenue Retention

Revenue retention may include:

- Gross Revenue Retention;
- Net Revenue Retention.

Definitions SHALL remain explicit.

---

# 30. ECU Retention

Where ECU is meaningful, Zyppi MAY measure retained ECU consumption by cohort.

This can reveal whether platform economic usage persists independently of commercial packaging.

---

# 31. Expansion Definition

Expansion occurs when a retained participant increases:

- usage;
- capability depth;
- organizational breadth;
- paid consumption;
- domains;
- workflows.

Expansion SHALL specify which dimension increased.

---

# 32. Expansion Rate

Programs MAY measure:

```text
retained participants with meaningful expansion
÷
eligible retained participants
```

The expansion criterion must be defined.

---

# 33. Buyer Bridge Metric

Where a Buyer Bridge is strategically important, a program SHOULD measure its actual passage.

Possible metrics:

- Bridge Artifact generated;
- Bridge Artifact viewed;
- deeper buyer engaged;
- enterprise evaluation started;
- deeper capability purchased.

This prevents assuming organizational expansion.

---

# 34. Bridge Conversion

A possible metric is:

```text
Entry organizations reaching deeper buyer
÷
eligible activated organizations
```

Exact eligibility remains program-level.

---

# 34A. Mental Availability and Physical Availability

Long-term market growth may require both:

- **Mental Availability** — being remembered in relevant buying situations;
- **Physical / Commercial Availability** — being sufficiently easy to find, evaluate, procure, and adopt.

`Marketing-009` governs distribution architecture.

`Marketing-012` SHALL permit metrics for both future demand formation and present demand capture.

A program focused only on immediate activation may under-measure long-cycle market development.

A program focused only on awareness may fail to prove useful distribution.

Both sides SHOULD remain visible where strategically relevant.

Each stage provides different information.

# 37. Channel Activation Rate

# 38. Channel Retention

# 43. Referral Coefficient

# 65. Compounding Metrics

The Marketing branch recognizes three compounding families.

## Horizontal
More participants / integrations / objects.

## Vertical
Deeper capability use.

## Temporal
Increasing value from history and continuity.

These SHALL remain distinct.

---

# 66. Horizontal Compounding Metrics

Possible metrics:

- active organizations;
- active developers;
- active integrations;
- partner distribution nodes;
- active identities.

The relevant measure is active, not merely registered.

---

# 67. Vertical Compounding Metrics

Possible measures:

- percentage using policy;
- percentage using workflow;
- percentage using verification;
- percentage using Trust;
- percentage using governed execution.

This measures depth.

---

# 68. Temporal Compounding Metrics

Possible measures:

- retained operational history;
- repeated decision history;
- returning workflows;
- history consulted;
- continuity duration.

History quantity alone does not prove value.

---

# 69. Compounding Health

Horizontal Health
Vertical Health
Temporal Health

---

# 70. Governed Execution Metric

As Zyppi matures, **Governed Executions** may become an important platform health metric.

A Governed Execution SHOULD mean an actual consequential capability invocation that passed applicable constitutional controls and produced appropriate Proof / Receipt.

The exact runtime definition belongs to the execution architecture.

---

# 71. Governed Executions Are Not a Baby Vanity Goal

The presence of a future high-value metric SHALL not pressure Baby products to manufacture meaningless executions.

The metric becomes relevant only when the market genuinely activates such capability.

---

# 72. Trust Metrics

Possible future metrics may include:

- verified Evidence evaluations;
- Standing evaluations;
- trusted decisions;
- governed capabilities admitted.

These SHALL use actual constitutional semantics.

Marketing SHALL not invent "trust score" vanity metrics.

---

# 73. Receipt Metrics

Receipt count MAY measure governed activity.

Useful context may include:

- receipts consumed by customer;
- audits using receipts;
- repeat execution.

---

# 74. Reliability Metrics

Infrastructure health may influence market health.

Relevant measures may include:

- uptime;
- latency;
- error rate;
- fallback success;
- delivery success.

These are product/operational metrics with direct marketing consequences.

---

# 75. Support Metrics

Support burden SHOULD be measured where it materially affects economics.

Possible metrics:

- tickets per active account;
- support minutes per customer;
- implementation hours;
- incident frequency.

---

# 76. Onboarding Burden Metric

Programs MAY measure:

- time to configure;
- human touches;
- implementation steps.

---

# 77. Human Attention Metric

Possible reporting:

- founder hours by initiative;
- support hours by product;
- sales hours by segment.

This may reveal hidden strategic cost.

---

# 78. Time-to-Decision Metrics

Where enterprise selling exists, programs MAY track:

- discovery to trial;
- trial to approval;
- approval to contract;
- contract to activation.

Long cycles may influence segment priority.

---

# 79. Buyer Role Metrics

Where buying-center complexity matters, programs MAY track:

- champion identified;
- economic buyer engaged;
- approver completed;
- blocker resolved.

These metrics are useful for enterprise motion.

---

# 80. Market Claim Metrics

Claims should not be assigned arbitrary confidence percentages merely for presentation.

Where confidence scoring exists, it must map to the evidence system.

---

# 81. Metric Evidence Grade

A metric may have:

- high measurement reliability;
- low strategic evidence grade.

Example:

```text
100,000 requests
```

can be measured precisely.

It may still provide weak evidence of willingness to pay.

Programs SHOULD keep measurement reliability and strategic evidence separate.

---

# 82. Metric Source of Truth

Every critical metric SHOULD have one defined source of truth.

Examples:

- production telemetry;
- billing;
- CRM;
- experiment log.

Conflicting sources SHALL be reconciled rather than silently selected.

---

# 83. Metric Versioning

When a metric definition changes materially, the definition SHOULD be versioned.

Historical comparisons SHALL disclose incompatible definitions.

---

# 84. Metric Drift

**Metric Drift** occurs when a metric's practical meaning changes while the name remains constant.

Examples:

- "active customer" becomes easier to qualify;
- "activation" changes from production use to signup.

Metric Drift is prohibited without explicit definition change.

---

# 85. Metric Gaming

The Marketing branch recognizes Goodhart's risk:

> **When a measure becomes the target, it can stop being a good measure.**

Programs SHALL monitor whether teams optimize the metric while harming the underlying value.

---

# 86. Guardrail Metrics

Important primary metrics SHOULD have guardrails where manipulation or false success is plausible.

Examples:

```text
Primary:
Activation rate

Guardrail:
Support burden
```

or:

```text
Primary:
Revenue

Guardrail:
Retention
```

---

# 87. Counter-Metrics

A counter-metric explicitly observes a likely negative consequence.

Examples:

- higher conversion vs higher churn;
- more free use vs higher COGS;
- more utility revenue vs more engineering drag.

Counter-metrics improve decision quality.

---

# 88. Vanity Metric Definition

A **Vanity Metric** is not inherently a bad number.

It becomes vanity when presented as strategic success without a valid causal or state relationship.

Examples may include:

- total signups;
- total scans;
- total API calls;
- page views;
- social followers.

---

# 89. Vanity Metric Rule

Vanity metrics MAY be reported operationally.

They SHALL NOT lead executive or investor conclusions without appropriate context.

---

# 90. Cumulative Metric Risk

Cumulative totals almost always increase.

Therefore:

```text
total identities ever created
```

can hide zero current adoption.

Programs SHOULD prefer active and cohort metrics for current health.

---

# 91. Average Metric Risk

Averages can hide distribution.

Programs SHOULD use:

- median;
- percentile;
- cohort;
- segment;

where meaningful.

---

# 92. Ratio Integrity

Every ratio SHALL make clear:

- numerator;
- denominator;
- period;
- exclusions.

---

# 93. Growth Rate Integrity

Growth rates SHALL identify:

- baseline;
- absolute values;
- period.

A 500% increase from 1 to 6 SHALL not be presented without context.

---

# 94. Seasonality

Where market behavior is seasonal, programs SHOULD avoid interpreting ordinary seasonality as strategic change.

---

# 95. Geography

Metrics SHOULD preserve geography where behavior or economics differ materially.

One geography SHALL not silently represent the world.

---

# 96. Segment Metrics

Programs SHOULD compare segments separately when:

- activation;
- retention;
- WTP;
- support burden;

differ materially.

---

# 97. Buyer Metrics

Developer activation SHALL not be merged with enterprise buyer conversion as if they represent the same stage.

This preserves Buyer Bridge integrity.

# 130. Closing Principle

Numbers are powerful because they appear objective.

That appearance makes bad metrics especially dangerous.

A precise count of the wrong behavior can create more confidence than an honest admission of uncertainty.

Zyppi therefore SHALL measure what matters to the decision, define the state behind the number, preserve the denominator, and refuse to call activity value merely because it is easy to count.

The Marketing branch adopts:

> **Measure the transition, not the vanity event. Measure retained value, not account existence. Measure qualified distribution, not traffic. Measure economic depth, not countability. Measure compounding only where something genuinely becomes stronger through use.**

And:

> **The purpose of a metric is not to make Zyppi look successful. The purpose of a metric is to make Zyppi harder to fool.**
