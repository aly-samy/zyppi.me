# 0. Purpose

Zyppi requires both permanence and change.

The Constitution must remain stable enough to govern.

Strategy must remain flexible enough to respond to Reality.

A one-year plan may focus almost entirely on Commerce and GS1.

A three-year plan may expand deeper into Commerce and possibly into another domain.

A five-year plan may contemplate a multi-domain Reality Sync platform.

None of those plans should redefine what Zyppi constitutionally is.

Likewise, the Constitution should not force a plan to preserve obsolete tactics merely because they were once important.

`Marketing-015` establishes the constitutional doctrine governing how Zyppi plans across time.

It governs:

- 1-year strategic plans;
- 3-year strategic plans;
- 5-year strategic plans;
- horizon-specific claims;
- scenario discipline;
- forecast discipline;
- assumption management;
- evidence re-rating;
- plan amendment;
- strategic continuity;
- strategic discontinuity;
- milestone governance;
- plan supersession;
- domain expansion timing;
- interaction between North Star and current execution.

The central rule is:

> **The Constitution defines what must remain true. Strategic plans define what Zyppi currently intends to do under present evidence. Plans may change quickly; the Constitution should change only when Reality proves that a governing principle itself is wrong or incomplete.**

---

# 1. Governing Hierarchy

The Marketing branch adopts the following hierarchy:

```text
NORTH STAR
Purpose / destination

        ↓

CONSTITUTION
Durable governing law

        ↓

STRATEGIC HORIZONS
1Y / 3Y / 5Y plans

        ↓

MARKET PROGRAMS
Specific market execution

        ↓

EXPERIMENTS
Real-world tests

        ↓

EVIDENCE
Reality that may re-rate everything above the experiment
```

No lower layer may silently redefine a higher layer.

---

# 2. Constitution vs Plan

The Constitution answers:

> **What must remain true across markets and time?**

A Strategic Plan answers:

> **Given what we currently know, what should Zyppi prioritize during this horizon?**

The distinction SHALL remain explicit.

---

# 3. Plan Is Not Ratified Reality

A plan may contain:

- priorities;
- targets;
- forecasts;
- scenarios;
- hypotheses;
- market assumptions.

Ratifying or approving a plan does not convert its assumptions into E3/E4 evidence.

---

# 4. Strategic Horizon Definition

A **Strategic Horizon** is a time-bounded planning frame used to organize:

- priorities;
- resource allocation;
- evidence objectives;
- market progression;
- capability progression.

The horizon is a planning instrument.

It is not a promise that Reality will conform to the calendar.

---

# 5. Horizon Families

The Marketing Constitution establishes three primary planning horizons:

```text
1 YEAR
Establish / Survive / Validate

3 YEAR
Grow / Deepen / Expand

5 YEAR
Compete Broadly / Multi-Domain / Platform Scale
```

These are strategic roles, not fixed product commitments.

---

# 6. 1-Year Horizon — Establish & Survive

The 1-year horizon SHOULD focus on:

- establishing market;
- entry wedge;
- buyer;
- activation;
- distribution;
- early retention;
- market evidence;
- survival economics;
- bootstrap cash where needed;
- core reusable infrastructure.

The 1-year plan SHOULD be the most specific and evidence-sensitive.

---

# 7. 1-Year Plan Does Not Define Zyppi

A narrow one-year focus is constitutionally desirable when it increases execution quality.

It SHALL not redefine:

- North Star;
- long-term domain scope;
- universal capability model.

---

# 8. 3-Year Horizon — Grow & Expand

The 3-year horizon MAY include:

- deeper current-domain capability;
- repeatable distribution;
- stronger economic engines;
- developer ecosystem;
- enterprise workflows;
- Trust / governance layers;
- one evidenced second-domain entry.

These are strategic possibilities.

They are not guaranteed milestones.

---

# 9. 5-Year Horizon — Compete Broadly

The 5-year horizon MAY include:

- multi-domain platform behavior;
- institutional adoption;
- cross-domain execution;
- ecosystem effects;
- network utility;
- global expansion;
- deeper infrastructure status.

Such statements SHALL be framed as strategic direction or scenario unless evidence supports stronger language.

---

# 10. Beyond 5 Years

Beyond five years, the Marketing branch SHOULD generally rely on:

- North Star;
- constitutional direction;
- scenario logic.

Detailed product roadmaps beyond that horizon are presumptively weak.

---

# 11. Constitutional Law — Horizon Precision Declines With Time

The farther the horizon, the lower the justified tactical precision.

Therefore:

```text
1Y
high specificity

3Y
moderate specificity

5Y
strategic direction

Beyond
constitutional ambition
```

This prevents false planning certainty.

---

# 12. Constitutional Law — Calendar Does Not Override Evidence

If evidence invalidates a milestone:

- delay;
- rework;
- reject;
- accelerate;

as Reality requires.

A date SHALL NOT keep a failed strategy alive.

---

# 13. Constitutional Law — Plans Must Carry Evidence Grades

Material assumptions SHOULD identify whether they are:

- evidenced;
- inferred;
- hypothesized;
- aspirational.

This prevents strategic prose from hiding uncertainty.

---

# 14. Strategic Assumption Definition

A **Strategic Assumption** is a belief upon which material plan logic depends.

Examples:

- target segment will activate;
- free GS1 access will accelerate adoption;
- Buyer Bridge will support enterprise expansion;
- utility MRR can reduce runway pressure.

Critical assumptions SHOULD be visible.

---

# 15. Assumption Register

A plan SHOULD maintain a lightweight assumption register:

```text
Assumption
Evidence Grade
Decision Impact
Current Status
Next Test
Review Trigger
```

The register SHALL not become bureaucracy.

---

# 16. Critical Assumption

An assumption is **Critical** when its failure would materially alter:

- market entry;
- pricing;
- distribution;
- funding;
- domain expansion.

Critical assumptions deserve explicit testing.

---

# 17. Scenario Definition

A **Scenario** is a coherent possible future state used for planning.

A scenario is not a forecast.

Examples:

- conservative;
- base;
- upside;
- stress.

Scenarios SHALL be labeled as scenarios.

---

# 18. Forecast Definition

A **Forecast** is an evidence-based estimate of likely future performance.

Forecasts SHOULD become more credible as:

- E3/E4 evidence grows;
- cohorts stabilize;
- distribution repeats;
- economics become measurable.

---

# 19. Scenario ≠ Forecast

The Marketing branch prohibits:

```text
interesting upside scenario
→ forecast
```

without evidence.

This inherits `MARKET-REALITY-001`.

---

# 20. Aspirational Outcome

An aspirational outcome expresses ambition.

Example:

> "Zyppi could become the trust layer of the global economy."

This is constitutionally valid as ambition.

It SHALL not be reported as a five-year forecast without evidence.

---

# 21. Horizon Claims Discipline

The language SHOULD become more conditional as the horizon extends.

Examples:

## 1Y
"We intend to establish..."

## 3Y
"If evidence supports the current thesis, Zyppi may expand..."

## 5Y
"The strategic direction is toward..."

This protects credibility.

---

# 22. Plan State Model

A strategic item MAY be classified:

- ACTIVE;
- VALIDATING;
- CONDITIONAL;
- DEFERRED;
- REWORK;
- REJECTED;
- COMPLETED;
- SUPERSEDED.

This helps distinguish intent from commitment.

---

# 23. Plan Priority Classes

Plans MAY classify priorities:

## P0 — Existential
Required for survival or fundamental validation.

## P1 — Strategic
Material to current horizon.

## P2 — Expansion
Important after P0/P1 evidence.

## P3 — Option
Preserve capability or monitor.

P2/P3 SHALL not starve P0 during Baby.

---

# 24. Baby Resource Rule

During Baby, resource allocation SHOULD strongly favor:

- market evidence;
- reliable core infrastructure;
- low-friction activation;
- survival.

Long-range possibilities SHALL not consume disproportionate current capacity.

---

# 25. Optionality Preservation

A plan MAY preserve future optionality through:

- canonical architecture;
- open standards;
- reusable APIs;
- domain-neutral primitives.

Optionality SHALL not require building future products now.

---

# 26. Build for Option, Not for Unvalidated Product

The Marketing branch adopts:

> **Preserve the ability to expand without prematurely implementing the expansion.**

This is the preferred balance between universality and focus.

---

# 27. Capability Readiness vs Market Readiness

A capability may be architecturally ready before the market is ready.

A market may be urgent before the full platform is ready.

Plans SHALL distinguish:

```text
Capability Readiness
```

from:

```text
Market Readiness
```

---

# 28. Capability Readiness

Capability readiness may include:

- implementation;
- reliability;
- governance;
- API availability.

It does not prove demand.

---

# 29. Market Readiness

Market readiness may include:

- buyer urgency;
- standards transition;
- distribution access;
- WTP.

It does not prove Zyppi can yet serve safely.

---

# 30. Strategic Gate — Both Sides

Material scaling SHOULD generally require sufficient:

```text
Capability Readiness
+
Market Readiness
```

The weaker side becomes the constraint.

---

# 31. Milestone Definition

A **Milestone** is a meaningful strategic state transition.

Good milestones are behavior or capability based.

Weak:

> "Launch in Q2."

Stronger:

> "Achieve repeatable production activation in the target segment."

Dates may still be attached.

---

# 32. Milestone Types

Plans MAY use:

- Market Milestones;
- Product Milestones;
- Economic Milestones;
- Evidence Milestones;
- Distribution Milestones;
- Domain Expansion Milestones.

---

# 33. Evidence Milestone

An Evidence Milestone specifies a desired evidence transition.

Example:

```text
E2 buyer thesis
→ E3 paid adoption evidence
```

This is often more useful than feature-count milestones.

---

# 34. Milestone Gate

A milestone SHOULD define:

- evidence required;
- outcome if passed;
- outcome if failed.

This connects plans to `Marketing-011`.

---

# 35. No Calendar Theater

The Marketing branch rejects milestones that are considered successful solely because a date arrived and work was shipped.

Market milestones require market behavior.

---

# 39. 5-Year Metrics

# 40. Horizon Metric Non-Transfer

A 5-year metric SHALL not become a Baby metric merely because it sounds strategically important.

This prevents premature optimization.

---

# 41. Strategic Re-Rating

A **Strategic Re-Rating** occurs when new evidence changes the confidence, role, timing, or priority of a plan element.

Re-rating may:

- promote;
- demote;
- defer;
- rework;
- reject.

---

# 42. Re-Rating Triggers

Possible triggers:

- E3/E4 evidence;
- competitor change;
- regulation;
- infrastructure cost;
- funding;
- domain evidence;
- failed experiment.

---

# 43. Re-Rating Does Not Require Constitutional Amendment

Most strategic changes SHOULD occur through plan amendment.

Constitutional amendment is reserved for durable governing principle changes.

---

# 44. Constitutional Amendment Trigger

A constitutional review may be justified when repeated evidence shows:

- a governing law is systematically wrong;
- a distinction is incomplete;
- a durable new principle is required.

One tactical failure is usually insufficient.

---

# 45. Plan Amendment

Plans SHOULD be easy to amend.

A plan amendment SHOULD preserve:

- previous assumption;
- new evidence;
- new decision.

This creates strategic memory.

---

# 46. Plan Supersession

A newer strategic plan MAY supersede an older one.

Superseded plans SHOULD remain archived.

They are evidence of what Zyppi previously believed.

---

# 47. No Silent Plan Rewrite

Rewriting history to make prior strategy appear correct is prohibited.

The organization SHOULD preserve:

- what was believed;
- what changed;
- why.

---

# 48. Strategic Memory

Historical plans help reveal:

- recurring mistakes;
- forecast bias;
- evidence quality;
- learning velocity.

They are not failures to hide.

---

# 49. Plan Review Cadence

Plans SHOULD be reviewed often enough to remain useful.

No universal fixed cadence is constitutionalized.

During Baby, more frequent review may be appropriate.

---

# 50. Review by Trigger

Important plans SHOULD also be reviewed when:

- major experiment closes;
- funding changes;
- market regulation changes;
- strategic assumption fails.

---

# 50A. Minimum Viable Planning

The 1-year plan is a living strategic control surface, not a contract written to manufacture certainty.

During Baby, the plan SHOULD contain only enough structure to make visible:

- priorities;
- critical assumptions;
- evidence targets;
- conditional branches;
- resource trade-offs;
- kill conditions.

Uncertainty SHALL be written as uncertainty.

The plan SHALL NOT invent false precision to appear complete before the Council, investors, or operating teams.

When evidence changes, the plan SHOULD change promptly while preserving the previous decision state in strategic memory.

# 51. Rolling Horizon

The 1-year horizon MAY be rolling rather than tied strictly to the calendar year.

The purpose is maintaining current strategic focus.

---

# 52. 3-Year and 5-Year Refresh

Long-range plans SHOULD be refreshed when near-term evidence materially changes the expansion path.

They SHALL not be treated as immutable roadmaps.

---

# 53. Horizon Coherence

The 1-, 3-, and 5-year plans SHOULD tell one coherent story.

Example:

```text
1Y
Establish Commerce

3Y
Deepen Commerce + test second domain

5Y
Multi-domain platform if replication succeeds
```

This is illustrative, not ratified strategy.

---

# 54. Horizon Dependency

Longer-horizon goals SHOULD identify important dependencies on earlier evidence.

Example:

```text
Second domain
depends on
repeatable first-domain activation + reusable capabilities
```

---

# 55. Conditional Expansion

Plans SHOULD use conditional logic where appropriate:

> **If** X evidence is achieved, **then** invest in Y.

This is stronger than pretending every branch will occur.

---

# 56. Branching Strategy

A plan MAY contain multiple branches.

Example:

```text
If Commerce retention strong:
deepen

If activation strong but monetization weak:
preserve free + test deeper layer

If adoption weak:
rework wedge
```

Branching plans reflect Reality better than one linear narrative.

---

# 57. Strategic Kill Condition

Material programs SHOULD define conditions under which they will be:

- stopped;
- demoted;
- replaced.

A plan without kill conditions may preserve weak strategies too long.

---

# 58. Strategic Preserve Condition

A failed revenue thesis may still preserve:

- acquisition utility;
- infrastructure;
- data model;
- distribution.

Plans SHOULD recognize role changes.

---

# 59. Funding Horizon

Funding strategy SHOULD remain separate but connected.

Plans MAY include:

- bootstrap runway;
- seed timing;
- capital needs.

Funding need SHALL not redefine permanent product economics.

---

# 60. Funding as Accelerator

External capital is appropriate when it accelerates:

- validated adoption;
- repeatable distribution;
- reusable platform capability.

Funding SHALL not be treated as validation itself.

---

# 61. Runway Discipline

The 1-year plan SHOULD know:

- cash runway;
- infrastructure burden;
- bootstrap contribution;
- expected funding need.

This is a survival requirement.

---

# 62. Revenue Horizon Separation

Plans SHOULD distinguish:

```text
Bootstrap Revenue
Core Adoption Economics
High-Leverage Revenue
```

This inherits `Marketing-010`.

---

# 63. Commerce Horizon Discipline

The current 1-year plan may be heavily Commerce/GS1-focused.

That focus is constitutionally valid.

The 3-year and 5-year plans SHALL not automatically inherit every current Commerce tactic.

---

# 64. Commerce Success Does Not Freeze the Platform

If Commerce becomes highly successful, Zyppi may rationally remain concentrated for longer.

Success does not require premature diversification.

---

# 65. Commerce Failure Does Not Automatically Trigger Domain Escape

If Commerce struggles, Zyppi SHALL diagnose the failure before jumping to another domain.

This inherits `Marketing-014`.

---

# 66. Domain Expansion Horizon

New-domain work SHOULD be governed by `Marketing-014`.

A horizon plan may reserve:

- research;
- experiment;
- establishment;

without guaranteeing scale.

---

# 67. Second-Domain Timing

No fixed year is constitutionally required for the second domain.

The timing depends on:

- market evidence;
- reusable capability maturity;
- capital;
- opportunity.

---

# 68. Third-Domain Timing

Likewise, the third domain is an evidence event, not a calendar requirement.

# 69. Long-Range Platform Story

The five-year narrative MAY describe:

- domain replication;

---

# 70. Scenario Portfolio

Plans MAY maintain:

- Downside;

- Upside;

scenarios.

---

# 71. Downside Scenario

The downside scenario may model:

- slower adoption;
- weaker monetization;
- delayed funding.

It is useful for survival planning.

---

# 72. Base Scenario

The base scenario SHOULD reflect the most defensible current evidence.

It SHALL not simply be the desired outcome.

---

# 73. Upside Scenario

The upside scenario may explore:

- rapid adoption;
- stronger economics;
- faster domain replication.

It SHALL remain labeled as upside.

---

# 74. Extreme Scenario

Very large outcomes MAY be modeled.

They SHALL remain clearly separate from ordinary forecasts.

---

# 75. Scenario Probability

The Constitution does not require probabilities.

If probabilities are used, they SHOULD not imply statistical precision unsupported by evidence.

---

# 76. Plan Language Discipline

Plan language SHOULD distinguish:

- SHALL / MUST for constitutional obligations;
- SHOULD for strategic preference;
- MAY for options;
- TARGET for current plan;
- HYPOTHESIS for uncertain assumptions.

---

# 77. No Constitutionalization by Repetition

If a plan uses the same tactic for several years, that tactic does not become constitutional automatically.

Promotion requires explicit constitutional review.

---

# 78. No Strategy by Inertia

A tactic SHALL not remain simply because:

> "This is what the plan says."

Evidence retains authority.

---

# 79. No Vision by Calendar

A long-range ambition SHALL not become "due" merely because five years passed.

The North Star is directional.

---

# 80. No Plan Completion Theater

Completing tasks is not the same as achieving strategic outcomes.

Plans SHOULD measure:

- evidence;
- market state;
- capability state.

---

# 81. No Roadmap Inflation

Plans SHALL avoid listing many future features simply to make strategy appear complete.

Capabilities and evidence should lead.

---

# 82. No Feature Commitment From Strategic Scenario

A five-year scenario SHALL not create an implied product promise to customers.

---

# 83. No Domain Commitment From Strategic Scenario

Mentioning a future domain SHALL not authorize product development.

`Marketing-014` governs permission.

---

# 84. No Investor Commitment by Accident

- target;
- thesis;
- scenario.

This inherits `Marketing-013`.

# 85. No Metric Lock-In

Metric continuity is useful.

---

# 86. No Organizational Lock-In

The Constitution governs principles, not permanent org charts.

# 97. Enterprise Commitment Horizon

---

# 98. Strategic Debt

A plan can accumulate **Strategic Debt** when it postpones known unresolved issues.

Examples:

- no buyer clarity;
- no Buyer Bridge;
- no distribution;
- unsustainable subsidy.

Strategic Debt SHOULD be visible.

---

# 99. Experiment Debt

Untested assumptions remain Experiment Debt under `Marketing-011`.

The horizon plan SHOULD prioritize existential Experiment Debt.

---

# 100. Capability Debt

If market demand outruns platform capability, the plan MAY track Capability Debt.

Capability Debt SHALL not be solved by unsupported marketing claims.

---

# 101. Distribution Debt

A product with no repeatable acquisition path carries Distribution Debt.

This should affect scaling decisions.

---

# 102. Economic Debt

A product with unclear unit economics or subsidy sustainability carries Economic Debt.

This should affect funding and scale.

---

# 103. Buyer Debt

A product with users but no economic buyer or Buyer Bridge carries Buyer Debt.

This should not be hidden by usage growth.

---

# 104. Debt Prioritization

Strategic debt types SHOULD be prioritized by:

```text
Existential impact
×
uncertainty
×
time sensitivity
```

No rigid formula is constitutionalized.

# 109. Horizon Escalation

A strategic objective SHOULD move from:

```text

→ 5Y platform direction
```

only when evidence supports the progression.

Calendar alone is insufficient.

---

# 110. Horizon Compression

The Constitution SHALL not slow validated opportunity merely to preserve the plan.

# 111. Horizon Extension

If evidence is slower, an objective MAY move later.

Delay is preferable to pretending validation occurred.

---

# 112. Strategic Abandonment

A planned direction MAY be abandoned entirely.

The abandoned plan SHOULD remain historically visible.

A failed plan does not automatically require North Star review.

A North Star review is justified only when repeated market and domain evidence materially challenges the core mission or platform premise.

Assuming the market will obey dates.

Treating five-year scenarios as promised products.

Erasing prior beliefs after evidence changes.

## Anti-Pattern 6 — Horizon Leakage
Letting Dominate-stage priorities consume Baby resources.

## Anti-Pattern 7 — Strategy by Inertia
Continuing because it was once approved.

## Anti-Pattern 8 — Escape Planning
Adding future domains to avoid present weakness.

## Anti-Pattern 9 — Milestone Theater
Shipping work instead of achieving market states.

Treating capital raised as proof of market truth.

# 133. Constitutional Success Criteria

`Marketing-015` succeeds when:

- Zyppi can focus aggressively today without narrowing tomorrow;
- 1-year plans become specific and executable;
- 3-year plans remain conditional and evidence-aware;
- 5-year plans communicate direction without false precision;

# 134. Closing Principle

Strategy exists because the future is uncertain.

Constitution exists because uncertainty should not erase first principles.

Zyppi therefore needs both:

```text
a stable direction
and
a flexible route
```

The route may change many times.

The destination may become clearer.

The evidence may force Zyppi to slow down, accelerate, abandon a market, discover a new one, or even reconsider parts of the thesis.

The Marketing branch therefore adopts:

> **Plan specifically for the near term. Plan conditionally for the medium term. Plan directionally for the long term. Preserve the North Star above all three. Let evidence change the route without allowing the current route to redefine the destination.**

And:

> **The Constitution protects Zyppi from strategic amnesia. The plan protects Zyppi from constitutional paralysis. Reality decides when either must change.**
