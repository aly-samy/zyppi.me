# ZT-001 — Zyppi Touch Constitution

###### Version 1.0

**Status:** LOCKED & RATIFIED

---

# 1. Purpose

`ZT-001` establishes the constitutional framework governing Touch Events within the Zyppi Runtime Architecture.

A Touch is the formal boundary between the external world and the Zyppi Runtime.

**`ZT-001` defines:**

- what constitutes a Touch
- how Touches are structured
- how Touches are validated
- how Touches resolve Addresses
- how Touches interact with Actors

**`ZT-001` does not govern:**

- Reality Modeling (ZRM)
- Projection Generation (PRJ)
- Experience Composition (EXP)
- Client Rendering (SDK)

---

2. Constitutional Position

`ZT-001` occupies the entry layer of the Zyppi Runtime Architecture.

Constitutional Stack:
````
ARM
 ↓
ZRM
 ↓
PRJ
 ↓
EXP
 ↓
ZT
 ↓
SDK
````
ZT is the sole constitutional entry point through which Actors initiate interaction with Zyppi.

---

# 3. Core Constitutional Principles

## ZT-CP-001 — Modality Independence

A Touch SHALL be defined by its semantic meaning, not its transport mechanism.

QR codes, NFC tags, APIs, AI agent requests, voice commands, RFID reads, BLE interactions, and future interaction technologies are constitutionally equivalent.

---

## ZT-CP-002 — Address Orientation

A Touch targets an Address.

A Touch never directly targets:

- an Experience
- a Projection
- a User Interface

Resolution occurs after Touch validation.

---

## ZT-CP-003 — Actor Neutrality

Human Actors, AI Agents, Machine Actors, and System Actors possess equal constitutional standing.

No Touch behavior may differ solely because of Actor class.

---

## ZT-CP-004 — Immutability

Touches are immutable events.

Once recorded, a Touch cannot be modified.

---

## ZT-CP-005 — Statelessness

A Touch contains no session state.

Journeys and Sessions are derived runtime constructs and possess no constitutional status.

---

# 4. Constitutional Definitions

## Touch

> A Touch is a verifiable, immutable interaction event through which an Actor transmits an Intent Signal toward a Zyppi Address for the purpose of initiating resolution within the Zyppi Runtime Architecture.

---

## Actor

> An Actor is any entity capable of initiating a Touch.

**Valid Actor classes:**

- Human Actor
- AI Agent
- Machine Actor
- System Actor

---

## Address

> An Address is a resolvable Zyppi URI identifying a constitutional target.

**Examples:**
````
zyppi://asset/z123
zyppi://product/z456
zyppi://service/z789
zyppi://experience/template
````

---

## Intent Signal

> An Intent Signal is the semantic purpose expressed by a Touch.

**Intent may be:**

### Human Intent

**Examples:**

- View
- Verify
- Inspect
- Share
- Activate

### Programmatic Intent

**Examples:**

- Scheduled Audit
- Agent Invocation
- Sensor Trigger
- Workflow Execution
- Automated Validation

Programmatic intent is constitutionally equivalent to human intent.

---

# 5. Touch Taxonomy

All Touches belong to one of two constitutional categories.
````
Touch
├── Physical Touch
└── Digital Touch
````
### Physical Touch

**Examples:**

- QR
- NFC
- RFID
- BLE
- UWB
- Vision
- Voice
- AR Selection
- BCI

### Digital Touch

**Examples:**

- API
- SDK Call
- Agent Invocation
- Webhook
- Service Request

Both categories possess identical constitutional standing.

# 6. Touch Event Specification

Every Touch SHALL contain the following fields.
````
touch_event:
  address:
  carrier_type:
  raw_payload:
  actor:
  confidence:
  asserted_timestamp:
  received_timestamp:
  context:
````

---

### asserted_timestamp

> The moment the Intent Signal was captured.

---

### received_timestamp

> The moment the Touch entered the Zyppi Runtime.

Both timestamps are mandatory.

**This guarantees proper operation in:**

- offline environments
- disconnected systems
- edge devices
- delayed synchronization scenarios

---

# 7. Context Envelope

Every Touch SHALL include a Context Envelope.

**Example:**
````
context:
  location:
  locale:
  jurisdiction:
  permissions:
  network:
  device:
````
Context influences resolution.

Context does not alter Touch identity.

---

# 8. Touch Identity

Touches possess no business identity.

Touches are identified by a Touch Signature.

**Example:**
````
touch_signature =
hash(
 actor +
 address +
 asserted_timestamp +
 nonce
)
````
The Touch Signature uniquely identifies a Touch Event.

---

# 9. Security & Integrity

**Touches SHALL support:**

- timestamp validation
- replay detection
- actor attribution
- auditability
- nonce protection

**Touches MAY include:**

- cryptographic signatures
- carrier attestations
- anti-tamper evidence
- hardware proofs

When present, signatures SHALL be verified before resolution.

---

# 10. Canonical Zyppi URI Scheme

All Touches SHALL resolve through the Zyppi URI scheme.

Examples:
````
zyppi://asset/z123
zyppi://product/z456
zyppi://service/z789
zyppi://experience/template
````

---

## Composite Resolution

The Zyppi URI scheme SHALL support composite resolution.

**Example:**
````
zyppi://composite?
targets=asset:z1,batch:b2,item:i3
````
A single Touch MAY resolve multiple constitutional targets.

---

# 11. Resolution Model

Resolution occurs after validation.
````
Touch
 ↓
Address
 ↓
Resolution Engine
 ↓
Target
````
`ZT-001` does not determine the target.

`ZT-001` only initiates resolution.

---

# 12. Resolution Neutrality

**A valid Touch MAY resolve to:**
````
Reality View
Projection
Experience Manifest
Service Endpoint
Action Endpoint
Future Constitutional Artifact
````
**Resolution depends upon:**

- Actor
- Intent
- Context
- Policy
- Address Type

`ZT-001` SHALL never assume Experience generation.

---

# 13. Touch Lifecycle

Touches possess no lifecycle.

**A Touch is:**
````
Generated
Recorded
Immutable
````
Subsequent interactions create new Touches.

---
