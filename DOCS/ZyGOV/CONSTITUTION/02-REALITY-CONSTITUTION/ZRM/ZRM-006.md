# ZRM-006 — Constitutional Boundaries
```
**Document ID:** ZRM-006
**Version:** 1.0 (Draft)
**Status:** Draft — Council Review
**Authority:** Tier 1 (ZRM-000, ZRM-001, ZRM-002) + ZRM-003A + ZRM-003B + ZRM-004 + ZRM-005
**Classification:** Tier 2 — the interface layer between the Reality/Mathematics tiers and everything built on top of them.
```
---

## 1. Purpose

Every document from ZRM-003A through ZRM-005 has ended with its own "Constitutional Boundaries" section, each stating what that specific document doesn't govern. This document does the equivalent work once, at the Tier level, so that Tier 3/4/5 documents have a single place to check what they may and may not do — instead of each one independently inferring it from five separate boundary statements scattered across Tier 2.

This document does not introduce new mathematics. It formalizes an interface.

## 2. Scope

Applies to every document in Tier 3 (Domain Constitutions), Tier 4 (Implementation Constitutions), and Tier 5 (Runtime Constitutions), per the Tier model established in the roadmap.

---

## 3. Boundary to Domain Constitutions (Tier 3)

**Contents of Tier 3:** WS series → RI series, in that sequential order (RI depends specifically on WS-05, not on Tier 3 generically).

**What Tier 3 may do:** specialize the constructs derived in ZRM-005 §6 (Touchpoint, Intent, Contract, System, Transaction, Outcome, Campaign, Intelligence) into operational registries, taxonomies, and cluster ownership models — this is exactly what WS-01 through WS-05E already did, pre-ZRM-v3, and what the Tier 3 Legacy Revision Pass (roadmap Phase 5) will bring into alignment with this document's derivations.

**What Tier 3 may not do:**
- Introduce a construct that doesn't trace back to a ZRM-005 §6 derivation, or to ZRM-003B/004 directly for lower-level concepts (Identity, Event, State, Time).
- Redefine any mathematical property established in ZRM-003A/B or ZRM-004 (e.g., a Tier 3 document cannot declare that Identity is mutable, or that Events may be modified after recording).
- Introduce a seventeenth-cluster-style ontology that competes with, rather than specializes, the ZRM-005 derivations. Per Rule 3.2, any conflict is resolved by revising the Tier 3 document, never by weakening this tier.

## 4. Boundary to Implementation Constitutions (Tier 4)

**Contents of Tier 4:** SDK-SPEC-001, PLATFORM-SPEC-001, DEV-ARCH-001.

**What Tier 4 may do:** define developer-facing interfaces (SDKs, APIs, CLI, plugins) that expose Tier 3's operational registries to builders, per PLATFORM-SPEC-001's own stated principle that "the Constitution may disappear from sight, it may never disappear from execution."

**What Tier 4 may not do:**
- Alter constitutional behavior for developer convenience. PLATFORM-SPEC-001 §5.2 already states this ("Convenience shall never weaken governance") — this document reaffirms it as a hard Tier boundary, not just a platform philosophy.
- Introduce implementation-specific mathematics (serialization formats, encoding schemes, storage models) as if it were constitutional content. ZRM-002 and ZRM-003B already explicitly excluded this; Tier 4 is where it actually belongs.

## 5. Boundary to Runtime Constitutions (Tier 5)

**Contents of Tier 5:** P4-000 → SEC-001 → RI-006 → POL-001 → SDK/Platform → FED-001 → MKT-001, per P4-000's own already-ratified critical path — sequential, not a flat bucket (per the roadmap's caution against flattening this).

**What Tier 5 may do:** define trust, security, policy evaluation, execution scheduling, and federation — none of which are mathematical claims about Reality, and none of which this document or any Tier 2 document attempts to govern.

**What Tier 5 may not do:**
- Treat a Policy or Runtime decision as capable of modifying `G` (the Reality Graph, ZRM-003B GR-D01) directly. Per ZRM-005 §6.5/§6.8, only a Transaction (an Event) may change `G`'s edge set; Policy and Runtime govern *whether* a Transaction is authorized, never substitute for one.
- Claim that Intelligence (ZRM-005 §6.8) may trigger Transactions directly. P4-000's own Runtime/Intelligence Separation (Principle 3) and WS-03A.9's CP-INT-03 already establish this; this document is the formal Tier-level backstop for both.

## 6. Constitutional Extension Rules

A downstream tier may extend a Tier 2 construct — adding a new Intent type, a new Outcome family, a new Contract category — provided the extension:

1. Is expressed as a further specialization of an existing ZRM-005 derivation, not a new primitive (per ZRM-003A §5.2, no new primitives, inherited downward).
2. Carries a citation back to the specific ZRM-005 derivation it specializes.
3. Does not require modifying any ZRM-003A/B, ZRM-004, or ZRM-005 content to accommodate it. If it does require that, it isn't an extension — it's a proposed amendment to Tier 2, and must go through Tier 2's own amendment process (ZRM-007), not be smuggled in downstream.

This is the operational form of Rule 3.2 (Constitutional Evolution) and the Document Granularity Principle, applied specifically to the Tier 2/3 boundary.

## 7. Constitutional Compliance Requirements

A downstream document is compliant with this boundary when:

- Every construct it uses traces to a Tier 2 derivation or definition, per §3–5 above.
- No downstream document redefines a Tier 2 term (ZRM-000 Rule 4.7, Single Authority, applies across tiers, not just within ZRM).
- Any apparent conflict between a downstream document and a Tier 2 document is resolved by revising the downstream document — never by treating the conflict as evidence Tier 2 needs to change, per Rule 3.2's "parents are never revised to accommodate children."

Compliance is checked the same way S6 checks anything else: by falsification, not by self-report. A Tier 3/4/5 document claiming compliance without having gone through its own review pass carries no more weight than an unratified theorem.

## 8. Explicit Negative Scope

This document, and Tier 2 as a whole, does not govern:

- Business logic, workflows, or product decisions (PRD-v4.1's domain)
- Pricing, billing, or commercial models (explicitly out of scope per PLATFORM-SPEC-001 §16)
- Marketing, UI/UX, or developer documentation style
- Any specific programming language, database, or cloud provider (ZRM-002's technology-independence exclusions apply transitively through every downstream tier)

## 10. Completion Criteria

Substantially complete as an interface document, since it doesn't introduce new mathematics — but two items remain open:

- §5's claim about Intelligence/Transaction separation depends on ZRM-005 §6.8, which itself depends on ZRM-003B's Projection Function (§10) being ratified, not just drafted. If that changes, this section needs re-checking.
- No S6 pass has touched this document yet, and unlike ZRM-003A/B and ZRM-004, this document has no legacy mining behind it at all — it's a fresh synthesis of patterns established elsewhere in Tier 2. That's appropriate for a boundary document, but worth being explicit that "no mining provenance" is expected here, not a gap.
