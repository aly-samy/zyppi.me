# Definition


A **Friction** is persistent, observable resistance arising within a System as it responds to Strategic Forces, resulting in measurable deviation from its Reference Organization.


Friction is descriptive rather than evaluative.

It describes resistance.

It does not imply failure, priority, or the need for intervention.

Friction is not a problem.

Friction is not a solution.

Friction is an analytical description of resistance.


---


# First Principles


A Friction:

- emerges only through interaction between Forces and Systems
- is observable
- persists beyond isolated Events
- is evidenced
- is measurable
- exists independently of Zyppi
- is domain-independent


Without a System there is no Friction.


Without Forces there is no Friction.

# Analytical Constructs


## Friction


Persistent resistance within a System.


---


## Constraint


A condition limiting possible behavior.


---


## Bottleneck


Localized resistance restricting movement through a System.


---


## Delay


Observable temporal resistance.


---


## Latency


The elapsed time between cause and observable effect within a System.


Latency is a temporal form of Friction.


---


## Throughput


The observable rate at which Subjects, Objects, or Events move through a System.


Throughput is measured, never assumed.


Reduced throughput may indicate Friction but does not define it.


---


## Saturation Point


The condition where available System capacity has been fully utilized.


Additional demand cannot be accommodated without structural change.


---


## Failure Point


The observable condition where a System can no longer sustain its Reference Organization under existing Strategic Forces.


Failure is descriptive rather than evaluative.


It does not imply poor design.


---


## Misalignment


Observable inconsistency between interacting components.


---


## Resistance


Observable opposition to structural change.

# Diagnostic Properties


Every Friction should be described through observable properties.


## Magnitude


Observable degree of resistance.


Magnitude is descriptive.


It is not prioritization.


---


## Persistence


Duration across multiple observations.


Temporary resistance is not necessarily Friction.


---


## Propagation


Extent to which resistance spreads across connected Systems.


---


## Recurrence


Frequency of repeated manifestation.


---


## Locality


Specific location where resistance originates.


---


## Latency


Time delay introduced into the System.


---


## Throughput Impact


Observable change in throughput relative to the System's Reference Organization.


Throughput impact is one indicator of Friction.


It is not the definition of Friction.

## Recursive Friction


A diagnosed Friction within one System may alter the operating conditions of another System.


When this occurs, the resulting conditions may function as Strategic Forces acting upon downstream Systems.


This allows constitutional analysis across nested and interconnected Systems without redefining either Force or Friction.
