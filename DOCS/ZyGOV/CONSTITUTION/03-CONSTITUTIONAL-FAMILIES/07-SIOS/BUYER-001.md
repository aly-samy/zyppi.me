# Purpose


BUYER establishes the constitutional framework for recognizing, defining, governing, and maintaining **Decision Authority** within a Category.


It exists to answer one constitutional question:


> **Who possesses the authority to authorize, deny, constrain, or approve changes affecting this Category?**


BUYER does **not** determine:

- messaging
- branding
- positioning
- pricing
- marketing
- products
- channels
- execution


Those responsibilities belong to downstream frameworks.


BUYER is authority recognition—not commercialization.

## Principle 4 — Decisions Are Structural

# Constitutional Definitions


## Buyer


A Buyer is a Participant possessing recognized Authority to authorize, deny, constrain, or approve changes affecting a Category.


Authority is recognized through observable evidence.


It is never assumed.


---


## Authority


Authority is the constitutional capacity to authorize, deny, constrain, or approve state change within a defined Boundary.


Authority is structural.


Not hierarchical.


---


## Decision


A Decision is an Event in which recognized Authority changes, preserves, rejects, or constrains a possible Category state.


Decisions are evidenced.


Not assumed.


---


## Decision Surface


The observable interface through which constitutional decisions become detectable.


A Decision Surface includes every constitutional point at which Authority may evaluate, approve, reject, delegate, delay, or constrain a proposed intervention.


Decision Surfaces may be human, organizational, computational, regulatory, contractual, or autonomous.


A Decision Surface does not create Authority.


It reveals where Authority becomes observable.


---


## Decision State


The current constitutional status of a decision within a Buyer's decision process.


Typical Decision States include:


- Unknown
- Observing
- Evaluating
- Pending
- Approved
- Rejected
- Delegated
- Deferred
- Constrained
- Executing
- Completed


Decision States describe observable progression.


They do not imply probability or preference.


---


## Authority Graph


The observable network of Authorities participating in constitutional decision-making.


Authority rarely exists as a single individual.


Authority is distributed across relationships between Subjects possessing different Authority Types, Authority Scopes, and Authority Boundaries.


Authority Graphs describe:


- reporting relationships
- approval relationships
- advisory relationships
- dependency relationships
- delegated authority
- shared authority


Authority Graphs describe constitutional structure.


They never prescribe organizational design.


---


## Authority Evolution


The constitutional process through which Authority changes over time.


Authority may emerge, expand, contract, transfer, merge, divide, or disappear as observable Events occur.


Authority Evolution is governed by constitutional Reality.


It is never assumed.


Every observed change requires supporting Evidence.

# Authority Types


Authority describes **what may be controlled**, not **who controls it**.


## Economic Authority


Controls the commitment or allocation of economic resources.


---


## Operational Authority


Controls operational adoption, execution, or ongoing operation.


---


## Technical Authority


Controls technical correctness, compatibility, architecture, or implementation acceptance.


---


## Regulatory Authority


Controls compliance with externally imposed laws, regulations, standards, or mandatory requirements.


---


## Policy Authority


Controls internally established governance, institutional rules, or organizational policies.


---


## Programmatic Authority


Controls automated or algorithmic authorization through deterministic systems including software, APIs, AI agents, smart contracts, or rule engines.


---


## Fiduciary Authority


Controls decisions exercised on behalf of another recognized Authority or represented entity.


Authority Types are independent.


None imply superiority.
