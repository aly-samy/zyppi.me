# Purpose


This document defines the immutable truths of reality upon which every other SIOS document depends.


It does not describe software.


It does not describe Zyppi.


It does not describe products, markets, customers, or technology.


It describes only truths that remain valid regardless of civilization, industry, implementation, or time.


Every higher document in SIOS must emerge naturally from this foundation.


If any higher document contradicts this thesis, the higher document is wrong.


---


# Constitutional Principles


Reality exists independently of observers.


Reality is never altered by our models.


Software does not create reality.


Software observes, represents, reasons about, and responds to reality.


The purpose of every higher SIOS layer is to become progressively better at representing reality without ever replacing it.


---




# Canonical Primitive Ontology


Reality SHALL be described using a closed set of constitutional primitives.


Every higher constitutional construct SHALL ultimately reduce to one or more of these primitives.


No additional primitive may be introduced unless this document itself is constitutionally amended.


The canonical primitive set consists of exactly five primitives:

- Subject
- Object
- Place
- Event
- Evidence

These primitives are irreducible.


Every higher construct within SIOS SHALL be derivable from them.


---




## Primitive P1 — Subject


A Subject is an entity capable of observation, action, decision, or participation.


Subjects may be human, organizational, biological, digital, or autonomous.


Subjecthood is determined by function rather than implementation.


---


## Primitive P2 — Object


An Object is an identifiable entity that exists independently of observation and may participate in Events.


Objects possess observable properties.


Objects are not defined by identity.


Identity emerges later through interpretation.


---


## Primitive P3 — Place


A Place is the spatial context in which Objects and Events exist.


Every Object exists somewhere.


Every Event occurs somewhere.


---


## Primitive P4 — Event


An Event is an observable change occurring through time.


Events connect Subjects, Objects, and Places.


Events produce Evidence.


---


## Primitive P5 — Evidence


Evidence is the observable trace produced by Events.


Evidence reduces uncertainty.


Evidence never becomes Reality itself.


---


# Primitive Closure


The primitive ontology defined above is constitutionally closed.


Every future constitutional concept SHALL satisfy one of the following:

- Primitive
- Derived Construct
- Analytical Property
- Evaluative Decision


Only REALITY-001 may introduce or retire primitives.


All higher documents SHALL remain primitive-neutral.

The canonical primitive ontology SHALL remain stable.

# Layer A — Reality Exists


These truths describe reality itself.


They exist whether or not they are observed.


---


## Article A1 — Things Exist


Reality consists of distinct things.


Things exist independently of observers.


Existence does not require recognition, naming, or representation.


---


## Article A2 — Time Exists


Reality unfolds through time.


Nothing is timeless.


Every occurrence belongs somewhere in time.


---


## Article A3 — Space Exists


Everything exists somewhere.


Location is inseparable from existence.


Nothing exists nowhere.


---


## Article A4 — Change Exists


Reality is never static.


Everything changes continuously.


Change does not require observation.


Observation merely reveals change.


---


# Layer B — Reality Persists


These truths govern how reality behaves.


---


## Article B1 — Continuity


Reality is continuous.


The present emerges from the past.


The future emerges from the present.


History cannot be undone.


---


## Article B2 — Causality


Nothing happens without causes.


Every change has antecedents.


Effects cannot precede causes.


Reality is causally ordered.


---


## Article B3 — Entropy


Reality naturally moves toward disorder.


Information degrades.


Physical evidence deteriorates.


Certainty diminishes unless renewed through observation.


---


## Article B4 — Scarcity


Reality imposes limits.


Attention is limited.


Energy is limited.


Movement requires time.


Presence occupies space.


Capacity is finite.


No thing can participate in unlimited interactions simultaneously.


---


# Layer C — Reality Is Observed


Reality and observation are not the same.


Observation is always incomplete.


---


## Article C1 — Observation


Reality becomes knowable only through observation.


Observation never changes reality.


It only reduces uncertainty.


---


## Article C2 — Evidence


Observations produce evidence.


Evidence is the observable trace of reality.


Evidence may be incomplete, delayed, corrupted, or lost.


Evidence is not reality.


It is representation.


---


## Article C3 — Uncertainty


Observation is always limited.


Perfect knowledge is unattainable.


Uncertainty is therefore a permanent property of observation.


It cannot be eliminated.


It can only be reduced.


---


# Layer D — Meaning Emerges


Meaning does not exist independently.


Meaning emerges from interpretation of observed reality.


---


## Article D1 — Identity


Identity is not an intrinsic property of reality.


Identity is the persistent interpretation assigned to distinguishable things across time.


Identity emerges from continuity and observation.


---


## Article D2 — Context


Facts alone possess little meaning.


Meaning depends upon surrounding circumstances.


Context emerges from combinations of:

- Time
- Space
- Change
- Observation
- Identity


The same observation may imply different meanings under different contexts.


---


## Article D3 — Relationships


Reality contains relationships independent of observation.


Understanding those relationships requires interpretation.


Meaning emerges from how identities, observations, contexts, and changes relate across time and space.


---


## Article D4 — Trust


Trust is never assumed.


Trust is derived.


Trust emerges from accumulated evidence observed over time.


Trust is therefore dynamic rather than absolute.
