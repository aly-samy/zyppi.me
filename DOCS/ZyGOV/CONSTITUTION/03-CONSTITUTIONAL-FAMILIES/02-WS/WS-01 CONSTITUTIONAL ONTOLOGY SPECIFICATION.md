## 1. PURPOSE
This document defines the constitutional ontology of Zyppi.

It establishes the foundational semantic model from which:
- The Knowledge Graph
- Entity Registry
- Glossary System
- Website Information Architecture
- Internal Linking Architecture
- Schema Strategy
- AI Authority Layer
- Intent Infrastructure
- Reality Infrastructure
will be derived.

No entity may be introduced into the Knowledge Graph unless it can be traced back to this ontology.

This document supersedes individual page-level interpretations.

## 2. DESIGN PRINCIPLES
The ontology must satisfy five independent systems simultaneously:
- North Star
- Founding Principles
- ZRM (Zyppi Reality Model)
- Website Knowledge Graph
- Future Product Architecture
A constitutional entity is any concept that remains valid across all five systems.

## 3. ONTOLOGICAL PHILOSOPHY
Most software platforms model records.

> Zyppi models reality.

### Traditional systems ask:
`"What happened?"`

### Zyppi asks:
> ***"Who did what, to which thing, through which identity, under which context, with what intent, using which system, producing which outcome, and with what confidence?"***

The constitutional ontology exists to preserve the answer.

## 4. CORE ONTOLOGY MODEL
The ontology describes how reality is represented.

### Constitutional Flow:

> Actor → Surface → Touchpoint → Identity → Referent → Intent → Intent Contract → System → Transaction → Outcome

The resulting interactions contribute to Reality.

#### **Important:**
Reality is NOT a row. Reality is NOT a record. Reality is NOT an entity instance.
Reality is the continuously evolving graph generated by all entities, relationships, context, signals, events, and outcomes over time.
Reality is therefore an emergent system-level state.
Reality is the derived graph state of the Zyppi Reality Graph and is not represented as a registry entity or cluster.

## 5. CONSTITUTIONAL PRIMITIVES (TIER 0)
These are the highest-order entities in the ontology.

Nothing sits above them.

### 5.1 ACTOR
**Definition:**
> An entity capable of initiating, participating in, observing, influencing, or executing interactions.

**Examples:**
- Human Consumer
- Enterprise User
- Retail Associate
- Supply Chain Operator
- AI Agent
- Autonomous System
- API Client
- Business Process

**Required Attributes:**
- actor_id
- actor_type
- confidence
- created_at

#### Actor Types:
Human, Organization, System, AI Agent, Autonomous Agent Application, API Client

#### Relationships:
- Actor INITIATES Touchpoint
- Actor EXPRESSES Intent
- Actor RECEIVES Outcome
- Actor OBSERVES Reality

### 5.2 SURFACE
**Definition:**
> The physical or digital environment where a touchpoint exists.

A surface hosts a touchpoint.

**Examples:**
- Product Package
- Store Shelf
- Retail Display
- Mobile Application
- Website
- Invoice
- Restaurant Table
- Email
- Smart Device

**Required Attributes:**
- surface_id
- surface_type

**Relationships:**
- Surface HOSTS Touchpoint
- Surface BELONGS_TO Referent

### 5.3 TOUCHPOINT
**Definition:**
> A mechanism through which an actor accesses an identity.

**Examples:**
- QR Code
- Dynamic QR
- Smart Link
- GS1 Digital Link
- NFC Tag
- Barcode
- RFID
- URL
- API Endpoint

**Required Attributes:**
- touchpoint_id
- touchpoint_type

**Relationships:**
- Touchpoint RESOLVES_TO Identity
- Touchpoint EXISTS_ON Surface
- Touchpoint GENERATES Signal

### 5.4 IDENTITY
**Definition:**
> A persistent digital representation of something.
>
> Identity is not the thing itself.
>
> Identity is the digital layer representing the thing.

**Examples:**
- Product Identity
- Asset Identity
- Location Identity
- Person Identity
- Campaign Identity
- Package Identity
- Document Identity

**Required Attributes:**
- identity_id
- identity_type
- canonical_reference

**Relationships:**
- Identity REPRESENTS Referent
- Identity EXPOSES Intent Contract
- Identity BELONGS_TO System

### 5.5 REFERENT
**Definition:**
> The actual thing represented by an identity.
>
> `Identity ≠ Referent` , This distinction is mandatory.

**Examples:**
- A product QR code resolves to a Product Identity.
- That Product Identity represents a physical product.
- The physical product is the Referent.

#### Referent Types
- Physical Product
- Store Location
- Package
- Package Contents
- Person
- Organization
- Device
- Document
- Event
- Vehicle
- Asset
- Digital Asset
- Campaign Asset
- Service

**Required Attributes**
- referent_id
- referent_type

**Relationships**
- Referent IS_REPRESENTED_BY Identity
- Referent EXISTS_ON Surface
- Referent PARTICIPATES_IN Reality

### 5.6 INTENT
**Definition:**
> A desired future outcome expressed by an actor.
> Intent exists before action.
> Intent is not execution.
> Intent is desire.

**Examples:**
- Verify
- Buy
- Register
- Learn
- Authenticate
- Contact
- Navigate
- Claim
- Activate
- Subscribe

**Required Attributes:**
- intent_id
- intent_type

**Relationships:**
- Actor EXPRESSES Intent
- Intent MATCHES Intent Contract
- Intent INITIATES Transaction

### 5.7 INTENT CONTRACT
**Definition:**
> A machine-readable declaration describing how an intent may be fulfilled.

Intent Contracts remain first-class entities.

They are not properties.

They are one of Zyppi's strategic category-creation assets.

**Examples:**
- Verification Contract
- Purchase Contract
- Registration Contract
- Authentication Contract
- Activation Contract

**Required Attributes:**
- contract_id
- contract_type
- conditions
- permissions

**Relationships:**
- Identity EXPOSES Intent Contract
- Intent MATCHES Intent Contract
- Intent Contract GOVERNS Transaction

### 5.8 SYSTEM
**Definition:**
> A mechanism capable of executing transactions.

**Examples:**
- Routing Engine
- Intent Resolution Engine
- Authentication Service
- Payment Gateway
- CRM
- ERP
- AI Agent
- Enterprise Platform

**Required Attributes:**
- system_id
- system_type

**Relationships:**
- System EXECUTES Transaction
- System GENERATES Event
- System GENERATES Signal

### 5.9 TRANSACTION
**Definition:**
> An execution event attempting to fulfill an intent.

#### Transaction Lifecycle:
- Initiated
- Pending
- Executing
- Completed
- Failed
- Rejected
- Expired
- Cancelled

**Required Attributes:**
- transaction_id
- transaction_state

**Relationships:**
- Transaction PRODUCES Outcome
- Transaction GENERATES Event
- Transaction GENERATES Signal

### 5.10 OUTCOME
**Definition:**
> A state change produced by a completed transaction.

**Examples:**
- Verified
- Purchased
- Registered
- Activated
- Authenticated
- Failed Verification
- Rejected Registration

**Required Attributes:**
- outcome_id
- outcome_type

**Relationships:**
- Outcome CONTRIBUTES_TO Reality
- Outcome GENERATES Intelligence

## 6. HORIZONTAL CONSTITUTIONAL CONCEPTS
These concepts operate across all primitives.
They are not sequential stages.

### CONTEXT
> Context modifies meaning.
**Examples:**
- Time
- Location
- Device
- Campaign
- Environment
- Audience Segment
- Language
**Context can influence:**
- Intent
- Contracts
- Routing
- Outcomes

### TRUST
**Definition:**
> The degree to which an identity, actor, transaction, or outcome is believed to be authentic.
Trust is accumulated.
Trust is not binary.

### CONFIDENCE
**Definition:**
> A measurable certainty score attached to decisions and outcomes.
**Examples:**
- Authentication Confidence
- Attribution Confidence
- AI Confidence

### RESOLUTION
**Definition:**
> The process of mapping a touchpoint to an identity.
**Examples:**
- QR Resolution
- Digital Link Resolution
- NFC Resolution
Resolution is a constitutional operation.

### SIGNAL
**Definition:**
> A measurable observation generated by interactions.
**Examples:**
- Scan
- Click
- View
- Redirect
- Verification Request

### EVENT
**Definition:**
> A recorded occurrence within a system.
Events become historical facts.

### INTELLIGENCE
**Definition:**

----

> **Knowledge derived from:**
> - Signals
> - Events
> - Outcomes
> - Context

----

**Examples:**
- Attribution Insight
- Consumer Behavior Insight
- Product Intelligence

### GRAPH
**Definition:**
> The relationship structure connecting all entities.
> The graph is the foundation of Reality Infrastructure.

## 10. TEMPORAL GOVERNANCE
**Every constitutional entity must support:**
- created_at
- updated_at
- valid_from
- valid_to

**Event-driven entities additionally support:**
- occurred_at

**Every relationship must support:**
- relationship_created_at
- relationship_valid_from
- relationship_valid_to

**Reason:**
Reality requires time.
Without time:
- Traceability fails
- Compliance fails
- Auditability fails
- Intelligence fails

## 11. RELATIONSHIP GOVERNANCE
1. Relationships are mandatory.
2. Relationships are typed.
3. Directional-only relationships are prohibited.

**Examples:**
- Actor INITIATES Touchpoint
- Touchpoint RESOLVES_TO Identity
- Identity REPRESENTS Referent
- Actor EXPRESSES Intent
- Intent MATCHES Intent Contract
- Intent Contract GOVERNS Transaction
- System EXECUTES Transaction
- Transaction PRODUCES Outcome
- Outcome CONTRIBUTES_TO Reality
