# 1. Purpose
 
This specification establishes the constitutional principles governing:

- Federation
 
- Sovereignty
 
- Cross-domain recognition
 
- Emergency governance
 
- Constitutional evolution
 
- Constitutional succession
 
This specification intentionally excludes federation protocols, networking mechanisms, synchronization algorithms, and runtime implementation.
  
# 2. Constitutional Federation
 
Federation is the constitutional cooperation of independent sovereign domains.
 
Federation preserves sovereignty.
 
Federation does not merge constitutions.
  
# 3. Sovereignty
 
Each constitutional domain remains sovereign.
 
No participating domain automatically inherits constitutional authority from another.
 
Participation in Federation shall never diminish constitutional independence.
  
## Sovereignty Principles
 
### FED-001
 
Sovereignty is preserved.
  
### FED-002
 
Recognition does not imply control.
  
### FED-003
 
Federation never creates a superior sovereign authority.
  
### FED-004
 
Withdrawal from Federation preserves constitutional identity.
  
# 4. Cross-Domain Recognition
 
Constitutional domains may recognize authorities, identities, permissions, or policies established by another domain.
 
Recognition is explicit.
 
Recognition is revocable.
 
Recognition is bounded.
 
Recognition is jurisdiction-specific.
 
Recognition is never implied.
  
## Recognition Principles
 
### CR-001
 
Recognition requires explicit constitutional agreement.
  
### CR-002
 
Recognition never transfers sovereignty.
  
### CR-003
 
Recognition may be partial.
  
### CR-004
 
Recognition may expire.
  
### CR-005
 
Recognition shall remain attributable.
  
# 5. Federation Trust
 
Trust between constitutional domains shall exist only through explicit constitutional relationship.
 
Trust is never transitive.
 
Trust shall always possess identifiable origin.
 
Termination of trust affects future cooperation only.
 
Historical constitutional records remain valid.
  
# 6. Emergency Governance
 
Emergency Governance is a temporary constitutional mechanism allowing extraordinary authority under exceptional constitutional circumstances.
 
Emergency Governance exists to preserve constitutional order.
 
It shall never permanently replace ordinary constitutional governance.
  
## Emergency Principles
 
### EG-001
 
Emergency authority shall possess explicit constitutional origin.
  
### EG-002
 
Emergency authority shall be temporally bounded.
  
### EG-003
 
Emergency authority shall remain fully attributable.
  
### EG-004
 
Emergency authority shall remain permanently auditable.
  
### EG-005
 
Termination of emergency governance restores ordinary constitutional governance.
  
# 7. Constitutional Evolution
 
The Constitution may evolve.
 
Evolution shall preserve constitutional continuity.
 
Evolution shall never rewrite constitutional history.
 
Evolution occurs through constitutional succession.
  
## Evolution Principles
 
### EV-001
 
Constitutional amendments create successor constitutional artifacts.
  
### EV-002
 
Historical constitutions remain permanent constitutional records.
  
### EV-003
 
Constitutional evolution shall preserve interpretability of historical events.
  
### EV-004
 
Constitutional evolution shall never invalidate previously valid constitutional history.
  
# 8. Organizational Succession
 
Organizations may undergo constitutional succession through events including:
 
- merger
 
- acquisition
 
- dissolution
 
- restructuring
 
- division
 
Succession transfers governance through explicitly established successor constitutional structures.
 
Historical organizations remain permanent constitutional identities.
  
# 9. Constitutional Conflict
 
Independent constitutional domains may define conflicting governance.
 
Conflict resolution shall occur through explicit constitutional agreements.
 
Absent such agreements, each sovereign domain governs independently.
 
The Constitution establishes the requirement for conflict resolution but does not prescribe specific resolution strategies.
  
# 10. Constitutional Independence
 
Federated domains retain independent authority over:
 
- identity governance,
 
- standing governance,
 
- authority governance,
 
- policy governance,
 
- constitutional evolution.
 
Federation shall not centralize constitutional control.
  
# 11. Permanent Invariants
 
The following invariants SHALL remain true.
 
- Sovereignty is preserved.
 
- Federation is voluntary.
 
- Recognition is explicit.
 
- Recognition never transfers sovereignty.
 
- Trust is never transitive.
 
- Emergency authority is temporary.
 
- Emergency authority is attributable.
 
- Constitutional evolution occurs through succession.
 
- History is immutable.
 
- Federation never creates a superior constitutional authority.

  
# 12. Constitutional Boundaries
 
POL-001.E establishes constitutional governance principles only.
 
It intentionally excludes:

- federation networking,
 
- trust exchange protocols,
 
- distributed synchronization,
 
- consensus mechanisms,
 
- conflict resolution algorithms,
 
- runtime emergency procedures,
 
- communication protocols.
 
Those concerns belong to FED-001 and Runtime specifications.
