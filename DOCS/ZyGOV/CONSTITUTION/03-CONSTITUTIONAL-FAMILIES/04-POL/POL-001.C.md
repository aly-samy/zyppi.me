# POL-001.C — Authorization & Constitutional Decisioning

# 1. Purpose
 
This specification establishes the constitutional principles governing Authorization.
 
It defines:

- what Authorization means,
 
- the constitutional dimensions considered,
 
- the relationship between Authority and Permission,
 
- the role of Compliance,
 
- the role of Consent,
 
- the constitutional nature of authorization decisions.
 
This specification intentionally excludes:
 
- evaluation algorithms,
 
- rule engines,
 
- execution order,
 
- implementation mechanisms.
  
# 2. Constitutional Definition
 
Authorization is the constitutional determination that a requested action is permitted under applicable Authority, Policy, Context, and Governance.
 
Authorization is a constitutional decision.
 
It is not an execution.
  
# 3. Constitutional Dimensions
 
Every Authorization SHALL consider the following constitutional dimensions.
  
## Subject
 
Who requests the action.
  
## Action
 
What is requested.
  
## Target
 
What Reality is affected.
  
## Context
 
The constitutional circumstances under which the action is requested.
 
Authorization exists only through the complete relationship between all four dimensions.
  
# 4. Authority and Permission
 
Authority and Permission are distinct constitutional concepts.
 
Authority defines:
 
> "What may this actor legitimately authorize?"
 
Permission defines:
 
> "Is this specific action constitutionally allowed?"
 
Authority enables Permission.
 
Authority does not guarantee Permission.
  
# 5. Constitutional Principles
 
## AU-001 — Explicit Authorization
 
Every permitted action SHALL possess explicit constitutional authorization.
  
## AU-002 — Contextual Authorization
 
Authorization is always contextual.
 
No permission exists independent of Context.
  
## AU-003 — Minimum Permission
 
Authorization SHALL grant only the permissions necessary for the requested constitutional purpose.
  
## AU-004 — Independent Decisions
 
Every authorization request constitutes an independent constitutional determination.
 
Previous decisions do not constitutionally imply future decisions.
  
## AU-005 — Deterministic Validity
 
Identical constitutional circumstances SHALL produce identical constitutional authorization.
  
# 6. Compliance
 
Compliance is a constitutional dimension independent from Authorization.
 
Authorization answers:
 
> "May this action occur?"
 
Compliance answers:
 
> "Does this action satisfy governing obligations?"
 
Neither implies the other.
  
## Compliance Principles
 
### CPM-001
 
Authorization without Compliance remains constitutionally distinguishable.
  
### CPM-002
 
Compliance without Authorization grants no permission.
  
### CPM-003
 
Compliance obligations may originate from:

- law,
 
- regulation,
 
- organizational governance,
 
- contractual obligation,
 
- federation agreement.
 

  
# 7. Consent
 
Consent is a constitutional declaration made by an affected Identity regarding actions involving itself.
 
Consent is distinct from Authority.
 
Consent is distinct from Permission.
 
Consent is distinct from Compliance.
  
## Consent Principles
 
### CST-001
 
Consent never creates Authority.
  
### CST-002
 
Consent never overrides constitutional prohibition.
  
### CST-003
 
Consent may be required as a constitutional prerequisite for Authorization.
  
### CST-004
 
Consent remains attributable to the consenting Identity.
  
# 8. Jurisdiction
 
Authorization exists within one or more constitutional jurisdictions.
 
Jurisdictions determine applicable governance.
 
Jurisdiction never modifies constitutional principles established by POL.
  
## Jurisdiction Principles
 
### JR-001
 
Authorization SHALL always occur within recognized jurisdiction.
  
### JR-002
 
Multiple jurisdictions may simultaneously govern one Authorization.
  
### JR-003
 
Conflicting jurisdictions SHALL be resolved through constitutional governance.
 
Resolution mechanisms are defined elsewhere.
  
# 9. Explainability
 
Every Authorization SHALL possess constitutional explainability.
 
Explainability requires that an authorization decision be capable of identifying:
 
- governing Authority,
 
- governing Policy,
 
- governing Context,
 
- governing Jurisdiction,
 
- governing Constraints.
  
Explainability is a constitutional property.
 
It is not a user interface.
  
# 10. Constitutional Decisions
 
Authorization produces one constitutional decision.
 
Possible constitutional outcomes include:

- Authorized
 
- Denied
 
- Conditionally Authorized
 
- Deferred
  
Additional implementation-specific outcomes may extend these classifications without modifying this Constitution.
  
# 11. Permanent Invariants
 
The following invariants SHALL remain true.
 
- Authorization requires Authority.
 
- Authority does not imply Permission.
 
- Permission is contextual.
 
- Compliance is orthogonal.
 
- Consent is independent.
 
- Jurisdiction constrains governance.
 
- Authorization is deterministic.
 
- Every decision is attributable.
 
- Every decision is explainable.
 
- Authorization never constitutes execution.

  
# 12. Constitutional Boundaries
 
POL-001.C establishes constitutional authorization principles only.
 
It intentionally excludes:
  
- policy evaluation algorithms,
 
- rule processing,
 
- runtime execution order,
 
- caching,
 
- optimization,
 
- policy languages,
 
- interface definitions.
 
Those belong to POL-001.F and Runtime specifications.
