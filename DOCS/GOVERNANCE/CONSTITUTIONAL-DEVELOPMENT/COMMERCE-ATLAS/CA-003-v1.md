# 8. Universal Commerce Interaction Pattern

Commerce Discovery established that although commercial domains differ in purpose, regulation, terminology, and implementation, they consistently exhibit the same recurring interaction structure.

This structure is **not** a workflow specification, business process, or software pipeline.

It is an **observed constitutional pattern** describing how commercial interactions repeatedly unfold across reality.

The pattern is descriptive rather than prescriptive.

Commerce may branch, recurse, repeat, terminate, or recover, but every observed interaction can be understood through this common structure.

---

## 8.1 Canonical Interaction Pattern

```
Presentation
      ↓
Observation
      ↓
Evaluation
      ↓
Decision
      ↓
Execution
      ↓
Evidence Generation
      ↓
Continuation
```

This sequence represents the most common recurring organization of Events observed throughout Commerce Discovery.

It is not mandatory.

Rather, it serves as the constitutional reference model for understanding commercial interactions.

---

## 8.2 Stage Definitions

### Presentation

Something becomes available for observation.

Examples include:

- a product presented at checkout
- a credential presented at a checkpoint
- a shipment arriving at a warehouse
- a QR code presented for scanning
- a document submitted for review

Presentation does not imply acceptance or action.

---

### Observation

A Subject acquires information about reality.

Observation may occur through:

- direct human perception
- automated sensing
- machine inspection
- digital acquisition

Observation creates the possibility for evaluation but does not itself alter reality.

---

### Evaluation

Observed information is examined against applicable references.

Evaluation may include:

- inspection
- comparison
- verification
- validation
- authentication
- measurement
- classification

Evaluation is an observable Event.

Its purpose is to determine whether sufficient conditions exist for a decision.

---

### Decision

A Subject determines how to proceed based upon the evaluated evidence.

Examples include:

- approve
- reject
- defer
- escalate
- redirect
- request additional information

A Decision influences subsequent Events but does not itself complete commerce.

---

### Execution

The chosen action is performed.

Examples include:

- transfer
- movement
- authorization
- fulfillment
- denial
- cancellation
- delivery
- transformation

Execution changes observable reality.

---

### Evidence Generation

Execution produces observable traces.

Evidence may include:

- receipts
- audit records
- digital logs
- signatures
- photographs
- confirmations
- transaction records
- shipping manifests

Evidence enables future observation, accountability, and reasoning.

---

### Continuation

Commerce rarely ends with a single Event.

Generated Evidence often becomes the Presentation stage for subsequent commercial interactions.

Commerce therefore continues through:

- completion
- further processing
- recovery
- escalation
- recursion
- termination

No special ontology is required as scale increases.
