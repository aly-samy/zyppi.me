# Purpose

This document summarizes the findings from **Mission CA-1C — Friction & Failure Observation**.

The mission intentionally observed situations where commerce **did not proceed normally**, without diagnosing causes or proposing solutions.

Its purpose was to understand **how commerce behaves when interactions fail**, providing additional observational evidence before constitutional reduction begins.

No ontology, architecture, or constitutional modeling was performed.

---

# Mission Scope

The council observed approximately **100 failure scenarios** spanning numerous industries and environments, including:

- Retail
- Banking
- Logistics
- Manufacturing
- Healthcare
- Aviation
- Customs
- Government
- Warehousing
- Digital Services
- Cloud Infrastructure
- Security
- Transportation
- Hospitality
- Construction
- Telecommunications

The observations remained strictly descriptive.

---

# Primary Findings

## 1. Commerce Frequently Stops at Decision Points

Most failures occurred immediately before an expected interaction could continue.

Examples included:

- payment declined
- shipment rejected
- boarding denied
- authentication failed
- customs hold
- contract rejected
- inspection failed

Observation:

Commerce frequently pauses at identifiable decision points rather than failing continuously.

---

## 2. Failures Produce Additional Evidence

Successful interactions generate evidence.

Failed interactions also generate evidence.

Observed examples included:

- rejection notices
- audit logs
- error messages
- exception reports
- inspection reports
- dispute records
- denial records
- warning alerts

Observation:

Failure often increases the amount of evidence created.

---

## 3. Failure Produces Observable State Changes

Even unsuccessful interactions change reality.

Examples observed:

- request pending
- request denied
- shipment held
- account locked
- order cancelled
- payment reversed
- access revoked
- inventory quarantined

Observation:

Failure is itself an observable event.

---

## 4. Human Responses Follow Failure

After failures, humans consistently performed additional actions.

Examples observed:

- waiting
- calling support
- resubmitting documents
- signing corrections
- providing identification
- escalating disputes
- cancelling transactions
- restarting procedures

Observation:

Failure creates secondary interaction sequences.

---

## 5. Systems Respond Before Humans

Many environments demonstrated automatic responses immediately after failure.

Examples included:

- account lockout
- fraud alerts
- transaction rollback
- retry mechanisms
- queue reassignment
- notification generation
- alarm activation

Observation:

Automated systems frequently react before human intervention occurs.

---

## 6. Failure Rarely Ends the Process

Across nearly every observed environment, failure initiated a new sequence rather than terminating activity.

Typical outcomes included:

- retry
- correction
- verification
- escalation
- appeal
- substitution
- cancellation
- reversal

Observation:

Commerce demonstrates recovery behavior following interruption.

---

# Repeating Observational Themes

Across industries, recurring patterns included:

- interactions being paused
- interactions being rejected
- interactions being delayed
- interactions being reversed
- interactions being corrected
- interactions being escalated
- interactions being retried

These patterns appeared regardless of industry or technology.

---

# Constitutional Significance

The workshop intentionally avoided constitutional interpretation.

However, these observations establish that:

- failure is observable
- interruption is observable
- recovery is observable
- correction is observable
- reversal is observable

These phenomena require constitutional reduction during **CA-2**, rather than being assumed as primitives.

---

# Deferred Questions

The following questions remain intentionally unanswered:

- Is failure a primitive?
- Is rejection an Event?
- Is dispute a Category?
- Is approval a State?
- Is recovery a composition?
- Is authorization a relationship?
- Are policies responsible for observed interruptions?

These questions are reserved exclusively for **CA-2 — Reduction Workshop**.

# Outcome

Mission **CA-1C** successfully expanded the Commerce Discovery dataset by documenting how commerce behaves under interruption and failure.

The workshop confirmed that abnormal interactions produce consistent observable behavior across diverse domains while remaining free from architectural interpretation.

These observations now become part of the empirical corpus supporting the constitutional reduction process.
