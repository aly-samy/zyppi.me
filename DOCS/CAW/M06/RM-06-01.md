### 3.2 Modulo-10 check-digit algorithm — §7.9.1 (printed page 544)

**Exact normative location:** `GS1 General Specifications 26.0, Section 7.9 "Check digit/character calculations", subsection 7.9.1 "Standard check digit calculations for GS1 data structures", Table 7-8; worked example Table 7-9 (page 545).`

**Normative wording (extracted):**

> "This algorithm is identical for all fixed length numeric GS1 data structures (including GDTI, GLN, GRAI, etc.) that require a check digit."

Table 7-8 "Check digit algorithm" (extracted):

- Digit-position rows are defined for GTIN-8 (N1–N8), GTIN-12 (N1–N12), GTIN-13 (N1–N13), GTIN-14 (N1–N14), 17-digit, and 18-digit structures; the check digit occupies the final position.
- Instruction rows: **"Multiply value of each position by"** — alternating `x3 x1 x3 x1 x3 x1 …`; **"Accumulated results = sum"**; **"Subtract sum from nearest equal or higher multiple of ten = check digit"**.

**Ordered calculation procedure (paraphrase — AGENT ANALYSIS derived from Table 7-8/7-9):**

1. Write the key without its check digit; label positions N1…Nn from left to right.
2. Multiply each data digit by the alternating factors 3 and 1.
3. Sum all products.
4. Check digit = (nearest equal-or-higher multiple of 10) − sum; equivalently `(10 − (sum mod 10)) mod 10`.

**Normative worked example (Table 7-9, 18-digit structure) [DIRECT NORMATIVE EVIDENCE]:**
Data `3 7 6 1 0 4 2 5 0 0 2 1 2 3 4 5 6` → Step 1 multipliers `3 1 3 1 3 1 3 1 3 1 3 1 3 1 3 1 3` (left to right, N1 first) → products sum to **101** → Step 3: `110 − 101 = check digit 9` → result `376104250021234569`. Arithmetic independently recomputed and confirmed during this research.

**Cross-check — GS1 UK official guidance [E-05]:** five-step description (sum alternate digits starting from the right-hand digit → ×3 → add remaining digits → check digit is the smallest number reaching a multiple of ten) with worked example `501234567000C → C = 3 → 5012345670003`. Arithmetic recomputed and confirmed.

**⚠ Interpretation caveat (flagged, not silently resolved):** Text extraction cannot fully resolve the column alignment of Table 7-8's shared `x3 x1 …` multiplier row for structures with an **even** number of data digits (notably GTIN-13 and the 17-digit structure). Read left-anchored, the GTIN-13 row would assign ×3 to N1; read right-anchored (rightmost data digit receives ×3), it assigns ×3 to N12. **Both official worked examples (Table 7-9; GS1 UK) are consistent only with the right-anchored reading** (the GS1 UK GTIN-13 example yields check digit 3 under right-anchoring and 5 under left-anchoring). Classification of this note: **AGENT ANALYSIS** built on DIRECT OFFICIAL EVIDENCE. Recommended: the Council treat "the rightmost data digit (immediately left of the check digit) receives factor 3, alternating leftward" as the operative rule, and confirm it against GS1's reference implementations (GS1 Barcode Syntax Engine; GS1 check-digit calculator) before M06 validation design is frozen.

Also retrieved: §7.9.2–7.9.4 (price/weight check digits — not relevant to M06 profile) and §7.9.5 (check-character pair for alphanumeric keys, MOD 1021/32 — GMN-related, not in M06 profile).

### 3.3 AI 17 expiration-date semantics — §3.4.7 (printed pages 213–214)

**Exact normative location:** `GS1 General Specifications 26.0, Section 3.4.7 "Expiration date: AI (17)"; Section 7.12 "Determination of century in dates" (page 554).`

**Normative findings (all DIRECT NORMATIVE EVIDENCE, extracted verbatim):**

1. **Format:** Structure is Year (tens and units, mandatory) · Month (number, mandatory) · Day ("the number of the day of the relevant month"). Element-string layout `1 7 N1 N2 N3 N4 N5 N6` (AI + 6 digits, YYMMDD).
2. **Day `00` — permitted and normatively interpreted:** "if it is not necessary to specify the day, the field must be filled with two zeroes." And: **"When it is not necessary to specify the day (the day field is filled with two zeroes), the resultant data string SHALL be interpreted as the last day of the noted month including any adjustment for leap years (e.g., '130200' is '2013 February 28', '160200' is '2016 February 29', etc.)."**
3. **Regulated-healthcare exception (effective 1 January 2025):** "How the day of the month is expressed for regulated healthcare products will change starting 1 January 2025. As of that date the day of the month SHALL NOT be expressed as two zeroes. A valid day of the month (e.g., last day of July = 31) SHALL be included." (This confirms and supersedes-in-detail the earlier GSCN 21-040 finding recorded during CCR-06-01 research.)
4. **Year window:** "This element string can only specify dates ranging from 49 years in the past to 50 years in the future of the current year. Determination of the correct century is explained in section 7.12."
5. **Century determination (§7.12):** the normative text establishes the AI list and the 49-past/50-future window and refers to **Figure 7-17 "Determination of century"**, which is a **graphic figure** — its procedural content could not be machine-extracted from the PDF. Classification: **NOT EXPLICITLY ESTABLISHED IN THE RETRIEVED MACHINE-READABLE TEXT** (figure-only). This is the one RG-A item that remains without fully quotable text.

**Explicit classifications requested by the mandate:**

| Question                                   | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Class                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Is day `00` permitted?                     | Yes — conditionally: permitted in general use; normatively interpreted as last day of month incl. leap-year adjustment; **prohibited for regulated healthcare products since 2025-01-01**                                 | DIRECT NORMATIVE                                                               |
| Does GS1 require full calendar validation? | No explicit SHALL requiring per-month day-limit validation at the syntactic level was found in the retrieved normative text. The semantic interpretation rule (day 00 → last day of month incl. leap years) is normative. | Structural absence: NOT EXPLICITLY ESTABLISHED; semantic rule DIRECT NORMATIVE |
| Are month-specific day limits normative?   | Not stated as a validation SHALL in extracted text.                                                                                                                                                                       | NOT EXPLICITLY ESTABLISHED                                                     |
| Is leap-year behavior normative?           | Yes — via the day-`00` SHALL interpretation ("including any adjustment for leap years", with 2016-02-29 example).                                                                                                         | DIRECT NORMATIVE                                                               |
| Sector-specific exception?                 | Yes — regulated healthcare, effective 2025-01-01 (above).                                                                                                                                                                 | DIRECT NORMATIVE                                                               |

**Corroboration from the official AI registry [E-02]:** AI 17 component format token is `"yymmd0"` (the `d0` variant is used by all six standard date AIs 11/12/13/15/16/17 in both the registry dataset and the GS1 Barcode Syntax Dictionary), and the registry regex `(\d{2}(?:0\d|1[0-2])(?:[0-2]\d|3[01]))` permits month 01–12 and day 00–31 — i.e., the machine-readable syntax permits day `00` and does not encode per-month limits. This is consistent with the normative text above. [DIRECT OFFICIAL EVIDENCE]
