2. Evidence Review Standard

The Council applies the following evidence hierarchy:

1. Direct evidence from the pinned GS1 standards edition;
2. Direct retrieval of the authoritative GS1 machine-readable registry;
3. Cryptographically anchored archival evidence;
4. Official GS1 tooling and licensing documentation;
5. Secondary official GS1 pages;
6. Search-indexed or historical material, used only as contextual evidence where direct authority is unavailable.

A research gate may be closed only when the evidence required by its mandate is present, attributable, and sufficiently reproducible.

A claim made in an agent summary is not itself treated as evidence. The underlying source extract, retrieval record, payload, hash, or licensing text remains the authoritative evidence object.

3. Consolidated Research Assessment

3.1 RG-A — GS1 Algorithm and AI "17" Constraint Extraction

Research Question

What are the exact normative rules governing:

1. the GS1 modulo-10 check-digit algorithm; and
2. AI "17" expiration-date semantics, including calendar behavior, leap-year treatment, and day "00"?

Consolidated Findings

The submitted research establishes the following direction:

- The GS1 modulo-10 algorithm is the governing check-digit method for GTIN validation.
- The implementation must preserve the correct right-anchored multiplier alignment when applying alternating factors.
- AI "17" is represented as "YYMMDD".
- The compact AI registry definition does not necessarily contain every operational or interpretive rule governing the encoded date.
- Historical GS1 General Specifications material indicates that day "00" may carry month-end semantics, including leap-year adjustment.
- Historical or secondary evidence alone is insufficient to establish that behavior for the pinned GS1 General Specifications Release 26.0 baseline.

The Council therefore distinguishes:

«Structural representation identifies the serialized form of the AI value.»

from:

«Normative semantic interpretation determines how special values, including day "00", are treated under the pinned standards edition.»

These are related but not interchangeable.

Council Finding

The Council accepts the research conclusion that AI "17" must not be reduced to a generic Gregorian six-digit date without first applying the applicable GS1 semantics.

However, the Council does not adopt a generic “strict Gregorian validation” rule, and does not treat "YYMM00" as invalid merely because day "00" is not a conventional Gregorian calendar day.

The applicable behavior must be determined from the pinned GS1 Release 26.0 authority.

RG-A Disposition

"RG-A" is CONDITIONALLY CLOSED, subject to the following evidence condition:

The RM-06-01 evidence package must contain and preserve:

1. the exact Release 26.0 source location for the modulo-10 algorithm;
2. the exact Release 26.0 source location governing AI "17" date semantics;
3. the relevant quoted or faithfully extracted normative text;
4. sufficient source metadata to establish that the evidence derives from the pinned Release 26.0 baseline rather than an earlier edition.

If the evidence package does not contain direct Release 26.0 support for the AI "17" day-"00" rule, that sub-finding remains open and must not be inferred from historical editions.

Required Closure Annotation

The following implementation-direction statement is ratified:

«The GS1 modulo-10 implementation shall use the right-anchored multiplier alignment established by the pinned GS1 authority. AI "17" shall be interpreted according to the complete semantics of the pinned GS1 Release 26.0 authority and shall not be subjected to an independently invented generic-calendar rule.»

This statement is a standards-derived M06 direction. It is not a "POL-001" amendment.

3.2 RG-B — GS1 AI Registry Archival and Verification

Research Question

Can the GS1 Application Identifier registry be retrieved and anchored as a reproducible evidence artifact, with direct verification of AI "01", "10", "17", and "21"?

Consolidated Findings

The research submissions establish that:

- the GS1 AI registry is a live, externally maintained authority;
- URL reference alone is insufficient to create a deterministic, immutable Zyppi evidence baseline;
- a dated payload capture and cryptographic integrity record are required;
- the previous retrieval anomaly appears related to a retrieval-path or endpoint-form difference, including the reported trailing-slash behavior, rather than evidence that the AI definitions are absent;
- the required AI entries are:

AI| Meaning
"01"| GTIN
"10"| Batch/Lot Number
"17"| Expiration Date
"21"| Serial Number

Required Evidence Record

The authoritative RM-06-01 archival package must contain:

- exact retrieval URL;
- UTC retrieval timestamp;
- HTTP retrieval status;
- captured payload or immutable archival reference;
- payload size or equivalent retrieval metadata;
- SHA-256 hash calculated over the captured raw payload;
- direct verification evidence for AI "01", "10", "17", and "21";
- a record of the retrieval-path anomaly and its resolution.

Council Finding

The Council accepts the architectural conclusion that the live GS1 endpoint must not be treated as a sufficient version pin by URL alone.

The captured payload and its SHA-256 hash are the reproducibility anchor for the M06 evidence baseline. The live registry remains the external authority, while the archived, hashed payload provides Zyppi with a deterministic record of the authority consulted at the time of ratification.

RG-B Disposition

"RG-B" is CONDITIONALLY CLOSED, subject to verification that the complete archival record listed above exists in the RM-06-01 evidence package.

The reported trailing-slash retrieval behavior is classified as:

"RETRIEVAL-PATH FINDING — NON-NORMATIVE"

It does not alter the meaning of the GS1 standards and does not constitute a defect in the AI registry.

3.3 RG-C — GS1 Conformance-Material Licensing

Research Question

Do the applicable licenses permit Zyppi to import GS1 Barcode Syntax Resource materials and GS1-Conformant Resolver Test Suite assets directly into Zyppi repositories?

Consolidated Findings

The evidence presented supports the following limited conclusions:

- The GS1 Barcode Syntax Resource includes tooling and implementation assets intended to assist software implementations.
- The BSR user documentation identifies Apache License 2.0 as applicable to relevant BSR software materials.
- The BSR is implementation support and does not replace the normative authority of the GS1 standards.
- The evidence reviewed does not establish complete asset-level licensing and redistribution rights for a distinct GS1-Conformant Resolver Test Suite.
- The evidence reviewed does not establish that every external test fixture, embedded text excerpt, dataset, or associated asset may be copied into a Zyppi repository under the same terms.

Council Finding

The Council rejects any blanket conclusion that all GS1 conformance materials are freely importable merely because Apache License 2.0 applies to identified BSR software materials.

Licensing must be established for the specific asset proposed for import, including any applicable notices, attribution requirements, copyright terms, and redistribution restrictions.

RG-C Disposition

"RG-C" is PARTIALLY CLOSED.

The following restriction is ratified:

«No GS1 Barcode Syntax Resource fixture, GS1-Conformant Resolver Test Suite fixture, external test dataset, normative text excerpt, or associated asset shall be copied into a Zyppi repository unless the exact asset-level license and applicable notices have been verified and recorded.»

Until that verification occurs:

- external tools may be used for read-only comparison or external conformance evaluation where permitted;
- Zyppi may create original, native test cases derived from independently established rules;
- Zyppi shall not represent original tests as official GS1 test-suite assets;
- no external fixture import is authorized.

Clean-Room Test Direction

Where implementation testing is required before external fixture rights are resolved, Zyppi shall create native tests from:

1. the pinned standards requirements;
2. the archived AI registry evidence;
3. independently derived input/output cases;
4. original Zyppi test descriptions.

The purpose is to preserve evidence traceability without importing unverified third-party material.

4. AI "17" Authority-Layer Clarification

The Council records the following constitutional clarification.

"POL-001" is an ecosystem-wide policy authority. It governs the general policy framework for Zyppi as a whole and is not a GS1-specific rules container.

Therefore:

- "POL-001" does not itself decide whether AI "17" day "00" is accepted, rejected, or interpreted as month-end.
- The GS1 standard determines the external standards semantics.
- "CCR-06-01" and the M06 support profile determine the Zyppi adoption scope.
- "M06-PLAN" will specify the resulting implementation behavior after all required evidence and reconciliation gates are closed.
- Any future execution-context policy must use the general POL framework without converting POL into a GS1-specific authority.

The Council rejects the unsupported assumption that a generic Policy Engine must automatically override a valid GS1 representation with stricter Gregorian validation.

No such override is adopted by this record.
