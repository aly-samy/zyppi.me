# G-0813 — Execution Budget Semantics

## Council Decision Record

**Document ID:** `G-0813` **Gate:** `G-0813 — Execution Budget Semantics` **Program:** `CAW-011 — Commerce Atlas Wedge` **Milestone:** `M08 — Runtime Verification Pipeline` **Status:** `CLOSED — DIRECTION A RATIFIED` **Decision Date:** `2026-08-08` **Authority:** `Zyppi Constitutional Council` **Implementation Authority:** `NONE IMPLIED BY THIS RECORD`

# 1. Constitutional Question

What does one unit of Runtime execution budget represent, and how shall that budget be consumed, exhausted, diagnosed, replayed, and represented within deterministic constitutional execution?

The current Runtime contract contains:
`budget: number `
but the semantic meaning of decrementing that value remained unresolved. The M08 GDR identifies four principal directions: Resolution-Step Budget, Weighted Gas Model, Fixed Maximum Evaluation Depth, and Budget Deferred.

This gate therefore determines the **constitutional semantics of execution budget**, not its implementation mechanism.

# 2. Selected Direction

## Direction A — Resolution-Step Budget

**RATIFIED.**

One unit of execution budget represents **one constitutionally defined resolution step**.

A resolution step is a discrete unit of constitutional resolution work whose meaning is defined by the Runtime's constitutional execution model rather than by:

- CPU time;

- wall-clock duration;

- memory consumption;

- implementation function calls;

- loop iterations;

- programming-language constructs;

- hardware characteristics;

- infrastructure performance.

The budget is therefore a **deterministic structural bound on constitutional resolution work**.

The exact catalogue of operations that constitute resolution steps SHALL be established by the relevant Runtime and Policy Semantics decisions, particularly G-0807, and SHALL NOT be invented by the implementation agent.

# 3. Rejected Directions

## 3.1 Direction B — Weighted Gas Model

**REJECTED.**

Different constitutional operations SHALL NOT receive arbitrary or governance-defined numerical prices merely because they are computationally different.

The GDR identifies weighted gas as more expressive but also notes its calibration burden, governance implications, artificial economics, and risk of coupling the model to the current implementation.

Zyppi's constitutional budget is not an economic accounting mechanism and shall not become one.

Computationally expensive operations may be operationally measured elsewhere. Such measurements SHALL NOT redefine constitutional budget consumption.

## 3.2 Direction C — Fixed Maximum Evaluation Depth

**REJECTED as the primary budget model.**

Depth is insufficient to represent general constitutional resolution volume.

A shallow operation may involve substantial constitutional work, while a deeper traversal may involve comparatively little. Depth may therefore remain an independent structural safety constraint where required, but it does not constitute the universal meaning of `budget`.

## 3.3 Direction D — Budget Deferred

**REJECTED.**

The existing `budget` field cannot remain semantically undefined while being carried through the Runtime contract.

Deferral would leave a constitutional execution-control field without a defined meaning and would weaken the fail-closed protection intended by the M08 Runtime architecture. The GDR explicitly identifies this as the principal weakness of Direction D.

# 4. Constitutional Definition

The Council establishes:

**A resolution step is one discrete, constitutionally meaningful advancement of the Runtime's resolution process that has been designated by the governing Runtime and Policy Semantics model as budget-consuming work.**

The definition SHALL be independent of implementation structure.

Therefore:

- refactoring a function SHALL NOT change budget consumption;

- replacing a loop with recursion SHALL NOT change budget consumption;

- replacing TypeScript with another implementation language SHALL NOT change budget consumption;

- changing CPU architecture SHALL NOT change budget consumption;

- optimizing an implementation SHALL NOT change the constitutional cost of the same resolution trace.

The constitutional budget measures **resolution semantics**, not computational effort.

# 5. Budget Input Semantics

The initial `budget` value is explicit execution input.

It SHALL NOT be obtained from:

- ambient system state;

- Runtime wall-clock observation;

- CPU characteristics;

- memory availability;

- infrastructure configuration;

- process state;

- network state;

- persistence;

- implicit Runtime defaults.

The Runtime SHALL treat the supplied budget as authoritative input subject to constitutional validation.

No implementation may silently substitute a different budget.

# 6. Consumption Semantics

Budget SHALL be consumed when a constitutionally defined resolution step is admitted for execution.

The Runtime SHALL establish that sufficient remaining budget exists **before initiating the governed step**.

The conceptual transition is:
`remainingBudget > 0         │         ▼ authorize resolution step         │         ▼ consume one budget unit         │         ▼ perform the governed resolution operation `
The exact association between a particular operation and a resolution step SHALL be defined by the applicable Runtime/Policy semantics.

A programming-language operation is not automatically a constitutional step merely because it executes.

# 7. Failed and Rejected Operations

A resolution step that is constitutionally initiated and produces a defined failure or rejection SHALL consume its allocated resolution step.

This includes, where the applicable semantics designate them as resolution steps:

- dependency rejection;

- compatibility rejection;

- policy rejection;

- evidence or verification failure;

- other deterministic constitutional failures encountered during resolution.

The purpose is replay stability.

A failure SHALL NOT retroactively restore budget merely because the resulting decision is unsuccessful.

However, an operation that is rejected **before it becomes an admitted resolution step** SHALL NOT consume a resolution-step unit.

This preserves the distinction between:

1. **failure during constitutional resolution**, and

2. **failure to admit a resolution step in the first place**.

# 8. Budget Exhaustion

If the next constitutionally required resolution step cannot be admitted because no sufficient budget remains:

**execution SHALL terminate deterministically.**

The Runtime SHALL:

1. stop before initiating the unbudgeted step;

2. preserve all previously committed constitutional results;

3. prevent the unadmitted step from modifying the resulting constitutional state;

4. produce a deterministic budget-exhaustion outcome;

5. produce the corresponding diagnostic information;

6. produce an execution receipt where receipt semantics permit constitutional failure outcomes to be materialized.

Budget exhaustion SHALL NOT be represented as:

- an infrastructure crash;

- an unclassified exception;

- an implicit timeout;

- an allow;

- a success;

- a trusted result;

- a valid result.

It is a defined constitutional execution outcome.

# 9. Zero-Budget Semantics

`budget = 0` is **valid input to the ExecutionContext**, subject to the general validation rules established by the ExecutionContext gate.

A zero-budget execution SHALL NOT be treated as malformed merely because its budget is zero.

Instead:

If execution requires a resolution step and the available budget is zero, the Runtime SHALL deterministically terminate with budget exhaustion before initiating that step.

This preserves the distinction between:

- **invalid execution input**, and

- **valid execution with insufficient constitutional resolution budget**.

A negative budget SHALL be invalid input and SHALL fail validation.

Budget SHALL be represented as an integral non-negative value.

Fractional budget values SHALL NOT be interpreted by rounding, truncation, coercion, or other implicit conversion.

# 10. Pipeline Scope

Budget semantics apply to constitutional work performed within the M04 Runtime pipeline according to the resolution-step definitions established by the governing Runtime semantics.

The nine constitutional stages remain:

1. Admission

2. Bundle Discovery

3. Bundle Verification

4. Dependency Resolution

5. Compatibility Validation

6. ACV Activation

7. Resolution Graph Construction

8. Active Execution

9. Receipt Generation

The existence of these stages does **not** imply that every stage consumes one or more budget units.

In particular:

- Admission SHALL validate the existence and validity of the budget itself.

- Receipt materialization SHALL NOT consume an arbitrary budget unit merely because serialization occurs.

- Specific budget-consuming operations within the pipeline SHALL be established by the applicable constitutional execution semantics.

G-0813 therefore establishes the **budget framework**, while G-0807 and the relevant Runtime stage semantics establish which operations constitute resolution steps.

This prevents G-0813 from silently redefining Policy Semantics.

# 11. Determinism and Replay

Budget consumption SHALL be deterministic.

For identical:

- explicit execution inputs;

- authorized constitutional context;

- governing policy semantics;

- governing Runtime semantics;

- relevant constitutional corpus;

the Runtime SHALL produce the same resolution-step consumption trace.

Therefore:
`Execution A ≡ Execution B         ↓ Resolution Trace A ≡ Resolution Trace B         ↓ Budget Consumption A ≡ Budget Consumption B `
Hardware speed, process scheduling, implementation language, optimization level, and execution duration SHALL NOT alter constitutional budget consumption.

This follows the M08 constitutional requirement that identical explicit inputs and authorized context produce equivalent Runtime output.

# 12. Budget Semantics Versioning

The semantics governing what constitutes a resolution step SHALL themselves be version-bound where required for replay stability.

A future amendment that changes the definition of a resolution step SHALL NOT silently reinterpret historical executions.

Any such change SHALL be governed through the applicable constitutional versioning mechanism and incorporated into the execution's authorized constitutional context where required.

G-0813 does **not** independently prescribe the final representation of this version identifier.

That representation remains subject to the relevant ExecutionContext, policy-version, and hash-domain decisions.

# 13. Hash-Domain Consequences

The initial budget is explicit execution input and therefore belongs to the constitutional input domain according to the final hash-domain rules.

The fact that budget was consumed during execution is derived execution information.

The final representation of:

- initial budget;

- consumed budget;

- remaining budget;

- resolution-step trace;

within cryptographic hash domains SHALL be determined by **G-0809 — Hash Domains**.

G-0813 establishes the semantic status of these values but does not prescribe their final byte-level serialization or hash-domain placement.

No implementation may infer the final hash construction from this gate alone.

# 14. Diagnostics

Budget exhaustion SHALL be diagnostically distinguishable from unrelated Runtime failure.

At minimum, the diagnostic model SHALL be capable of establishing:

1. that budget exhaustion occurred;

2. the initial authorized budget;

3. the remaining budget at exhaustion;

4. the resolution boundary at which the next step could not be admitted;

5. sufficient causal provenance to establish why the Runtime reached that boundary.

The final representation, size limits, and diagnostic schema SHALL be governed by **G-0817 — Diagnostics**.

G-0813 therefore defines the diagnostic requirement without prematurely defining the complete diagnostic payload.

# 15. Trust Result Consequence

Budget exhaustion SHALL be represented as a deterministic execution outcome and SHALL NOT be collapsed into a generic infrastructure error.

The exact canonical `TrustResult` value or taxonomy member is deferred to **G-0806 — TrustResult**.

G-0813 therefore establishes:

**Budget exhaustion is a constitutional outcome requiring explicit representation.**

G-0806 determines its final taxonomy and trust semantics.

# 16. Receipt Consequence

A budget-exhausted execution SHALL remain auditable.

Where the Runtime has entered constitutional execution and reached deterministic budget exhaustion, the resulting outcome SHALL be capable of being materialized into the execution evidence/receipt model.

The exact receipt field representation belongs to:

- G-0804 — Receipt Field Set;

- G-0816 — `decisionSummary`;

- G-0817 — Diagnostics;

- G-0809 — Hash Domains.

G-0813 does not independently define the final receipt schema.

# 17. Constitutional Invariants

The following invariants are ratified as part of G-0813.

### B-0813-01 — Deterministic Consumption

For identical authorized execution conditions, budget consumption SHALL be identical.

### B-0813-02 — Explicit Budget

The initial budget SHALL enter execution explicitly and SHALL NOT be derived from ambient Runtime state.

### B-0813-03 — Semantic Independence

Budget units SHALL represent constitutional resolution work, not computational duration or hardware effort.

### B-0813-04 — Pre-Consumption Admission

The Runtime SHALL establish budget availability before initiating a budget-consuming resolution step.

### B-0813-05 — Deterministic Exhaustion

Insufficient budget SHALL terminate the affected execution path deterministically and fail closed.

### B-0813-06 — No Silent Recovery

The Runtime SHALL NOT silently replenish, borrow, reset, round, or otherwise alter budget to continue execution after exhaustion.

### B-0813-07 — Failure Consumption

An admitted resolution step that deterministically produces rejection or failure SHALL consume its designated resolution-step unit.

### B-0813-08 — Implementation Independence

Implementation refactoring SHALL NOT change budget consumption for an equivalent constitutional resolution trace.

### B-0813-09 — No Artificial Economics

The Runtime SHALL NOT introduce arbitrary computational prices or economic weighting into constitutional budget semantics.

### B-0813-10 — Historical Stability

Changes to resolution-step semantics SHALL NOT silently reinterpret previously governed executions.

# 20. Dependency Boundary

G-0813 depends semantically on the definitions established by the Runtime and Policy Semantics framework.

The dependency relationship is:
`G-0807 — Policy Semantics           │           ▼ G-0813 — Execution Budget Semantics           │           ├──────────► G-0806 — TrustResult           │           ├──────────► G-0809 — Hash Domains           │           ├──────────► G-0816 — decisionSummary           │           └──────────► G-0817 — Diagnostics `
G-0813 does **not** authorize G-0807 to be inferred from budget semantics, nor does it authorize G-0809 to be implemented before the hash-domain decision is recorded.

The existing M08 dependency framework explicitly places G-0807 before G-0813 and identifies the relationship as:

`G-0807 → G-0813 → G-0817`.

# 21. Implementation Boundary

This decision grants **no implementation authority**.

Jules SHALL NOT:

- invent a resolution-step catalogue;

- assign arbitrary weights to operations;

- define budget consumption from function calls;

- define budget consumption from CPU time;

- define a gas schedule;

- silently redefine zero-budget behavior;

- decide which policy operations constitute resolution steps where that decision belongs to G-0807;

- finalize hash inclusion;

- finalize receipt fields;

- invent a TrustResult taxonomy.

Implementation may proceed only after the required downstream contracts and semantic dependencies have been reconciled.

# 22. Primary Advantages

The selected Direction A provides:

1. **Determinism** — identical constitutional executions consume identical budget.

2. **Replayability** — budget behavior remains reproducible.

3. **Portability** — semantics do not depend on CPU or infrastructure.

4. **Architectural stability** — implementation refactoring does not redefine constitutional cost.

5. **Clear separation of concerns** — constitutional complexity is separated from operational performance.

6. **Fail-closed execution** — unbounded resolution cannot silently continue.

7. **Governance simplicity** — no artificial gas-pricing schedule is introduced.

# 23. Accepted Costs and Risks

The Council accepts the following costs:

### 23.1 Exact Step Definition

The Runtime must maintain a precise definition of what constitutes a resolution step.

### 23.2 Policy Coordination

The specific operation catalogue cannot be finalized entirely within G-0813 and must remain coordinated with G-0807.

### 23.3 Complex Resolution Traces

Complex constitutional requests may legitimately consume large numbers of resolution steps.

### 23.4 Versioning

Changes to the semantic definition of a resolution step may require explicit version governance to preserve historical replayability.

### 23.5 Diagnostic Attribution

Budget exhaustion requires sufficient causal information to establish where and why the execution terminated.

These are accepted architectural costs of preserving deterministic constitutional execution.

# 26. Final Council Decision

**G-0813 — EXECUTION BUDGET SEMANTICS**

**Selected Direction:** **Direction A — Resolution-Step Budget, refined**

**Decision:**

Zyppi Runtime execution budget SHALL represent a deterministic bound on constitutionally meaningful resolution work. One unit SHALL represent one constitutionally defined resolution step. Resolution-step semantics SHALL be independent of hardware, execution duration, implementation language, and implementation mechanics. Budget consumption SHALL be deterministic, replay-stable, explicitly supplied, and fail-closed upon exhaustion.

- Direction B — Weighted Gas Model

- Direction C — Fixed Maximum Evaluation Depth as primary budget semantics

- Direction D — Budget Deferred
