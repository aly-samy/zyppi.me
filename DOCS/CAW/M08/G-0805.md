## 1. Purpose

`G-0805` establishes the authoritative-corpus and conflict-resolution discipline under which the remaining M08 Council Gates shall be investigated, decided, recorded, and implemented.

The purpose of this gate is **not** to define the semantics of the other M08 Gates.

It establishes:

1. which categories of authority may govern M08;

2. how existing Zyppi authority and lifecycle rules apply to M08 artifacts;

3. how conflicts between authoritative materials are resolved;

4. how supersession and version progression are recognized;

5. how implementation-critical authority is preserved for reproducibility;

6. how external standards acquire authority within Zyppi;

7. how draft, legacy, reconnaissance, and worked-example material is treated;

8. how an M08 AMS obtains a reproducible authority context.

This record does not amend the Constitution, CEngS, CAW, or any higher authority.

# 2. Constitutional Principle

M08 shall not create a parallel constitutional authority hierarchy.

The existing Zyppi authority and lifecycle framework remains controlling.

In particular, M08 shall apply the authority-loading and context discipline established by the applicable CEngS framework, including the authority ordering defined by `CEngS-003 §5`, rather than creating an independent M08 hierarchy.

M08-specific documents shall therefore be **mapped into the existing authority framework**, not used to replace it.

# 4. Existing Authority Framework — No Parallel Hierarchy

The Council considered whether M08 should establish a new seven-tier authority hierarchy.

The Council rejects that approach as a constitutional framework.

A separate M08 hierarchy would create a second governance mechanism that could conflict with the existing Zyppi authority model.

Instead:

**M08 shall map its artifacts into the existing Zyppi authority and lifecycle framework.**

For operational clarity, M08 may use classifications such as:

- constitutional;

- engineering standard;

- domain authority;

- program authority;

- milestone decision;

- implementation mandate;

- reference/evidence.

These classifications are descriptive and operational.

They do not constitute a new constitutional hierarchy.

# 5. Conflict Resolution

When two materials appear to conflict, the following procedure shall apply.

### 5.1 Authority-status test

First determine the formal authority status of each material under the existing Zyppi governance framework.

A lower-authority document cannot override a higher-authority document merely because it is:

- newer;

- more detailed;

- more specific;

- easier to implement;

- closer to source code.

### 5.2 Explicit supersession test

Where materials possess the same relevant authority level, determine whether a formal supersession relationship exists.

Supersession shall follow the applicable Zyppi lifecycle rules.

A document does not supersede another merely because it has:

- a later date;

- a higher-looking version number;

- a newer filename;

- a newer repository modification timestamp.

### 5.3 No implied supersession

No implementation agent or Council member may infer that an older authority has been superseded solely from recency.

Formal supersession, lifecycle progression, or an explicitly authorized replacement mechanism is required.

### 5.4 Specificity

Specificity may assist interpretation within the scope of an authority, but specificity does not permit a lower-authority document to violate a higher-order constitutional invariant.

# 6. Recency Is Not an Authority Rule

The Council explicitly rejects a general:

"latest document wins"

rule.

Recency may be relevant evidence when investigating the current state of the corpus.

It is not, by itself, a supersession mechanism.

The controlling question is:

**What is the formally authoritative version under the Zyppi lifecycle?**

not:

**Which document appears newest?**

# 7. Worked Examples

The Council confirms that worked examples are illustrative.

A worked example shall not create constitutional meaning merely by appearing in:

- a CAW document;

- a milestone plan;

- an implementation specification;

- a reconnaissance report;

- a test description.

Where a worked example conflicts with normative text, the normative text prevails.

Accordingly, CAW-012 examples such as:

- `10,000` replay executions;

- execution-duration examples;

- example receipt fields;

- example acceptance measurements;

shall not be interpreted as M08 acceptance requirements unless independently authorized through the applicable governing mechanism.

Any conflict between an example and an authoritative requirement shall be recorded as a documentation defect or synchronization issue.

# 8. Draft, Proposed, Legacy, and Superseded Material

Draft or proposed documents may be used by the Council as:

- research material;

- historical context;

- architectural intent;

- evidence for identifying unresolved questions.

They shall not automatically become implementation authority.

In particular, Council-supplied ZRM, Foundations, SIOS, or other proposed material shall remain reference material unless its formal lifecycle status grants it authority applicable to M08.

Similarly, superseded material shall not regain authority merely because it contains useful detail.

Historical material may be retained for traceability without becoming operative authority.

# 9. Reconnaissance and Investigation Reports

Reconnaissance and investigation documents are evidence.

They may establish facts such as:

- what currently exists in the repository;

- which contracts are present;

- which stages are stubbed;

- which dependencies currently exist;

- which discrepancies were observed;

- which external standards were researched.

They do not, by themselves:

- amend constitutional rules;

- redefine contracts;

- close Council Gates;

- authorize implementation;

- supersede CAW tasks.

A Council Decision Record may use reconnaissance as evidence when making an authorized decision.

# 10. External Standards

An external standard does not become Zyppi constitutional authority merely because:

- it is authoritative in its own ecosystem;

- it is widely adopted;

- it is technically superior;

- a library implements it;

- an AI agent recommends it.

External standards must be explicitly adopted, referenced, or pinned through the applicable Zyppi governance mechanism before they may govern M08 implementation.

For implementation-critical external standards, the adopted authority must identify the relevant:

- standard;

- edition/version;

- publication or effective state;

- applicable scope;

- canonical source;

- snapshot or reproducibility mechanism where required.

This principle applies particularly to cryptographic and canonicalization standards.

# 11. Implementation-Critical Version Discipline

The Council adopts a **formal-pinning principle for implementation-critical authority**.

An issued AMS must have a reproducible authority context.

At minimum, the AMS shall identify the exact versions or ratified decision states of the authority materials that materially determine its implementation behavior.

This includes, where applicable:

- execution contracts;

- policy semantics;

- temporal semantics;

- receipt semantics;

- identifier semantics;

- hash-domain rules;

- canonical serialization rules;

- adopted external standards.

This does not freeze the Constitution.

A future constitutional or standards revision may legitimately supersede an earlier authority through the normal governance process.

However, an already-issued AMS must remain historically reproducible against the authority context under which it was issued.

# 12. Floating vs. Pinned Authority

The Council therefore adopts the following distinction:

### Constitutional evolution

Ratified constitutional and engineering authorities may evolve through their formal lifecycle.

They are not permanently frozen by M08.

### Implementation mandate

Once an AMS is issued, its operative authority context shall be identifiable and reproducible.

An implementation agent shall not silently switch to a newer authority during execution of an existing AMS.

### Result

The governing model is therefore:

**Evolving constitutional authority + reproducibly pinned implementation mandates.**

This preserves both constitutional evolution and implementation reproducibility.

# 13. External Standards and Historical Reproducibility

Where an external standard is used as implementation-critical authority, M08 shall avoid implicit dependence on a continuously changing upstream state.

If a live registry, standard, or machine-readable external resource is inherently dynamic, the adopted authority shall define an appropriate reproducibility mechanism, such as:

- version pinning;

- dated snapshot;

- integrity hash;

- archived reference;

- explicit effective date.

The mechanism shall be selected according to the nature of the external source.

# 16. AI-Agent Authority Discipline

An implementation agent shall never resolve an M08 semantic ambiguity by selecting the most convenient:

- library behavior;

- language default;

- serialization behavior;

- clock source;

- random source;

- external standard version;

- repository precedent;

- worked example.

Where the governing authority is unresolved, the agent must stop and report the ambiguity according to the CEngS operating model.

Examples include:
`Date.now() JSON.stringify(...) crypto.randomUUID() live external registry lookup ambient environment configuration undocumented repository conventions `
None may become constitutional semantics merely because they are technically convenient.

# 17. CI/CD Implication

G-0805 establishes a governance requirement that should eventually be mechanically enforced.

Where technically practical, CI shall validate that implementation-critical M08 artifacts reference the applicable authority context.

The exact CI enforcement mechanism is not decided by G-0805.

Possible enforcement may include:

- authority manifests;

- version/pin validation;

- contract hash verification;

- prohibited API checks;

- runtime purity checks;

- dependency-boundary validation;

- canonical serialization tests.

These mechanisms remain implementation and engineering-design matters.

G-0805 establishes the **governance requirement**, not the particular CI implementation.

# 18. Required M08 Documentation Discipline

Every M08 implementation artifact that depends upon a Council decision shall be traceable to:

1. the applicable Gate;

2. the Council Decision Record;

3. the authoritative source material supporting that decision;

4. the applicable AMS;

5. the version/state of the authority context used by that AMS.

This provides a trace:
`Authority    ↓ Council Gate    ↓ Council Decision    ↓ M08 Plan / Task    ↓ AMS    ↓ Implementation    ↓ Verification Evidence `
No implementation artifact may invert this chain by using implementation behavior as the source of constitutional meaning.

# 19. Council Findings

Based on the investigation conducted for G-0805, the Council records the following findings:

### Finding F-0805-01

The existing Zyppi authority and lifecycle framework is sufficient to govern M08.

A separate M08 constitutional hierarchy is unnecessary.

### Finding F-0805-02

Formal supersession, not recency, determines whether an older authority has been displaced.

### Finding F-0805-03

Worked examples are illustrative and cannot silently create implementation authority.

### Finding F-0805-04

Draft, proposed, legacy, and reconnaissance material may inform Council deliberation but do not automatically constitute implementation authority.

### Finding F-0805-05

External standards require explicit Zyppi adoption or pinning before becoming implementation-critical authority.

### Finding F-0805-06

Implementation-critical authority must be reproducibly identifiable once an AMS is issued.

### Finding F-0805-07

G-0805 is a root authority gate but does not itself resolve the semantic questions assigned to G-0801–G-0817.
